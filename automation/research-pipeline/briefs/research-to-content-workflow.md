# Guide Brief — Turn saved research into content you can actually publish

Status: `brief_ready`
Opportunity: `research-to-content-workflow`
Primary source cluster: `research-to-content-loop`

## Reader

A non-technical business owner, expert or consultant who saves useful articles, posts and videos but still starts content creation from a blank page.

## Desired outcome

Turn a stream of saved research into a repeatable pipeline that produces useful, original content ideas without copying the source or manually rereading everything.

## Category

`think_and_create_better`

## Problem

Saved research becomes a graveyard. The reader either forgets it, copies someone else's angle too closely, or spends too long re-reading before they can write.

## Primary promise

Build a simple research-to-content loop that captures useful material, distils the signal, adds your point of view, drafts, verifies and learns from what performs.

Stop saving links you never use. Build a loop that turns research into your point of view, then into content.

## Learning structure

### ANSWER

The useful system is not “AI writes from a URL”. It is:

**Capture → Distil → Find the angle → Add your point of view → Draft → Verify → Publish → Learn**

Explain why the point-of-view step prevents the system becoming a copying machine.

### SEE IT

Responsive Excalidraw-style SVG/HTML diagram showing the 8-stage loop.

Use a feedback arrow from **Learn** back to **Find the angle**, making clear that performance improves future choices.

Colour meaning:

- research/input = teal
- thinking/angle = violet
- AI drafting = Shift & Lead blue
- human judgement/verification = amber
- output = deep blue

No decorative floating doodles. Every annotation must be anchored to a stage.

### TRY IT

Mini exercise:

1. Pick 1 saved source.
2. Write the useful fact/idea in 1 sentence.
3. Write “What do I think that the source does not say?”
4. Choose one reader problem it helps solve.
5. Draft only after those 4 lines exist.

Optional interactive reveal: reader enters/selects source type + outcome and reveals the 4 questions to answer before drafting.

### USE IT

Give the reader a compact reusable framework:

**SOURCE** — what is actually useful?

**SIGNAL** — what changes or repeats?

**STANCE** — what do I think?

**SERVICE** — what does my reader get from this?

**SHIP** — what is the smallest useful format to publish?

Working framework name: **The 5S Research-to-Content Loop**. Validate against existing named-framework inventory before publication to avoid collision.
