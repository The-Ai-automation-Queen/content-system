## Part 1 — The Brain Template

This is the single file everything else reads from. Set it up first.

### Section 1: Your Brand Brain

Answer these once. Everything you write later pulls from here — so a vague
answer here means generic content forever. Be specific.

- **Who you are** (2–3 lines): your background, what makes you credible on
  this topic, in your own words — not a résumé, a real sentence someone
  would say about you at a dinner party.
- **Who you serve**: one specific person, not "everyone." Name their job,
  their frustration, what they're afraid of, what they actually want.
- **Your promise** (1 sentence): what changes for them because of you.
- **Your differentiator** (2–3 lines): why you, not the ten other people
  saying the same thing.
- **Your content pillars** (pick 4–6): the recurring topics you'll always
  come back to. Example set to copy and adapt:
  1. Quick wins — a specific tool/trick that saves real time this week
  2. Systems — something you built once that keeps working
  3. The bigger picture — where this is all going, and why it matters
  4. Real talk — your actual journey, mistakes included
  5. Curated — what's actually worth paying attention to (not everything)

### Section 2: Your Content Vault

A table. Every piece of content you write gets a row. Never delete a row —
this becomes your history and your proof of consistency.

| # | Date | Platform | Topic | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (example) 03/07/2026 | LinkedIn | "The 3 tools I actually use" | Posted | Best week yet |
| 2 | (example) 05/07/2026 | Instagram | Behind the scenes | Draft | Needs a hook |
| 3 | | | | | |

Status is always one of: **Draft → Ready → Posted**. Nothing skips a step.

### Section 3: Your Research Notes

A table for what you find worth talking about. Two minutes a day, that's it.

| Date | Source | Finding | Content angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| (example) | A competitor's post | They got huge engagement leading with a number | Try opening my next post with a stat |
| (example) | An industry article | 40% of small businesses don't use AI yet | "You're not behind — most people haven't started" |

### Section 4: Your Weekly Checklist

Run this every week, same day, same order:
1. [ ] Spend 10 minutes in Research Notes — what's worth talking about?
2. [ ] Write 3 drafts using Guide 1 (below)
3. [ ] Review each draft against your Brand Brain — does it sound like you?
4. [ ] Move approved drafts to "Ready"
5. [ ] Schedule them using Guide 2
6. [ ] Check what happened to last week's posts — what worked? Note it.

## Part 2 — Writing Style Clone Lite

*Make AI actually sound like you. This is the difference between content
that gets called out as "obviously AI" and content people assume you wrote
by hand at 11pm.*

Most people skip this step and wonder why every AI draft feels off. It's not
the AI's fault — it was never shown what "you" sounds like. Fix that once,
here, and every guide after this one gets easier.

**Step 1. Gather 5 samples of your real writing.** Not your best writing —
your *typical* writing. A few LinkedIn comments, an email to a friend, a
caption, a text message you sent that sounded like you. Paste them somewhere
you can copy from.

**Step 2. Run the Voice Calibration prompt.** Paste this into Claude,
ChatGPT, or your tool of choice, with your 5 samples pasted in:

```
Here are 5 things I've actually written: [paste your samples]

Describe my voice in exactly 6 rules — things like sentence length, whether
I use contractions, how blunt or soft I am, words I clearly avoid, how I open
a thought, how I close one. Be specific enough that someone could impersonate
me from these 6 rules alone. Then keep those 6 rules visible and follow them
in everything you write for me from here on.
```

**Step 3. Stress-test it.** Give the AI a topic it knows nothing about and
ask it to write one paragraph using your 6 rules. Read it out loud. If a
sentence makes you wince, that rule isn't tight enough — tell the AI exactly
what's wrong and ask it to rewrite the rule, not just the sentence.

**Step 4. Lock it in writing.** Save your final 6 rules somewhere permanent
(the top of your Brand Brain is perfect). From now on, paste them into every
new AI conversation before you ask for a draft — or, in tools that support
it, save them as a custom instruction so you never have to paste them again.

**Step 5. Re-run this every few months.** Your voice shifts as you grow. A
stale voice clone is worse than none — it makes you sound like an old
version of yourself. Five minutes, twice a year, keeps it current.

**The tell that this worked:** you read a draft and can't immediately point
to what an AI would have written differently. That's the bar — not "good
writing," *your* writing.

## Part 3 — Content Engine Lite

*How to use AI to write in your voice, step by step.*

**Step 1.** Open your Brand Brain. Read your positioning section out loud.
Yes, actually — hearing it primes you to write like yourself.

**Step 2.** Go to Claude, ChatGPT, or whichever AI tool you use. Paste this:

```
I want you to write like me. Here's my context:

Who I am: [paste from your Brand Brain]
Who I serve: [paste from your Brand Brain]
My promise: [paste from your Brand Brain]
My voice: casual and direct, contractions are fine, no corporate jargon,
warm but not fluffy — [add your own 2-3 voice words here]

Today's topic: [your topic from Research Notes]
Platform: [LinkedIn / Instagram / etc.]

Write me a first draft. Open with a hook in the first line — a question, a
number, or a bold claim, no warm-up. Keep it to one idea. End with a clear
next step for the reader.
```

**Step 3.** Read the draft out loud. If a sentence makes you cringe or
doesn't sound like something you'd say, rewrite that line yourself — don't
accept a draft that doesn't sound like you.

**Step 4.** Add a call to action. Simple options: ask a question, invite a
comment with a keyword, point to a free resource, or just "let me know if
this is useful."

**Step 5.** Copy the final version into your Content Vault. Set status to
"Ready."

## Part 4 — Distribution Lite

*Getting it everywhere in under 30 minutes.*

**Step 1.** Pick ONE scheduling tool to start (don't overthink this):
Buffer or Later are the easiest for beginners; both have free tiers.

**Step 2.** Connect your accounts (Instagram, LinkedIn, whichever you use).
This is a one-time, 10-minute setup.

**Step 3.** For each "Ready" post in your vault, adapt it per platform:
- **LinkedIn**: keep it as written, maybe add one more line of context —
  this audience reads longer.
- **Instagram**: needs a strong visual and a shorter caption. If you don't
  have a visual, that post might work better as a Reel script instead.
- **X/Twitter**: cut it down. One idea, under 280 characters, or thread it.

**Step 4.** Schedule for a consistent time — same time each day beats
"whenever I remember." Consistency is the actual algorithm hack.

**Step 5.** Mark the vault row "Posted" and note the date.

## Part 5 — DM Responder Lite

*Turning comments into leads, without hiring anyone.*

**Step 1.** Pick ONE keyword you'll use consistently (example: STACK, TEAM,
GUIDE — short, memorable, on-topic).

**Step 2.** Create one lead magnet: a simple PDF or guide that solves one
small problem for your audience. One page is enough to start.

**Step 3.** Set up a free ManyChat account (or your platform's native
automation). Build one flow: "comment contains [keyword]" → auto-DM with
your resource link → tag the contact.

**Step 4.** Add "Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you [the thing]" to your
next 3 posts.

**Step 5.** Check your email/CRM daily for new subscribers. That's it — the
system is now capturing leads while you sleep.
