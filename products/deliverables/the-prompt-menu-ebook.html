Before you order

How to use this menu

One menu, ten kitchens, the same four courses in every one. Here's how to read it.

  1. Find your kitchen. Each of the 10 AI tools gets its own chapter: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Grok, Mistral, DeepSeek, Kimi, Manus, Meta AI. If you already know which tool you're opening today, jump straight to that chapter.
  2. Order by course. Every kitchen serves the same four courses, in the same order: Starters (fast wins), Mains (real business work), Chef's Specials (only that kitchen can cook these), and Verify (make it prove its work). Start with Starters if it's your first time in that kitchen.
  3. Adapt every bracket. [Like this] marks a spot that's yours to fill in. The more specific you make it, the better the dish turns out. A vague bracket gets a vague answer.
The Traffic Light Rule

Every prompt in this menu is written to model good data hygiene: names become [Client] or [Me], numbers get rounded, nothing sensitive gets pasted in. Use this rule for anything you put into an AI tool, in this menu or anywhere else.

Green, share freely. General questions, public information, your own drafts with nothing identifying in them.
Amber, redact first. Anything about a real client, real numbers, or real names. Swap them for [Client], [rounded figure], [Me] before you paste.
Red, never in a personal AI account. Credentials, contracts, anything covered by an NDA, anything you wouldn't want screenshotted. That data needs a business-grade tool with a real data agreement, not a free personal chat window.

Every prompt in this menu already follows this rule. Copy them as they are, or tighten them further for your own situation.

The house rules

The menu convention

Four courses, every kitchen, no exceptions. Once you know the convention, you can order confidently anywhere on this menu.

Starters
4 prompts, 5 minutes each. The first wins in that kitchen. Fast, low-stakes, built to prove the tool is worth opening today.
Mains
6 prompts, real business work. Client updates, proposals, research, planning: the everyday dishes that make up most of what you'll actually order.
Chef's Specials
3 prompts, only this kitchen can cook these. The feature that's genuinely unique to that tool: a live X search, an unattended agent run, a working artifact. Skip these in the wrong kitchen and you'll get a worse dish somewhere else.
Verify
2 prompts, make it prove its work. Every kitchen ends the same way: a confidence check and a sycophancy check, so you never accept a confident-sounding answer as a correct one. The full verification system lives in The Judge's Prompts.

15 prompts per kitchen, 10 kitchens, 150 prompts total. Every single one uses [brackets] for your variables and models redaction by default: names as [Client]/[Me], numbers rounded, nothing sensitive ever pasted in.

14The Confidence Check
Go back through what you just told me and mark each claim as one of three things: VERIFIED (you have real evidence for it in what I gave you), LIKELY (a reasonable inference, but not confirmed), or GUESSED (you filled a gap because I didn't give you the information). Don't soften this. I'd rather see an honest GUESSED than a confident answer that isn't backed up.
When: Any answer with facts, figures, or claims you'll act on. Confident and correct aren't the same thing, and this is how you tell them apart before you rely on either.

When: Any moment it would be easy for AI to just agree with you because you asked. Politeness isn't the same as being right, and this forces the distinction.
