Before you start

How to use this pack

Three ideas, then you're free to jump straight to the prompts.

  1. Don't use all 42 at once. Pick the three that match your current failure mode. Most people start with #11 (Prove It's Done), #21 (Interview First), and #29 (The Acceptance Test). That trio alone changes how you work with AI.
  2. Chain them in order. Council prompts before you build, so bad ideas get argued down early. Full Context prompts while you build, so the AI isn't guessing. Self-Verify prompts after, so nothing ships on a claim. Goal-Run prompts for anything you'll leave unattended.
  3. Adapt every bracket. [Like this] marks a spot that's yours to fill in. The more specific you make it, the stronger the prompt gets. A vague bracket gets a vague answer.
The Traffic Light Rule

Every prompt in this pack is written to model good data hygiene: names become [Client] or [Me], numbers get rounded, nothing sensitive gets pasted in. Use this rule for anything you put into an AI tool, in this pack or anywhere else.

Green, share freely. General questions, public information, your own drafts with nothing identifying in them.
Amber, redact first. Anything about a real client, real numbers, or real names. Swap them for [Client], [rounded figure], [Me] before you paste.
Red, never in a personal AI account. Credentials, contracts, anything covered by an NDA, anything you wouldn't want screenshotted. That data needs a business-grade tool with a real data agreement, not a free personal chat window.

Every prompt in this pack already follows this rule. Copy them as they are, or tighten them further for your own situation.

Why this exists

You're not the builder anymore

Every AI guru sells you speed. "Look how fast it builds!" Here's what they don't show you: the part that matters to a real business owner. How do you know it's right?

I spent 20+ years inside big corporate tech. The thing that environment beat into me wasn't how to build. It was how to verify. Nothing shipped because someone said it was done. It shipped because it proved it was done.

That's the mindset shift this pack installs. You're not the builder anymore. You're the judge. AI does the work. These prompts are how you check it, without doing the work yourself.

Every prompt here is one I actually use, running a business where 99 AI employees do the work and I sign off on it. Copy, paste, adapt. Tool-agnostic: they work in Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, anything.

The 4 Upgrades

The framework behind every prompt in this pack.

Most people use AI like an intern: give a task, accept the answer, hope. Here's the upgrade path.

1
The Council

Make AI disagree with you before you build. One opinion is a guess. A panel with assigned opposing roles is a decision.

2
Self-Verify

Never accept "done." Make it prove its work against explicit criteria, and hunt for its own mistakes.

3
Full Context

Generic answers mean a starving AI. Feed it your real business (offers, voice, clients, constraints) before you ask.

4
The Goal Run

Hand it an outcome, not a step. Then judge the outcome against the checklist you wrote before it started.

Every prompt below serves one of these four. Use the section that matches the failure you're seeing.

Part 01 · 10 prompts

The Council

Make AI disagree with you before you build. One opinion is a guess; a panel is a decision.

Prompts 01–10
Part 01 · The Council

Decide before you build

Start here
01The Devil's Advocate
Before I act on this, argue AGAINST it as strongly as you can. What breaks, what's the cost of being wrong, and what would a skeptic who dislikes this idea say? Only after that, tell me if you still recommend it and why.
Use when: any decision that costs money or reputation if wrong.
02The Three-Chair Panel
Answer this question three times, as three different experts: [a cautious CFO], [an aggressive growth marketer], [my most demanding customer]. Have them disagree where they honestly would. Then write the one-paragraph verdict that survives all three.
Use when: strategy calls, pricing, positioning.
03The Pre-Mortem
Assume this plan failed completely six months from now. Write the honest post-mortem: the three most likely reasons it failed, ranked. For each, what early warning sign would have shown up first?
Use when: launching anything.
04The Steelman Swap
Here are two options I'm choosing between: [A] and [B]. Steelman each one, the strongest honest case for it, before you compare. No verdict until both cases are made properly.
Use when: you're leaning one way and want to check the pull is honest, not just comfortable.
05The Missing Option
I've framed this as a choice between [A] and [B]. What option C am I not seeing? Give me two alternatives that reframe the problem, even if they seem unrealistic at first.
Use when: a decision feels stuck between two bad choices.

Part 01 · The Council

Decide before you build, continued

06The Assumption Audit
List every assumption baked into my request, things I've treated as true without saying so. Rank them by how wrong they'd have to be to change the answer. Flag the one you'd verify first.
Use when: the plan relies on things nobody's actually checked.
07The Base-Rate Check
Before your recommendation: what usually happens when people in my situation try this? What's the honest base rate of success, and what separates the ones who succeed?
Use when: you want reality instead of optimism before you commit.
08The Confidence Ladder
Give your answer, then rate your confidence 1-10 and explain what information would move it up or down two points. If it's below 7, say what you'd need to check first.
Use when: you can't tell if an answer is solid or a best guess dressed up as one.
09The Second Opinion (cross-tool)
[Paste output from one AI into another:] Another assistant produced this answer to my question. Review it as a rival expert: what did it get right, what did it miss, what would you have done differently?
Use when: the decision is big enough to justify a second, independent set of eyes.
10The Silent Disagreement Hunt
Read my plan. Where do you actually disagree with me but have been trained to be agreeable about it? Say the uncomfortable part plainly, I'm paying for the disagreement, not the encouragement.
Use when: you suspect you're getting agreement instead of honesty.

Part 02 · 10 prompts

Self-Verify

Never accept "done." Make it prove its work against explicit criteria, and hunt for its own mistakes.

Prompts 11–20
Part 02 · Self-Verify

Never accept "done"

The most valuable prompt in this pack
11Prove It's Done
You say this is complete. Prove it: walk through each requirement from my original request, quote the part of your output that satisfies it, and mark any requirement you only partially met. Be strict, a miss labeled as a miss is worth more to me than a claimed pass.
Use on everything. This single prompt does more work than any other in this pack.
12The Error Hunt
Before I use this, hunt your own output for errors: factual claims that could be wrong, numbers that don't add up, steps that skip something, names/dates you might have invented. List every suspect item with a risk rating.
Use when: the output will be read by someone other than you.
13Source or Silence
Go back through your answer and mark every factual claim as either [VERIFIABLE, here's where] or [MY GENERATION, treat with caution]. Rewrite the answer keeping only what survives.
Use when: facts and figures matter and you can't tell which is which.
14The Nut-Free Check
You've told me this is safe/correct/compliant. That's exactly the kind of claim that's dangerous when wrong. What would have to be true for your claim to hold? Which of those things have you actually verified vs assumed?
Use when: a wrong answer here could hurt someone, not just embarrass you.
15Read It Back Cold
Read your output as if you'd never seen my request, as a stranger receiving it. What's confusing, what's missing context, what would make the stranger ask "wait, what about…"? Fix those before showing me again.
Use when: the reader won't have the context you had while asking.

Part 02 · Self-Verify

Never accept "done", continued

16The Edge-Case Sweep
What inputs or situations would break this? Give me the five most likely edge cases, and for each: does your solution handle it, fail loudly, or fail silently? Silent failures first.
Use when: this will run without you watching it every time.
17The Numbers Audit
Recompute every number in your answer from scratch, showing the arithmetic. If any number can't be recomputed from stated inputs, flag it as unverified.
Use when: money, time, or metrics are part of the answer.
18Two Drafts, One Judge
Produce two genuinely different versions of this. Then judge them against [my criteria] and tell me which wins, where, and what you'd steal from the loser.
Use when: the first draft feels fine but you haven't seen an alternative.
19The Regression Check
You just changed [X]. What else in this document/plan/system did that change silently affect? Check every section that references or depends on it.
Use when: you've asked for one small edit to something with many moving parts.
20Score It Before I Do
Score your own output against this rubric before I see it: [accuracy / completeness / voice match / usability, each /10]. Anything under 8, fix first and tell me what you changed.
Use when: you want a consistent quality bar without re-explaining it every time.

Part 03 · 8 prompts

Full Context

Generic answers mean a starving AI. Feed it your real business before you ask.

Prompts 21–28
Part 03 · Full Context

Stop starving your AI

Start here
21The Interview First
Before you write anything: interview me. Ask the 5-7 questions whose answers would most change the result. Don't start until I've answered.
Use when: any important deliverable from a cold start.
22What Am I Not Telling You?
Based on what I've asked, what context are you missing that would most improve your answer? List what you'd want to know about my business, audience, and constraints, I'll fill in the blanks.
Use when: you've already asked once and the answer felt generic.
23The Voice Calibration
Here are three things I've written that sound like me: [paste]. Describe my voice in 6 rules, then keep those rules visible and follow them in everything you produce for me in this conversation.
Use when: the output is technically right but doesn't sound like you.
24The Constraint Load
Before answering, restate my constraints back to me: budget, time, tools I use, things I refuse to do. If I haven't given you some, ask. Solutions that ignore constraints are worthless to me.
Use when: you're worried the answer will assume resources you don't have.

Part 03 · Full Context

Stop starving your AI, continued

25The Audience Stand-In
My reader is [a non-technical corporate professional who's overwhelmed by AI]. Before you answer me, describe that person's day, fears, and vocabulary in three lines, then answer for them, not for me.
Use when: you're writing for someone other than yourself.
26Business Brain Load
Here's my business context: [paste your offers, audience, voice rules]. Confirm you've absorbed it by summarizing my business back to me in 4 lines, including the thing you'd say I care most about. Then we start.
Use when: starting a new project or a new AI conversation from scratch.
27The Definition Lock
We keep using the word [X]. Define what YOU mean by it, I'll correct it, and we lock that definition for the rest of this work. Misaligned definitions are where wrong answers hide.
Use when: a conversation keeps circling without landing.
28Refresh the Context
We're deep in this conversation. Summarize: what we've decided, what's open, and what constraints are active. I'll correct any drift, then we continue from the corrected summary.
Use when: a long conversation is starting to feel like it's lost the thread.

Part 04 · 7 prompts

The Goal-Run

Hand it an outcome, not a step. Judge the outcome against the checklist you wrote first.

Prompts 29–35
Part 04 · The Goal-Run

Judge outcomes, not steps

The judge's core move
29The Acceptance Test (write it first)
Before you start: write the checklist I should use to judge whether your result is acceptable. I'll approve or edit the checklist, then do the work and grade yourself against it.
The idea in one line: define "done" before the work exists.
30The Handoff Brief
You're about to do this multi-step job. First give me: the steps you'll take, what you'll produce at each, and where you might need my judgment. I'll flag where I want a checkpoint.
Use when: handing off a job with several stages.
31The Checkpoint Gate
Stop at [step]. Show me what you have, list what changed from the plan, and wait for my go before continuing. Don't optimize past a gate.
Use when: one step in the job needs your sign-off before the next begins.
32Judge the Runner
[After a long task:] Here was the goal: [paste]. Here's the checklist we agreed: [paste]. Grade the result item by item, pass, partial, fail, with evidence quoted for each grade. No narrative, just the grading table first.
Use when: a long task just finished and you need the verdict, not the story.

Part 04 · The Goal-Run

Judge outcomes, not steps, continued

33The Diff Report
Compare what you delivered against what I asked for. Three lists: delivered as asked / delivered differently (and why) / not delivered (and why). The middle list is where trust is won or lost.
Use when: the delivered result feels slightly off from what you asked for.
34The Rollback Plan
Before we ship this change: if it turns out wrong, what exactly do we undo, in what order, and what can't be undone? If anything is irreversible, flag it now, irreversible needs my explicit sign-off.
Use when: the change is hard or costly to reverse.
35The Unattended Rule
You'll do this while I'm away. Rules: [what you may do freely], [what waits for my approval], [what you must never do]. Repeat the rules back, then begin.
Use when: AI will keep working after you've stepped away.

Part 05 · 5 prompts

Sensitive Work

Money, clients, reputation: the places where a wrong answer costs the most.

Prompts 36–40
Part 05 · Sensitive Work

Money, clients, reputation

36The Client-Facing Gate
This will be read by a client. Before I see it: check it for anything that overpromises, anything that could embarrass me if screenshot, and anything that doesn't sound like me. Rewrite those parts and show me what you changed.
Use when: a client will read this before you get a second look at it.
37The Money Math Proof
This involves money. Show every calculation, state every assumption with its source, and mark clearly which numbers are estimates. Then tell me the worst-case if each estimate is 30% off.
Use when: a number here influences a real financial decision.
38The Legal Humility Check
Does any part of this touch legal, tax, or compliance territory? Mark those sections [PROFESSIONAL REVIEW NEEDED], and never present them as settled advice.
Use when: the topic brushes up against legal, tax, or compliance ground.
39The Screenshot Test
If this exact text were screenshot and posted publicly with my name on it, what's the worst honest reading of it? Fix anything that fails that test.
Use when: anything you're about to publish or send publicly.
40The Send-Delay Judgment
I'm about to send this while [annoyed/excited/tired]. Read it as the recipient. What lands differently than I intend? What would 'tomorrow-me' soften or cut?
Use when: you're about to hit send in a heightened state of mind.

Bonus · 2 prompts

The Judge’s Daily Habit

Two small prompts that keep the judge's seat occupied, every single day.

Prompts 41–42
Bonus · The Daily Habit

Keep the judge's seat occupied

41The Morning Brief
Each morning: list what you completed since yesterday, what you're blocked on, and the ONE decision only I can make today. Nothing else.
Use when: every morning, before anything else, if AI is doing ongoing work for you.
42The Friday Receipts
It's Friday. Give me the week's receipts: tasks completed, hours saved (honest estimate with reasoning), anything you got wrong and how it was caught. The 'got wrong' section is mandatory, empty means you didn't look.
Use when: the end of every week. The mandatory "got wrong" section is what makes this one honest.

These two prompts are the smallest habit in this pack and the one most people skip. Run them for a month and you'll have a written record of exactly where AI earned its keep, and exactly where it needed you.

Closing

What to do next

You now have all 42. Don't try to use them all this week.

  1. Pick your trio. Start with #11 (Prove It's Done), #21 (Interview First), and #29 (The Acceptance Test). Run them on whatever you're working on today.
  2. Notice what breaks. The prompt that makes you uncomfortable is usually the one you needed. That discomfort is the judge's seat working.
  3. Build the chain. Council before you build, Full Context while you build, Self-Verify after, Goal-Run for anything unattended. Once it's a habit, you stop thinking about which prompt to reach for.

You're not behind. You were just never taught to judge.
