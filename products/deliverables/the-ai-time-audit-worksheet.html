Before you start

How this worksheet works

One sitting, four parts, one decision at the end.

  1. Map your week. Part 1 is a blank table. You fill in every task you do in a normal work week, honestly, including the boring ones.
  2. Score each task. Part 2 gives every task a number, 0 to 20. The number tells you if AI should take it.
  3. Pick your first hire. Part 3 matches your top 3 scoring tasks to five employee types. You choose one to start.
  4. Do the math. Part 4 turns hours back into a weekly and monthly number, plus an honest word on ramp-up.

Print it and fill it by hand, or type straight into the shaded cells on screen. Either way works. This is a working document, not a display piece.

Part 02

The Scoring Guide

Take the tasks you circled. Score each one against four questions, 0 to 5 apiece.
Repetitive
You do roughly the same steps every time this task comes up.
0 = never the same · 5 = identical every time
Rule-based
There's a clear "if this, then that" you could explain to a new hire.
0 = pure instinct · 5 = fully explainable
Text-or-data-shaped
The task is mostly reading, writing, sorting, or summarizing information.
0 = physical or in-person · 5 = pure text/data
Low-stakes if wrong
A mistake here is cheap to catch and cheap to fix.
0 = high stakes · 5 = truly low stakes

Add the four scores. Maximum possible is 20.

14+
Automate first. Anything scoring 14 or above is your strongest candidate for an AI employee. Below 14 isn't off-limits, it just needs more human judgment sitting alongside the AI.

Part 03

Pick Your First AI Employee

Five employee types. Match your top 3 tasks to the type that fits, then choose the one you'll set up first.
01
The Follow-Up Responder
Fits: repeat questions, check-ins, anything you answer the same way twice a week

Reads incoming messages and drafts the reply in your voice. Flags anything it isn't sure about instead of guessing. Never sends without your review until you've trusted it on 10 in a row.

02
The Summarizer
Fits: long documents, meeting notes, reports, anything you skim instead of reading

Turns a long document or transcript into a short brief: what happened, what matters, what needs a decision. Rounds numbers, redacts names. Never treated as the final word on anything legal or financial.

03
The Drafter
Fits: emails, proposals, posts, anything where a blank page is the hard part

Writes the first version of anything you'd otherwise stare at for twenty minutes before starting. Works from your rules and past examples, not from a blank prompt. You edit, you never originate from zero again.

04
The Researcher
Fits: gathering information, comparing options, prep work before a decision

Pulls together what's known on a topic, a competitor, or an option you're weighing, and hands you a short comparison instead of ten open tabs. States what it's unsure about instead of filling gaps with a guess.

05
The Scheduler
Fits: reminders, recurring tasks, anything that only needs to happen "on Tuesday"

Runs the same job on a set day and time without you remembering to start it. Reports what it did in one line afterward. Starts on the smallest, lowest-stakes recurring task you own.

The honesty line. You will not get the full number back in week one. Week one is setup and correction: you're teaching the AI employee what "good" looks like for you. Expect roughly half the time saved in week one, closer to the full number by week three or four. That ramp-up is normal. It is not a sign the task was a bad pick.

Closing

What to do on day 1

You've mapped the week, scored the tasks, picked the employee, and done the math. Here's the only thing left: start.

  1. Open your favorite AI tool. Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, whatever you already use is fine. You don't need a new tool to start.
  2. Describe the one task you circled in Part 3. Tell it what you do, step by step, and show it one real example of "good" (redact names and numbers first, see the traffic light rule below).
  3. Give it a small, real version of the task today. Not a test question, an actual piece of this week's work. Review the output before it goes anywhere.
  4. Write down what you'd fix. That note is what makes the second attempt better than the first. This is the whole loop, repeated.
One reminder before you paste anything real
Green, share freely. Your own drafts, general process descriptions, nothing identifying.
Amber, redact first. Real client names become [Client], real numbers get rounded.
Red, never in a personal AI account. Credentials, contracts, anything under an NDA.

You don't need to automate everything this month. You need one task, fully handed off, that you trust.

Start there.
