01 The twelve words, decoded

Hallucination

A friend who never says "I don't know"

AI confidently makes something up. Always double-check facts, numbers, legal claims.

RAG

An open-book exam

AI looks it up in your documents first, then answers based on what it found.

Agent

Hiring a smart friend for the afternoon

AI that takes action: browses, reads files, fills forms. Not just talk.

MCP

USB-C for AI apps

A standard way for AI tools to plug into other software.

Context window

A desk that only fits so much paper

How much text the AI can hold in its head at once.

Token

A syllable, roughly

A small chunk of text. AI companies charge by the token.

API

A waiter in a restaurant

A way for two apps to talk behind the scenes, no copy-paste needed.

Prompt engineering

Briefing a freelancer well

Writing clearer, more specific instructions to get better AI results.

Multimodal

Reads, looks at photos, listens

An AI that works with more than text: images, audio, sometimes video.

Fine-tune

Training from scratch vs. hiring a contractor

Retraining a model on your data. Expensive, usually overkill.

Temperature

A dimmer switch for randomness

Controls how predictable or creative the AI is. Rarely touched.

Open source

Renting a kitchen vs. owning your stove

The model is publicly downloadable. ChatGPT and Claude aren't; Llama is.

Overheard in a meeting, now fully decoded

"We're connecting our support docs to a Claude agent through MCP, so it pulls from our own data instead of hallucinating (that's RAG). If quality slips, we'll fine-tune. Tokens are cheap now."

The question that shows you understood

"What happens when the AI can't find the answer in your docs? Does it make something up, or does it say it doesn't know?" That question alone proves you understand the difference between RAG and hallucination.

Try this today

Next time someone drops jargon in a meeting, ask the plain-English question behind it. It moves the conversation forward instead of nodding through it.
