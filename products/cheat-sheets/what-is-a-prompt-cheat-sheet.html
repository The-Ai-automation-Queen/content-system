What Is A Prompt?

Plain English series · No. 03 · the one-page version
Shift & Lead guides.shiftandlead.com

A prompt is just what you type. The quality of your answer is almost entirely determined by the quality of your question. That's the whole game.

01 The formula: four things, every time

1

Role

Who it should be. Shapes tone and vocabulary.

"You are an experienced social media manager."
2

Context

Your situation. AI knows nothing unless you tell it.

"I sell handmade candles, 2,000 followers, engagement dropped."
3

Task

Exactly what you want. Not "help me", be specific.

"Give me 5 post ideas with captions to boost engagement."
4

Format

How to structure the answer. Without it, long paragraphs.

"Numbered list, bold titles, captions under 50 words."

02 See it work: same request, before and after

Without the formula

"Help me with my Instagram."

You get: a generic 500-word essay. Nothing specific. Nothing to post today.

With the formula

Role + context + task + format, one message

You get: 5 specific ideas you can use today, tailored to your product, ready to post.

03 Know before you eat: the two mistakes

Being too vague

"Help me with marketing" is so broad the AI has to guess. It guesses wrong and you conclude "AI doesn't work." It works fine, the prompt didn't.

Not iterating

Your first prompt rarely nails it, that's okay. Treat it as a conversation, not a one-shot command. Refine until it's right.

Try this today

Pick one task you're already doing this week. Write the prompt with all four parts: role, context, task, format. Notice the difference.
