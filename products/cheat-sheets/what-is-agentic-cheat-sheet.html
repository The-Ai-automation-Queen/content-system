What "Agentic" Means

Plain English series · No. 05 · the one-page version
Shift & Lead guides.shiftandlead.com

A chatbot answers questions. An agentic system finishes tasks. The difference is not intelligence, it's autonomy: how much you let the AI do before checking back in with you.

01 Think of it like delegation

Short leash

You review every step

"Draft a reply. Show me before you send it." One task, then it waits for your next instruction.

Long leash

It runs the whole job

"Handle my inbox this morning. Flag anything urgent." It reads, decides, replies, comes back with a summary.

02 The four levels of AI autonomy

1

Chat

You ask, it answers. No memory, no tools, no actions.

"Write a thank-you email." You paste it yourself.
2

Chat + tools

It can reach outside the chat: search, read a file, calculate.

Upload a report, ask "what are the top 3 risks?"
3

Workflow

A fixed sequence that runs the same way every time.

Every Monday, it pulls stats and drops a summary in a doc.
4

Agentic

The AI decides the steps itself. You set the goal, it drives.

"Research 5 competitors, flag gaps." You never defined the steps.
The one question that separates hype from reality

When someone pitches you an "AI agent," ask: "Does it decide the steps, or do I?" If you define the steps, it's a workflow. If the AI decides, it's agentic. Both are useful, but they cost different amounts and fail in different ways.

Try this today

Pick one repetitive task in your week. Ask: is this Level 2 (chat with tools), Level 3 (fixed workflow), or does it truly need Level 4 judgment? Start one level lower than you think.
