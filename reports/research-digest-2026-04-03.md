## Top 5 Stories Worth Knowing

### 1. Gartner: 40%+ of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027
Gartner published a stark prediction: more than 40% of current agentic AI projects will be cancelled due to escalating costs, unclear ROI, and inadequate risk controls. ### 2. EU deepfake labelling rules go live August 2026 — and businesses aren't ready
The EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations take effect August 2, 2026, requiring deployers to label AI-generated video, audio, and images — including deepfakes — at first exposure. A common EU icon is being developed; in the interim, an "AI" or "IA" label is the standard. The Code of Practice finalises May–June 2026, leaving a very short compliance window. Most SME owners and CEOs have no idea this applies to their marketing content.
**Source:** [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/what-the-eus-new-ai-code-of-practice-means-for-labeling-deepfakes/) · [EU AI Act Newsletter #93](https://artificialintelligenceact.substack.com/p/the-eu-ai-act-newsletter-93-transparency)

### 3. 72% of Global 2000 companies now run AI agents in production — but scaling is broken
New March 2026 data shows 72% of the world's largest companies have deployed AI agents beyond pilot programs. Telecom leads at 48% agentic adoption, retail at 47%. Translation: buying the tool ≠ getting the outcome.
**Source:** [Enterprise AI Agent Adoption: March 2026 Data](https://insights.reinventing.ai/articles/openclaw-enterprise-adoption-march-2026-03-16) · [Gartner via NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/state-of-ai-report-2026/)

## Signals: Early Indicators Not Yet Mainstream

The WEF and PwC are both reporting that companies getting real ROI from AI aren't running scattered pilots — they're building a centralised "AI Studio": a hub that owns use-case assessment, reusable components, testing sandboxes, and deployment governance. This is the organisational design answer to the pilot trap. Not mainstream in mid-market yet. Fatiha could get ahead of this conversation.
Source: [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2026/03/where-is-ai-moving-beyond-experimentation-leaders-scaling/) · [PwC AI Predictions](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-predictions.html)

**2. The 50-point action gap in AI reskilling**
54% of workers say AI skills are critical for their role. Only 4% are actively training. That's a 50-point gap — and an estimated $18,000 annual opportunity cost per worker. This is not yet a board-level conversation in most companies. It will be.
Source: [Asanify AI Reskilling Digest](https://asanify.com/blog/news/ai-reskilling-workforce-gap-april-2-2026/)

This is creating distrust that will hit the entire AI tools market, not just bad vendors. Leaders who can read the room (and a vendor pitch) will have a major advantage.

## One Contrarian Take

**Everyone is treating the AI skills gap as a training problem. It isn't.**

The prevailing narrative: companies need to invest in AI reskilling programmes, and the gap will close. But the data tells a different story. 80% of tech-forward organisations say upskilling is the most effective solution to the skills gap — but only 28% are actually planning to invest in it over the next 2-3 years. Meanwhile, 120 million workers are at medium-term redundancy risk specifically because reskilling won't reach them in time.

The real problem isn't training. It's that most companies are making adoption decisions before they've made workforce decisions. Tools get bought; people get left behind. The "reskilling gap" is actually a *leadership sequencing failure* — and no amount of online courses fixes the fact that leaders haven't decided what their people are supposed to become.

This is the angle that separates Fatiha's advisory from everyone selling AI training: she addresses the *decision layer* before the *tools layer*. That's not a training conversation. That's a strategy conversation.
