## Top 5 Stories Worth Knowing

### 1. EU AI Act Omnibus Deal Reached May 7 — High-Risk Deadlines Extended, Deepfake Ban Added

One week after the April 28 trilogue collapse (covered in the last digest), the European Council and Parliament reached a provisional agreement on May 7, 2026. The deal extends the compliance deadline for standalone high-risk AI systems (Annex III: employment, education, biometrics, critical infrastructure, law enforcement) to **December 2, 2027** — and for high-risk AI embedded in physical products (medical devices, machinery, connected cars) to **August 2, 2028**. The agreement adds a new explicit prohibition on AI-generated non-consensual sexual imagery and CSAM. The deal still requires formal adoption by both Parliament and Council, which negotiators say they intend to complete before August 2, 2026.

One sting in the fine print: the grace period for implementing transparency solutions for AI-generated content (deepfake labelling under Article 50) was *shortened* from 6 months to 3 months — creating a binding **December 2, 2026** deadline that most companies are not tracking.

**Why it matters:** The relief most businesses have been waiting for is now provisionally real — but not yet law. And the deepfake labelling clock is moving faster, not slower.

### 2. Anthropic Declares War on McKinsey: $1.5B Enterprise AI Services Firm Launched

On May 4, Anthropic announced a joint venture with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs — backed by $1.5 billion in committed capital (roughly $300M each) and additional backing from General Atlantic, Apollo Global Management, GIC, and Sequoia. The new firm is a standalone entity that embeds Anthropic engineers directly inside mid-size businesses to redesign workflows and integrate Claude into core operations. Fortune described it as "taking a direct shot at the consulting industry." OpenAI announced a parallel joint venture the same day.

**Why it matters:** The AI model makers are not waiting for consulting firms to distribute their technology. They are becoming the consulting firms. This restructures the entire market for AI transformation advice — the question of who guides your AI strategy is no longer just a capability question, it is a market positioning question.

**Source:** [Fortune — Anthropic takes shot at consulting industry in joint venture with Wall Street giants (May 4)](https://fortune.com/2026/05/04/anthropic-claude-consulting-industry-joint-venture-blackstone-goldman-sachs/) · [CNBC — Anthropic and Goldman, Blackstone partner on $1.5 billion AI venture (May 4)](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/04/anthropic-goldman-blackstone-ai-venture.html) · [TechCrunch — Anthropic and OpenAI are both launching joint ventures for enterprise AI services](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/04/anthropic-and-openai-are-both-launching-joint-ventures-for-enterprise-ai-services/)

### 3. Freshworks CEO: "Over Half Our Code Is Written by AI" — 500 Jobs Cut While Beating Earnings

On May 5–6, Freshworks CEO Dennis Woodside announced 500 layoffs (11% of workforce) alongside Q1 2026 results showing revenue of $228.6 million — up 16% year over year, ahead of analyst estimates, with two of the largest contracts in company history. The CEO stated directly that over half of Freshworks' code is now written by AI. The restructuring charge is estimated at $8 million.

This is structurally different from the "AI layoffs as cover for overhiring" pattern documented in the April 25 Fortune op-ed. Freshworks is profitable, growing, signing its biggest deals, and still needs fewer people. The same week, Coinbase announced layoffs, bringing total 2026 tech layoffs to approximately 127,000 across 283 companies.

**Why it matters:** The "companies cutting people are just optimising, not transforming" argument becomes harder to hold when a company beats earnings and still reduces headcount. This is the business case for AI finally materialising in a clear, visible way — and the hardest leadership conversation of 2026.

### 4. OpenAI B2B Signals: Frontier Firms Now Use 3.5x More AI Per Worker — and the Gap Is Widening

OpenAI released its first B2B Signals report, analysing usage patterns across its enterprise customer base. The headline finding: companies at the 95th percentile of AI usage ("frontier firms") now use 3.5x as much AI per worker as typical firms — up from 2x a year ago. Volume alone explains only 36% of the frontier advantage; the larger share comes from richer, more complex use. Frontier firms send 16x as many Codex (agentic coding) messages per worker. Education and enablement — using AI to help employees build AI fluency — is where the task-level advantage is largest.

**Why it matters:** The gap between the firms that understand AI deeply and the firms that have deployed it superficially is widening faster than it was 12 months ago. The window to cross to the frontier side is not closing — it is already narrowing.

**Source:** [OpenAI — How frontier firms are pulling ahead (B2B Signals report)](https://openai.com/index/introducing-b2b-signals/) · [OpenAI — B2B Signals](https://openai.com/signals/b2b/)

### 5. PayPal Cuts 4,760 Jobs (20%) to Build an "AI-Native Operating Model"

New PayPal CEO Enrique Lores announced on May 5 that the company will eliminate 20% of its global workforce — approximately 4,760 employees — phased over two to three years, in pursuit of $1.5 billion in run-rate cost savings. The stated rationale is an explicit shift to an "AI-native operating model." This is the largest fintech workforce reduction of 2026 by absolute headcount, surpassing Block (4,000, February) and Coinbase (700, May 5).

"AI-native" marks a linguistic shift from earlier vocabulary. Not "AI-assisted." Not "AI-enhanced." AI-native — meaning the operating model is designed from scratch around AI capability, not retrofitted onto existing structures.

**Why it matters:** When a major financial services company makes "AI-native" the official framing for a 20% workforce reduction, it becomes board-level language. Leaders who don't know what it means — or what it demands from them — are already behind.

## Signals: Early Indicators Not Yet Mainstream

**1. AI Labs as the New Consulting Industry**
Anthropic and OpenAI both announcing enterprise AI services joint ventures on the same day (May 4) is not a coincidence — it is a market capture move. The traditional AI transformation path runs through consulting firms (McKinsey, Deloitte, BCG) that pick tools and implement them. Both Anthropic and OpenAI are now offering to cut that middleman out. Within 18–24 months, every major AI lab will have a direct enterprise implementation arm. The advisory market for AI transformation is being restructured from the supply side.

**2. "AI-Native" as the New Vocabulary for Org Design**
PayPal's explicit use of "AI-native operating model" to justify a 20% headcount reduction signals a linguistic shift about to reach boardrooms everywhere. Not "we are adopting AI" — "we are rebuilding around it." Leaders need to understand what they are agreeing to when their CFO or incoming CEO starts using this term. No governance framework or vocabulary guide exists for it yet.

**3. The Frontier/Mainstream Gap Is Hardening Faster Than Expected**
OpenAI's B2B Signals data shows the usage gap between frontier firms and typical firms grew from 2x to 3.5x in one year — and the gap is primarily driven by how AI is used, not how much. This is a compounding advantage. The longer typical firms wait to shift from chat-based assistance to agentic, delegated work, the harder it becomes to close the gap. No one in the CEO advisory market is framing this as a closing window — but it is.

## Contrarian Take

**The consensus:** The EU AI Act Omnibus deal is good news — compliance obligations are delayed, requirements are simplified, businesses have more time.

**What might be wrong:** The "delay" narrative is obscuring a tightening in one of the most commercially relevant provisions. The grace period for implementing transparency solutions for AI-generated content (the deepfake labelling requirement under Article 50) was *shortened* from 6 months to 3 months in the May 7 deal — creating a **December 2, 2026** deadline. Any business using AI to generate video, audio, images, or text in professional or commercial contexts has roughly seven months to implement machine-readable labelling. This is not a compliance edge case. This is marketing, advertising, social media, and every piece of AI-generated customer content. The high-risk AI delay is a relief for developers. The AI-generated content deadline is a problem for every communications team in every company that uses an AI content tool.

The contrarian read: **The headline says delay; the fine print says accelerate.** Leaders who read summaries will miss the December 2026 obligation entirely. The ones who understand the actual structure of the law — not the PR version — will be ready. That gap is precisely where her positioning lives.
