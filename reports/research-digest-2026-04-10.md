## Top 5 Stories Worth Knowing

### 1. Gartner: Only 28% of AI infrastructure projects actually deliver ROI (April 7, 2026)
A new Gartner survey of 782 infrastructure and operations leaders found that only 28% of AI use cases fully succeed and meet ROI expectations — with 20% failing outright. The primary cause of failure: 57% of leaders admitted they expected too much, too fast. The most common collapse points are auto-remediation, self-healing infrastructure, and agent-led workflow management — exactly the areas vendors are actively pitching. Success correlates with one factor above all others: full executive involvement, not tool selection.

**Sources:** [Gartner Press Release, April 7](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-07-gartner-says-artificial-intelligence-projects-in-infrastructure-and-operations-stall-ahead-of-meaningful-roi-returns) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/07/ai_returns_gartner/)

### 2. FOBO — "Fear of Becoming Obsolete" — crystallises as the defining workforce anxiety of 2026 (April 5)
Fortune named it this week: FOBO is the creeping sense that skills are degrading in real time, that the window to stay relevant is closing. Unlike traditional job insecurity, FOBO isn't about getting fired — it's about becoming irrelevant while still employed. Four in 10 workers now name AI-driven job loss as a primary fear, a figure that has nearly doubled in a single year (KPMG). Sixty-three percent say AI will make the workplace feel less human. The practical consequence: quiet resistance — employees appearing to use AI tools while not genuinely integrating them.

**Sources:** [Fortune, April 5](https://fortune.com/2026/04/05/what-is-fobo-ai-angst-adoption/) · [HR Grapevine, April 7](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2026-04-07-tech-workers-fear-becoming-increasingly-obsolete-after-layofs) · [Allwork.Space](https://allwork.space/2026/04/fobo-is-spreading-at-work-but-the-timeline-for-ai-making-you-obsolete-may-surprise-you/)

### 3. Salesforce CEO Benioff calls AI-attributed layoffs "the lazy way out" (April 7)
Forty-seven-point-nine percent of Q1 2026 tech layoffs (78,557 total) were attributed to AI and automation — but three major CEOs pushed back hard this week. Marc Benioff called using AI as the explanation for layoffs "too simplistic — the lazy way out." Marc Andreessen labelled it "the silver-bullet excuse" for pandemic overhiring. Andy Jassey walked back Amazon's own earlier language, saying the cuts were "not really AI-driven, not right now at least." Bloomberg had called it in March: the AI-washing of job cuts is "corrosive and confusing." The real driver in many cases — companies self-funding $650B in AI infrastructure by cutting payroll — is more uncomfortable than the AI story.

**Sources:** [Benioff via Business Today, April 7](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/ai-isnt-the-real-reason-for-tech-layoffs-salesforce-ceo-marc-benioff-calls-it-a-lazy-way-out-524411-2026-04-07) · [SF Standard, April 2](https://sfstandard.com/2026/04/02/ai-washing-layoffs/) · [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/tech-industry-lays-off-nearly-80-000-employees-in-the-first-quarter-of-2026-almost-50-percent-of-affected-positions-cut-due-to-ai) · [Bloomberg, March 13](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-03-13/the-ai-washing-of-job-cuts-is-corrosive-and-confusing)

### 4. HBR research: LLMs give every CEO the same strategy — regardless of context
Harvard Business Review published research showing that leading LLMs systematically recommend the same trendy strategies — differentiation, augmentation, collaboration, decentralisation — regardless of the specific context they're given. The researchers called it "trendslop": AI producing polished, buzzword-aligned strategic advice that sounds rigorous but ignores situational logic. Models also fall into a "hybrid trap," recommending conflicting choices that blur strategic focus. The implication is direct: business leaders using AI as a strategic advisor are getting homogenised recommendations that flatten competitive differentiation.

**Source:** [Harvard Business Review, March 16](https://hbr.org/2026/03/researchers-asked-llms-for-strategic-advice-they-got-trendslop-in-return)

### 5. 54% of C-suite executives say AI adoption is "tearing their company apart"
Writer's enterprise AI adoption report for 2026 found that 79% of organisations face significant challenges adopting AI — a double-digit increase from 2025 — and 54% of C-suite executives admit the process is actively creating internal conflict: between IT and business units, between early adopters and resisters, between speed and governance. Despite this, 42% of companies believe their strategy is highly prepared for AI — while feeling deeply unprepared on infrastructure, data quality, risk management, and talent. The gap between stated readiness and operational reality is widening.

**Source:** [Writer Enterprise AI Adoption 2026](https://writer.com/blog/enterprise-ai-adoption-2026/) · [Codewave State of AI Enterprise Adoption 2026](https://codewave.com/insights/ai-enterprise-adoption-2026/)

## Signals: Early Indicators Not Yet Mainstream

**1. "Quiet AI resistance" as an unmeasured adoption failure**
FOBO is producing a new adoption pathology: employees who visibly use AI tools without genuinely integrating them — appearing compliant to avoid scrutiny while protecting their existing workflows. No enterprise has a measurement framework for this yet. The adoption dashboard says green; the actual change is not happening. This will surface as a leadership problem, not a technology problem, and Fatiha can get ahead of this conversation before it becomes the next big enterprise failure story.

**2. "AI outcomes auditor" emerging as a demand signal**
The 28% ROI success rate from Gartner, combined with 80%+ of organisations not seeing significant ROI from generative AI despite high investment, is beginning to create demand for post-deployment accountability — not just pre-deployment strategy. Watch for "AI ROI audit" as a consulting category. This is adjacent to Fatiha's work and positions the next natural service expansion: not just building understanding before implementation, but holding the mirror up after it.

**3. Executive burnout from AI adoption pressure**
"Tearing their company apart" (54% of C-suite, Writer) is language that signals something beyond friction — it signals burnout. AI adoption is now a leadership stress event, not just an operational one. The fastest-growing private conversation among senior executives is not about tools but about pace: how fast is too fast, and what do you sacrifice when you move at the speed the board expects. No one in the advisory space is addressing this directly. It is a white space.

## One Contrarian Take

**Everyone is saying AI adoption is failing because companies move too fast. The more precise explanation: they're measuring the wrong outcome.**

The prevailing advice — slow down, be strategic, nail your use cases — assumes the problem is pace. But Gartner's data says otherwise. The single factor that most differentiates AI projects that succeed (28%) from those that fail (72%) is not speed. It's whether senior executives stayed involved. The technology is not the variable. The leadership layer is.

Companies are tracking deployment: did we launch the tool? They are not tracking capability: do our people understand what they are using, and why, and when not to? These are different questions with different answers — and almost no enterprise is asking the second one.

The advisory industry is still selling tool selection, vendor evaluation, and implementation roadmaps. All of that addresses deployment. None of it addresses understanding. Fatiha's positioning — *from AI confusion to AI confidence* — is the only answer to the actual problem. The market is beginning to realise the problem exists. The window to own this conversation is now, not next year.
