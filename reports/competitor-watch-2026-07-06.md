## Gaps: Topics None of Them Covered That You Could Own

### 1. Translating the agent era for solo operators (THE most urgent gap this week)
Mollick's "twilight of the chatbots" piece is brilliant and widely shared — for
knowledge workers and enterprise buyers. Nobody is translating the agent-manager
shift for a solo operator or small-team entrepreneur. Allie talks about 34 agents
for a company that can afford to build 34 agents. Matt curates tools. Ruben teaches
Claude tricks. Nobody is saying: *"You are a one-person business. Here is what one
agent, set up this week, looks like in practice — and what it actually does for you
while you sleep."* This is the clearest gap in the current conversation and maps
directly to your Build Once, Runs Forever pillar.

### 2. Learning obsolescence as an entrepreneurial crisis (not a corporate one)
Mollick names the "obsolescence gap" for enterprise employees. Nobody covers it for
the solopreneur or early-stage entrepreneur who just spent three months — and
several hundred dollars — on an AI prompting course, only to discover that prompting
is being automated away. The emotional hook ("you did everything right and it is
still not enough") combined with the practical reframe ("here is what actually
future-proofs you") is completely unoccupied. It maps to Stop Doing That by Hand
and Real Talk simultaneously.

### 3. The AI displacement → entrepreneur pivot (the missing bridge)
Allie's "if I got laid off" post is aimed at employees who want to stay employed.
Nobody is writing the adjacent piece aimed at employees who want to use AI-caused
disruption as the trigger to leave and build their own thing. The framing:
*"If AI just made your role redundant, the worst thing you can do is find another
job that AI will eliminate next."* This is your lane — the corporate-to-entrepreneur
bridge — and nobody in the monitored set is owning it.

### 4. What the orchestrator concept means for a five-person business
Allie's "34 agents, the coordinator does no work" post is for companies that have
the resources to build 34 agents. Nobody is writing the accessible version: what
does one coordinator agent look like for someone who runs a team of five? What does
it route? What does it decide? This is practical, teachable, and directly serves
your audience's ambition without requiring them to be a developer.

### 5. The moral question for creators (what AI video means for personal brand)
Wes's "AI video went too far" content is macro and aimed at watchers, not doers.
Nobody is publishing the creator-specific version: *"AI-generated video is
flooding every platform. Here is what that actually means for whether people trust
your face on screen."* The answer (authentic human creators become more valuable,
not less) is reassuring for your audience and contrarian to the fear. Maps to
What's Worth It.
