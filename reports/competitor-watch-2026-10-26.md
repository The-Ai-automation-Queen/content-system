## What Defined This Week

Three separate narratives converged this week. The first: AI capability cost collapsed to near-zero (Mollick's 30-month exponential chart, published alongside his new book *Co-Existence*). The second: AI agents began acting without being asked — contacting service providers, flagging errors, coordinating on their own (Mollick again, plus Allie K. Miller on process automation). The third: as AI content floods the platforms, authenticity became the explicit differentiator — Matt Wolfe framing it on YouTube, Allie Miller raising it on LinkedIn, Nina Schick's trust data underlining it.

The macro signal: the gap between creators who discuss AI and creators who demonstrate AI in operation is widening. Demonstration is winning.

## Gaps: Topics None of Them Covered This Week

### Gap 1 — The trust gap from the customer side
Nina Schick published the HAPI data (62% of Americans distrust AI developers). No one asked the follow-on question for business owners: **what does this mean if your customers feel the same way about your AI use?** How do you use AI in client-facing work while your customers actively distrust it? This is owned territory for a brand anchored in data and security awareness.

### Gap 2 — The AI price collapse translated for the small business operator
Mollick's 30-month chart is written for academics and AI practitioners. Nobody translated it for the business owner who is not watching Substack: **the tasks you dismissed as too expensive to automate in 2024 are now nearly free.** This is the most actionable angle this week and it is sitting unclaimed by anyone in the tracked set speaking to a non-technical business audience.

### Gap 3 — The personal agent for a one-person business
Mollick's agent stories are Wharton-professor stories. Nobody asked: **how does a solopreneur or small business owner set up the same thing this week?** The concept of proactive agents is circulating in high-end content circles. The practical "here is the first agent you should build" post for a non-technical business owner does not exist in this set.

### Gap 4 — Internal capability vs. vendor dependency
Allie K. Miller touched on process-management AI. Wes Roth touched on the software moat collapsing. But nobody addressed the direct business implication: **if the tools you depend on are being replicated by solo operators overnight, what does that mean for your vendor dependencies?** The case for building internal AI capability — rather than buying perpetual subscriptions — is being demonstrated by the market and not yet articulated for the CEO audience.

### Gap 5 — "When do I start" for the still-waiting founder
Every creator this week assumed their audience is already acting. Mollick, Hassid, Wolfe, Roth — all writing for people who are in motion. The founder who is still waiting — still deciding — has nobody speaking to them this week. This is the highest-tension entry point for a brand positioned on moving from confusion to confidence.
