**Hook 1 — The Bold Reframe**
> "Everyone keeps calling these new AI facilities data centres. That's fundamentally wrong."
— Nina Schick, *They're Not Data Centres, They're Intelligence Factories* (Substack, Mar 27)

**Why it works:** Opens by naming a widespread assumption and immediately declaring it wrong. No warm-up, no credentials — just a direct challenge to consensus. Forces the reader to ask: "What *is* it, then?"

His "How to never sound like an AI" post (8-step numbered list) hit an estimated 731K views. The format: 1-sentence contrarian hook, then a numbered how-to list of 8–12 steps. First step is concrete and unexpected. Length: short enough to read in 60 seconds. This is his dominant format and it is working.

**2. Reactive opinion posts timed to breaking news (Allie K. Miller)**
228 comments on one post. Her formula: 1 sentence reacting to news ("This is insane"), 1 sentence showing she was ahead of it ("I told a skeptic this was coming"), 1–2 paragraphs of the real story. The reaction is the hook; the anecdote is the proof. Requires being fast — she posts within hours of breaking news.

**3. Bold reframe essays (Nina Schick)**
Her Substack essays consistently reframe breaking news through her "industrial intelligence" thesis. The hook names a common assumption and declares it wrong in the first sentence. New addition this week: a short film/video essay format (4–6 minutes, embedded in Substack) — her first foray beyond text. Early signal of long-form video gaining traction for serious AI commentary.

**4. Weekly roundup threads with crowdsourced engagement (Matt Wolfe)**
His weekly X thread format: opening tease line, then 8–12 bullet-point news items, then "let me know what I missed." The CTA drives comments organically without being transactional. Works because readers feel invited to contribute, not sold to.

**5. Research-backed long-form essays (Ethan Mollick)**
Mollick posts once or twice a month to Substack but each post circulates for weeks. This week's reach came from a post published March 12 ("The Shape of the Thing"). Benchmarks and empirical framing give him durability — posts keep getting shared long after publication. High barrier to replicate but high moat once established.
