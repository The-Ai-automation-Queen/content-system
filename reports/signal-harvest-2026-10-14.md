## shift-lead evidence (public-eligible)

### S1 — Leaders say judgment is the most essential AI-era skill; the people who hold it don't rank it that way (Starting point 2)
- **Source:** IBM, "New IBM CHRO Study: AI Puts Critical Thinking at the Center of Workforce Priorities" (IBM Institute for Business Value, fielded with surveying partners)
- **URL:** https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-09-21-new-ibm-chro-study-ai-puts-critical-thinking-at-the-center-of-workforce-priorities
- **Sample:** 1,500 CHROs/senior workforce-strategy executives + 8,800 full-time employees, across 21 geographies, 28 countries, 23 industries | **Field dates:** April–June 2026 | **Published:** 2026-09-21 | **Captured:** 2026-10-14 (~3.5 weeks old)
- **Exact data:** 71% of CHROs name "the ability to supervise, validate and override AI outputs" the most essential human capability going forward; 57% name critical thinking, 48% name human judgment. Only 29% of employees rank judgment as important, and 49% say critical thinking matters most. 60% of employees worry AI is eroding their skills, and among those worried, three out of four say it has already begun to happen. 46% of organizations do not involve their CHRO in defining AI strategy. Organizations that explicitly define workflows as human-led, AI-assisted, or AI-executed report 18% risk reduction and 20% quality improvement.
- **Read as:** sharpens pillar 2 with a new angle not yet logged — not just "people still prefer their own judgment" (Resume Now, logged 2026-10-02) or "the strongest performers protect it deliberately" (Microsoft WorkLab, logged 2026-10-12), but a *disconnect*: the organizations asking people to use AI rate judgment as the top differentiator, while the employees holding that judgment rank it near the bottom of what they think matters. That gap — undervaluing the exact thing that's supposedly uniquely theirs — is a sharper hook for "recognise and protect what's uniquely yours" than passive preference data.
- **Caveat:** CHRO/enterprise-employee sample, not solo-founder-specific — same audience-skew caveat as other large workforce surveys logged so far for this pillar. IBM is also a vendor with a stake in enterprise AI-governance narratives; read the framing as directional, the disclosed topline numbers as the citable fact.

### S2 — Solo founders already answer "where does AI belong" with a consistent line: content/ops yes, pricing and customer relationships no (Starting point 3)
- **Source:** Hostinger, solo-founder digital-habits survey
- **URL:** https://www.hostinger.com/blog/solo-founder-digital-habits/
- **Sample:** 640 U.S. side-hustle founders and solo business owners | **Field dates:** September 2026 | **Published:** 2026-10-08 | **Captured:** 2026-10-14 (6 days old)
- **Exact data:** 56% used AI for at least one core task (63% among hypergrowth founders vs. 46% among slower-growing ones). Most common AI uses: marketing content/copy (40%), website building/updating (28%), legal/contract work (27%). Seven in 10 said they set their own prices and answered their own customers, and would not hand either responsibility to AI. 63% of founders who had planned to hire delayed it because of AI availability.
- **Read as:** the best audience-matched pillar-3 source logged so far — actual solo founders/side-hustlers (not the Zapier mid-market leaders flagged with an audience-skew caveat on 2026-10-12), and it answers "decide where AI belongs ... and choose a first test" with a concrete, already-lived pattern: the live "first tests" people actually choose are content and admin-adjacent tasks, and the explicit no-go line is anything touching price-setting or direct customer relationships. Also a live example of pillar 2's boundary ("using AI for one part of a business didn't mean wanting it involved in every decision") operating inside a pillar-3 decision, i.e. the two starting points interlock in practice.
- **Caveat:** single-vendor blog publishing its own commissioned survey (Hostinger sells hosting/website tools) — no independent pollster disclosed, and methodology beyond sample size/dates is not detailed in the article. Cite the topline numbers, not Hostinger's own interpretive framing.

### S3 — Fresh national poll on AI-and-work dread; a harder-edged version of "overwhelmed," not a direct pillar-1 fit (Starting point 1 — gap narrowed, not closed)
- **Source:** Elon University Poll (fielded by SSRS)
- **URL:** https://www.elon.edu/u/news/2026/10/13/elon-survey-finds-americans-fear-ai-will-hollow-out-the-meaning-of-work/
- **Sample:** 1,158 U.S. adults, including a 775-worker oversample | **Field dates:** 2026-08-20 to 2026-08-24 | **Margin of error:** ±3.4 points at 95% confidence | **Published:** 2026-10-13 | **Captured:** 2026-10-14 (1 day old)
- **Exact data:** 62% expect most available jobs to be "dull and unfulfilling"; 65% agree that if AI makes work less meaningful, people will lose their sense of purpose and dignity; 83% expect AI to eliminate more jobs than it creates; 52% agree AI's impact will leave most workers with little control over their futures; 80% of recent hands-on LLM users still expect net job loss despite the experience.
- **Read as:** the freshest and most rigorously fielded source checked this run (disclosed pollster, field dates, margin of error, one day old), but it is dread/meaning-of-work anxiety, not literally "I don't know where to begin with AI" — the pillar-1 framing on the website. Logging it as a harder, more existential register of the same "overwhelmed by AI" audience condition named in `positioning.md`, useful for naming the fear underneath the hesitation rather than as a direct pillar-1 statistic. The 2026-10-02 PwC figure (51% can access the AI learning they need, down from 59%) remains the standing direct-fit evidence for pillar 1's literal "where to begin" framing; that gap is not fully closed by this source.
- **Caveat:** general U.S. population, not the experienced-professional/founder audience specifically; do not cite the job-loss percentages as if they describe this audience's risk — cite them only for the emotional register (dread, loss of control) they establish.

## internal-research evidence (private — tooling/build subjects, not for public content)

### R1 — Independent audit found agent benchmarks can be gamed to perfect scores without solving the underlying task (agent-evaluation integrity)
- **Source:** Wang, Li, Mang, Cheung, Sen, Song (UC Berkeley) — "Do Androids Dream of Breaking the Game? Systematically Auditing AI Agent Benchmarks with BenchJack" (arXiv)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12673
- **Submitted:** 2026-05-12 | **Captured:** 2026-10-14 (~5 months old)
- **Exact data:** An automated red-teaming tool (BenchJack) found 219 distinct exploitable flaws across 8 flaw categories in 10 widely used agent benchmarks (software engineering, web navigation, desktop computing, terminal operations), producing agents that scored at or near 100% on several benchmarks — including a 10-line Python file scoring perfectly on SWE-bench Verified and an empty JSON object completing all 890 FieldWorkArena tasks — without the agent solving a single real underlying task. The same pipeline reduced the exploitable-task ratio from near 100% to under 10% on four benchmarks, with two (WebArena, OSWorld) fully patched within three iterations.
- **Read as:** direct relevance to this content-system's own build posture — any internal claim that an agent/workflow "passed" a benchmark or validation check is only as trustworthy as the check's own adversarial robustness. Connects to the 2026-10-12 report's R2 (ThinkingBox: agents self-report "done" on work the backend didn't actually complete) — this is the evaluation-side mirror of that same failure mode: not just agents misreporting their own state, but benchmarks themselves failing to verify state at all. Internal build interest only, not eligible for public shift-lead content per `positioning.md`'s private-operating-subjects rule.
- **Caveat:** did not independently verify BenchJack's own exploit code or re-run the audit; relying on the paper's self-reported methodology and topline numbers. ~5 months old — flagging as still the most concrete, named, verifiable source on this exact failure mode found this run, not as breaking news.
