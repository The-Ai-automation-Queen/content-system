# Carousel Playbook — One Message Per Slide, Pain → Relief

**Prepared for:** Fatiha Chikh / AI Strategic Advisor
**Date:** 2026-06-17
**Use:** Load this before designing or rewriting any carousel. It replaces the "list of topics on slides" habit with a "felt journey" structure.

---

## Why your current carousels need this

Entries 009 and 018 (the AI Maturity "Reality Check" carousels) are organized by **coverage** — slide 1 does the cost of manual work, slide 2 does the five stages, slide 3 does the rules, and so on. That is a table of contents, not a story.

A carousel that converts is not a summary. It is a **descent into a pain the reader already feels, then a climb into the relief they want.** One message per slide is what makes that journey legible at swipe speed.

---

## Rule 1 — One message per slide

Each slide carries **exactly one idea**, readable in under two seconds.

- **≤ 12 words of body copy per slide.** If it needs a second sentence to land, it is two slides.
- **Big text, no paragraphs.** The slide is a billboard, not a page. If they have to lean in to read, you lost them.
- **One idea = one job.** A slide either names the pain, twists it, reframes it, or relieves it. Never two jobs on one slide.
- **The swipe test:** read only the headline of each slide in sequence. If the story still makes sense with nothing else, the spine is right. If it collapses, the messages are doing too much.

The reason is mechanical, not stylistic: one clean idea per slide increases dwell time (they swipe to complete the thought) and increases saves (each slide is independently screenshot-able). Saves and dwell time are what the algorithm rewards.

---

## Rule 2 — The Pain → Relief arc (the only structure that converts)

Every carousel walks this eight-beat arc. One slide per beat (you can split the "shift" across 2 slides if needed, but never merge beats).

| # | Beat | Its one job | What it does to the reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cover — name the pain** | State the reader's pain in their own words, as a hook | "That is me." Stops the scroll. |
| 2 | **Agitate** | Make the pain concrete and a little worse | "Yes — and it is costing me." Earns the swipe. |
| 3 | **Reframe** | Reveal the real problem is not what they thought | "Oh. I have been solving the wrong thing." |
| 4 | **The shift** | The one idea that changes everything | Hope appears. |
| 5 | **The shift, made real** | A concrete proof or example of the idea working | "This is actually possible." |
| 6 | **The relief** | Paint the desired state — life on the other side | Desire. "I want that." |
| 7 | **The bridge** | What it takes to get there (small, not scary) | "That is doable." |
| 8 | **CTA — save / share / DM** | One specific next action | They act while the desire is warm. |

**The cover is 80% of the result.** Apply Chris Donnelly's test before you design anything else: *"Is this slide good enough to stop someone mid-scroll?"* If the cover names a pain the reader feels in their body, the rest of the carousel gets read. If it states a fact, it gets skipped.

---

## Rule 3 — Lead with pain, sell the relief, never the feature

Your reader (a mid-market CEO, 35+) does not want "an AI maturity framework." That is a feature. They want the **relief** the framework delivers:

- They feel: *"I cannot tell if we are behind. I might be wasting money. I look uncertain in front of my board."*
- They want: *"To know exactly where we stand, what the next move is, and to stop guessing."*

So the carousel never opens with the framework. It opens with the uncertainty, and the framework arrives at beat 4 as the **way out of the pain** — never as the subject.

**Translation rule:** every slide, ask "is this about my expertise, or about their feeling?" If it is about your expertise, rewrite it toward what they feel or want.

---

## Voice rules (from the inspiration library — do not break these)

- Non-contracted English. Short. Practitioner-grounded.
- No jargon on a slide, ever. No "leverage," "ecosystem," "transformation journey," "scalable."
- No engagement bait ("like if you agree," "tag someone").
- One clear point of view. Take a side. Do not present "both perspectives."

## The 60-second pre-design checklist

Before any carousel goes to design, confirm:

- [ ] The cover names a **pain**, not a fact or a topic.
- [ ] Reading only the **headlines** in sequence still tells the story.
- [ ] **No slide carries two ideas.** (If it does, split it.)
- [ ] Body copy on every slide is **≤ 12 words**.
- [ ] There is a clear **relief slide** (beat 6) where the reader feels desire.
- [ ] The **final slide has one specific action** — save, send-to-a-named-person, or comment trigger.
- [ ] Zero jargon. Read every slide aloud.
- [ ] It belongs to **one content pillar** (see content-storytelling-plan.md).
