THE BASICS

Agentic AI starts when AI stops only answering and starts doing.

The useful question is not “Is this agentic?” It is “What can it do without asking me first?”

What is agentic AI?

Agentic AI is AI that can take actions toward a goal instead of giving one answer and stopping.

A normal chatbot might tell you how to organise a meeting.

An AI agent might check calendars, suggest times, prepare the invitation and wait for you to approve it.

PICTURE IT

Chatbot: “Here is what you could do.”
Agent: “I did the first steps. Here is what needs your approval.”

What is an AI agent?

An AI agent is software that can use AI plus tools to complete several steps of a task.

Those tools might include a browser, email, calendar, files, a database or another business app.

The agent still needs instructions, access and limits. It is not an independent employee.

The four levels of AI autonomy

It helps to think of agentic AI as four levels.

1. Answers

The AI gives you information and stops.

Example

“Write a reply to this customer.”

2. Uses tools

The AI looks things up or reads files before answering.

Example

“Read these three reports and tell me where they disagree.”

3. Acts with approval

The AI prepares an action, then waits for you.

Example

Draft the email, choose the recipient, then stop before Send.

4. Acts alone

The AI completes the action without waiting for you.

Example

Send the email, update the CRM and book the meeting automatically.

Why does approval matter?

The risk changes when AI moves from creating work to changing the real world.

A wrong draft can be fixed before anyone sees it.

A wrong email that was already sent can damage a relationship.

A wrong payment can cost money.

THE SIMPLE RULE

The harder an action is to undo, the more human approval you need.

What are AI agent permissions?

Permissions are what the agent is technically allowed to access or do.

If an AI only needs to draft replies, it may need permission to read email and create drafts. It does not need permission to send.

This is stronger than writing “do not send” inside a prompt.

PICTURE IT

A childproof lock is stronger than a note that says “please do not open this cupboard.”

What are guardrails in agentic AI?

Guardrails are rules and checks that keep an AI agent inside the job you gave it.

Examples:

  • Never send money
  • Never delete a customer record
  • Ask for approval before sending an external email
  • Stop if the information is incomplete
  • Log every action so a person can review it

What is a good first AI agent task?

Start with work that is repeatable, easy to check and easy to undo.

Good examples:

  • Sort incoming emails and draft replies
  • Prepare a morning summary from several sources
  • Turn meeting notes into action items
  • Check new leads against clear rules
  • Prepare a report from data that already exists

A bad first task is something where one wrong action can create a serious problem.

AI agent example: inbox manager

Here is a simple agentic workflow:

FLOW

New email → AI reads it → sorts it → drafts a reply → you approve → email sends.

The AI is doing several steps. The human still controls the action that reaches the customer.

When should an AI agent act alone?

Only after the task has become boringly predictable.

Before removing approval, check three things:

  1. The agent has done the same job correctly many times.
  2. The action is easy to reverse or low risk.
  3. You can see what the agent did without hunting for it.

How is agentic AI different from automation?

Automation follows fixed rules. Agentic AI can make choices between steps using AI.

Automation: “If a form arrives, send this exact confirmation email.”

Agentic AI: “Read the form, decide which type of request it is, prepare the right response, then ask me before sending.”

You often need both. The automation moves the work. The AI handles the part that needs interpretation.

The question to ask any AI agent vendor

What can this system do without asking me first?

Then ask:

  • What can it access?
  • What can it change?
  • What needs approval?
  • How do I see what it did?
  • How do I stop it?

Those answers tell you more than the word “agentic” on a pricing page.
