Tool verdict / Google

Gemini

FREE TOOL VERDICT · Updated 28 Jul 2026 · 5 min read

The one that already has your calendar, your Drive and your inbox. That's the whole argument for it.

  • Best forTeams already living in Google Workspace, and research across your own files.
  • Careful withOpening up your whole Drive before you have checked what it can see.
  • What I would doIf your business runs on Google, start here. If it doesn't, skip it.

Where it earns its keep

Every other tool on this site starts each conversation knowing nothing about you. Gemini starts already knowing what is in your calendar next Tuesday, which document you edited last night, and what that client emailed you on Friday. Nothing else can do that, and for a small business it's worth more than a few points on a benchmark.

Ask it to pull the three action items out of yesterday's meeting notes and put them in an email to your supplier, and it doesn't need you to paste anything in. The material is already there. That removes the copy-and-paste step, and the copy-and-paste step is the reason most people quietly stop using AI after a fortnight.

Deep Research is genuinely strong. It will work for several minutes and come back with a structured, sourced report rather than a paragraph. NotebookLM, which sits alongside it, is the tool I recommend most often to people who have a folder of PDFs they have been meaning to read for six months. Point it at the folder and ask it questions.

It also generates images and video, holds a live voice conversation, and can run a scheduled action so a summary lands in your inbox every Monday morning without you asking.

Where it'll burn you

The access that makes it useful is the thing to be deliberate about. When you switch on Workspace access you're giving it your mail, your files and your calendar. For most small businesses that's a fine trade. But go and look at what you have actually shared, particularly if your Drive contains client contracts, staff records or anything you signed an agreement about. Do that before you switch it on, not after.

It's also the most uneven of the big three. On a good day it's excellent. On a bad day it will misread a document you can see perfectly well, or refuse something entirely reasonable. Claude and ChatGPT are steadier. Gemini has a wider spread between its best and its worst answer, so check more, not less.

And Google keeps renaming things. The plan you bought will be called something else in six months, and features move between tiers. Don't build a process that depends on a specific product name staying put.

Do this

Before you connect anything, open your Drive and search it for the words "contract", "invoice", "passport" and "salary". Whatever comes back is what you're about to share. Decide with that list in front of you.

You're not buying the smartest model. You're buying the one that already knows where everything is.

What I'd actually do

If your business runs on Gmail, Google Docs and Google Calendar, start here and don't overthink it. The free tier will show you the shape of it within a week, and the paid tiers mostly buy you more usage and the better models.

If your business runs on Microsoft, this is the wrong tool and Copilot is the right one, even though Copilot is more expensive. Buy the one that sits where your work already is. Everything else is a detail.

The words you'll keep hearing

  • Gems. A saved setup with its own instructions, for a task you repeat. The same idea as Custom GPTs.
  • Deep Research. A long sourced report instead of an instant answer. One of the better versions of this on the market.
  • NotebookLM. Ask questions of your own documents. If you take one thing from this page, take this one.
  • Canvas. A side panel where a document or a piece of code is built and edited, instead of scrolling back through chat.
  • Scheduled actions. It runs on a timer without being asked. Useful for a Monday morning summary.
  • Grounding. Checking its answer against Google Search before giving it to you. Reduces invented facts. Doesn't remove them.

Is it the right one for you?

Yes, if your working day happens inside Google. The access advantage is real and it compounds, because the tool that needs no setup is the tool you'll still be using in March.

No, if you're on Microsoft, or if your work is long-form reading and writing where Claude is simply better, or if you want one steady generalist and would rather have ChatGPT.
