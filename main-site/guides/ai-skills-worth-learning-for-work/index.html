Introduction

Any AI · 20 min read · Beginner

Nobody can promise what AI will change. What makes you valuable is the part AI can't do alone: briefing it well, catching its mistakes and deciding what stays human. Practise those three on real work.

A guide by Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

What you leave with: 3 skills with a 10-minute exercise each, a prompt for each exercise, and a way to show the result to your manager.

01

How to use this guideIn 30 seconds.

Pick one task you do every week. Use it for all three exercises, so you can see the difference each skill makes.

02

Skill 1Brief it.

The skill:
turning a vague request into a clear brief.
Why it matters:
most bad AI answers come from a thin request. The fix is to let the AI ask what it needs to know.
The vague request

Write a LinkedIn post about our new service.

→
What a good brief looks like

Who it's for · the one problem it solves · one real example · the tone · the length · what not to say.

Skill 2Check it.

The skill:
spotting what's wrong in an answer that sounds right.
Why it matters:
this is the skill managers notice. Anyone can get an AI draft; few people check it properly.
The AI answer

The report shows sales rose 12% in March, driven mainly by the new pricing.

→
What checking finds

The report says 8%, and never mentions pricing as the reason.

Skill 3Own it.

The skill:
splitting a task into what AI can draft and what you must decide.
Why it matters:
judgement, relationships and responsibility stay with you. Being clear about that is what makes you trusted.
The task

Reply to an unhappy client about a late delivery.

→
A good split

AI drafts the apology and the timeline · you decide the offer, the tone with this client, and whether to call instead.

The honest partCheck before you use it.

No course or certificate protects a job. Being the person who knows what good looks like, and can prove the AI's work is right, does.

Skill 1: The "interview me first" prompt

I need [what you want, for example a LinkedIn post about our new service].

Before you write anything, ask me up to 5 questions you need answered to do this well. Ask them one at a time and wait for my answer each time. Then write it.

Skill 2: The fact-check prompt

Check this answer against the source I've pasted.

For each claim, tell me: supported (quote the source) · not supported · contradicted. Be strict. If the source doesn't say it, it's not supported.

Skill 3: The task-split prompt

Here's a task: [describe it].

Split it into two lists:
1. What AI could draft or prepare for me.
2. What I should decide or do myself, and why.

Be honest about the parts where getting it wrong would hurt a relationship, cost money or break a rule.

Show your manager: one before-and-after from this week. The vague request vs. the brief, or the mistake you caught.
