How to Get ChatGPT to Recommend My Business

Check what it says about you and fix it.

Start the guide ↓
Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen Free guide
Shift & Lead

When people ask ChatGPT for someone like me, I don't show up.

You can't buy your way into AI answers. You can check what AI finds today, fix the facts it gets wrong or can't find, and make your own pages say clearly who you help and how.

What you leave with

a client-style search to run every month, a check on what AI can verify about you, and a prompt that rewrites your services page around facts AI can find.

How to use this guide: Do job 1 before you change anything, and save the answer. That's your "before".

01

Job 1 Search like a client.

The job:
seeing who AI recommends for what you do.
Why it matters:
your clients don't search for your name. They describe their problem.
The example

I'm a small café owner in Lyon. I need a bookkeeper who understands French VAT. Who should I compare?

→
What a good answer shows you

Which providers come up, what AI says each one does, and the pages it used.

Job 2 See what AI knows about you.

The job:
checking what AI can confirm about your business.
Why it matters:
missing or wrong facts are usually the reason you don't appear.
What a good answer looks like

What you do, who for, where, with a link for each fact · plus a list of what it couldn't confirm.

→
What to look for

Anything missing, out of date or contradicted between your website and your profiles.

03

Job 3 Fix the one page that matters.

The job:
making your services page clear about who you help, what you do and why it's true.
Why it matters:
clear, checkable facts on your own site are what AI tools can find and repeat.
Before

We offer tailored financial solutions for your business needs.

→
After

"Bookkeeping and VAT returns for cafés and restaurants in Lyon. 40 local clients. Monthly fixed fee."

04

The honest part Check before you use it.

No one can promise you'll appear in AI answers, and more AI-written pages won't help. Clear facts, consistent everywhere, are the part you control.

✓

Your prompts Ready to copy.

I'm a [type of customer] in [place]. I need [the problem you solve, in your client's words].

Which providers should I compare? For each one: what they offer, and the public page you're basing that on (with the link). Don't invent reviews or prices.
What can you find about [your business name] in [place]?

List each fact with its source link: what we do, who for, where, prices if public. Then list what you couldn't find or confirm, and anything that looks out of date or contradictory.
Rewrite my services page using only these facts:
- Who I help: [type of client]
- What I do: [each service in plain words]
- Where: [place or online]
- Proof: [numbers, examples, credentials you can back up]

Start with one sentence a client would search for. Use plain words, no buzzwords. Don't add any claim that isn't in my facts.

Every month: run job 1 again, save the answer next to last month's, and fix one missing fact.
