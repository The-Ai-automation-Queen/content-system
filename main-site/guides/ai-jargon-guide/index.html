What Do AI Words Like "Agent" and "Hallucination" Mean?

Six AI words in plain English, and a short test for each.

Start the guide ↓
Fatiha Chikh Free guide
Shift & Lead

Six AI words, made plain

People say agent and hallucination, and you nod along. This guide explains 6 words and shows each one in a chat. It takes 20 minutes.

What you end up with

  • Six AI words you can explain in one line
  • A made-up answer you caught yourself
  • Your memory setting found and checked
  • A one-page cheat sheet with a question for each word

You need a ChatGPT or Claude account. The free plan works. Copy each prompt as it is and change the [brackets].

01

Learn the six words One short table.

Each word changes how far you can trust an answer. Read them once.

WordPlain meaningWhy it matters
HallucinationThe AI makes something up and sounds sureA made-up fact looks just like a real one
SourceWhere a fact came from: a file, a web page, your notesOpen it yourself before you tell a client
Context windowEverything the AI can see in this one chatIn a long chat it can lose early details
MemoryA setting that may keep details between chatsKnow if it is on
AgentAI that does steps by itself: clicking, filling in, sendingIt can act without you
ConnectorA link that lets AI into your email, files or calendarIt can read client details
  1. Open ChatGPT ↗ or Claude ↗ and sign in.
  2. For each word, write one job in your business where it matters.
02

Catch a made-up answer Hallucination and source.

You ask about a book that does not exist. Watch what the AI does.

  1. Click New chat.
  2. Paste the prompt below. Press Enter.
Summarise the business book "The Velvet Ledger" by Orla Penrith.
Give me its 3 main lessons for small business owners.
Then give me a link to a real source that shows this book exists.
If you can't, tell me which parts you made up.
  1. A summary with no real link? It made it up. The book does not exist.
  2. Got a link? Click it. Check the title and author are on the page.
  3. Before you tell a client a fact, ask where it came from. Then open it.
03

Test its memory Context window and memory.

One chat can see what you told it. A new chat may not.

  1. Click New chat. Paste the prompt below. Press Enter.
  2. Click New chat again. Type: "What is my next workshop called? If you don't know, say so."
Remember this: my next client workshop is called Bluebird and it runs on Friday.
  1. It says it doesn't know? Memory is off, or it did not save this.
  2. It says Bluebird? Memory is on.
  3. ChatGPT: click your name, then Settings, then Personalization, then Memory.
  4. Claude: click your name, then Settings, then Capabilities.
  5. Either way, paste key details into each new chat. Don't count on memory.

Can't find it? The menus move. Search "memory" on OpenAI's help pages ↗ or Claude's help centre ↗.

04

Answers versus actions Agent and connector.

A chat gives you words to send. An agent with a connector can act in your email or calendar.

Explain the difference between these two requests in 3 lines:
1. "Write a reply to [client] saying I can't make Thursday's call."
2. "Decline Thursday's call with [client] in my calendar and email them to rebook."

Then tell me:
- what an AI needs access to for the second one
- what could go wrong for a small business
- 3 rules to set before AI acts in my email or calendar
Plain English.
  1. Click your name, then Settings.
  2. Open Apps or Connectors.
  3. See a tool you don't use? Click it, then Disconnect.

The honest part: if AI can send, book or delete for you, it can get it wrong for you. Do that last step yourself.

05

Make your cheat sheet One page you keep.

Each word gets one question you ask before you trust an answer.

I run a small business: [what you sell and who to].
Write me a one-page AI cheat sheet.

First ask me these, one at a time, and wait for each answer:
1. Which AI tools do I use, and for which jobs?
2. Is anything connected: email, files, calendar?
3. Which client details must stay private?

Then make a table:
Word | Plain meaning | Where it shows up in my business | The question I ask

Use these words and questions:
hallucination: "Is every fact here in what I gave it?"
source: "Where did this come from, and have I opened it?"
context window: "Did I paste the key details in this chat?"
memory: "Do I know what it keeps between chats?"
agent: "What can it do without me?"
connector: "What can it see, and does this job need it?"

Use only what I tell you. If something is missing, write [CHECK].
Never make up names, tools or settings. Under 300 words.
  1. Fill in every [CHECK] yourself.
  2. Paste it into a note called AI cheat sheet, or print it.
