USE AI BETTER

A good AI prompt is just a clear brief.

If another person could not do the job from your instructions, AI probably cannot do it well either.

What is an AI prompt?

An AI prompt is the instruction you give an AI tool such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.

It can be one sentence, a long brief, a file, an image or a conversation. The important part is not the length. The important part is whether the AI knows what job you want done.

PICTURE IT

A prompt is the note you hand to a helper before they start the job.

Why do vague prompts give vague answers?

If you type, “Write me a marketing email,” the AI has to guess almost everything.

Who is the email for? What are you selling? What happened before this email? What tone do you want? How long should it be?

When the brief is empty, the AI fills the gaps with generic choices.

The four parts of a useful AI prompt

You do not need a complicated prompt formula. Start with four things:

  1. Goal. What should the AI do?
  2. Context. What does it need to know?
  3. Example. What does good look like?
  4. Format. What should come back?

THE SIMPLE FORMULA

Goal + context + example + format.

AI prompt example: weak vs useful

Weak prompt: “Write a follow-up email.”

Useful prompt: “Write a follow-up email to a client who asked for a quote two weeks ago and has not replied. We have worked together before, so keep it warm and direct. Keep it under 100 words. Give them an easy way to tell me the timing is wrong.”

The second prompt works better because the AI can see the situation.

What does context mean in a prompt?

Context is the information the AI needs before it can do a good job.

Think about what a new person would ask you before starting:

  • Who is this for?
  • What happened before?
  • What are we trying to achieve?
  • What should we avoid?
  • What information is already available?

If the answer matters, include it.

Why examples improve AI prompts

Examples are often faster than long explanations.

If you want the AI to match your tone, paste one email you already like. If you want a certain structure, show one example of that structure.

PICTURE IT

Telling someone “make it warm” is vague. Showing them one warm email lets them see what you mean.

What should you tell AI not to do?

Useful prompts can include limits too.

  • No exclamation marks
  • Do not invent facts
  • Do not make legal claims
  • Do not use jargon
  • Ask me if information is missing

Limits are especially useful when the output could create risk.

Should you use role prompts?

Sometimes. “Act as a lawyer” or “act as a marketer” is not magic.

A useful role explains the point of view you need. For example: “Explain this like an accountant speaking to a small business owner who does not work with numbers every day.”

The job and context still matter more than the label.

How to improve a prompt after the first answer

You do not need to get the perfect prompt on the first try.

  • Make it shorter.
  • Use simpler words.
  • Give me three options.
  • Explain what you are unsure about.
  • Keep the facts but change the tone.

A good AI conversation often looks like editing with a fast assistant.

A prompt you can use today

COPY THIS

Goal: [What I need done]
Context: [What you need to know]
Example: [What good looks like]
Format: [How I want the answer]

The question to ask before you press send

Could a capable stranger do this job from what I wrote?

If the answer is no, add the missing information.

That one habit will improve your prompts more than collecting a hundred clever prompt tricks.
