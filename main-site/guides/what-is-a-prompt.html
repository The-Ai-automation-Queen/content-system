How to write an AI prompt that gets a useful answer

Stop guessing what to type. This guide shows you where a prompt goes, what to include and how to get an answer you can actually use.

A prompt is simply your instruction to the AI. It can be a question, a request or a set of directions.

You do not need a complicated formula. Tell the AI what you want, what it needs to know, what it must avoid and what the finished answer should look like.

Explore the guide one section at a time, then put it into practice.

Try it now

Use this for a real task you need to complete today.

I need help with [describe the task]. Before you answer, ask me up to 3 questions that would help you give me a more useful result. Use only the information I provide. If something important is missing, tell me instead of making it up. The finished result should be [email, summary, checklist, plan or another format]. Keep it [short, friendly, professional, direct or another style].

Give an agent clear instructions

Try a complete example first, then adapt it to a task of your own.

Checklist progress stays on this device. Your typed content is not saved or sent to a service.

1. Start with a worked example

A prompt is the instruction you give AI. When an agent can take actions, also say what it may use, what it may change and what it must check. Its available tools and permissions determine what it can actually do.

These are fictional practice examples. You can copy one as it is before writing your own.

Prepare a meeting: complete agent instructions
View full instructions
Goal
Create an agenda for a 20-minute project planning meeting.

Context and allowed material
Use only the sample notes below: the launch date is undecided; the team needs to agree who writes the announcement and who checks the website. The audience is a small project team.

What to deliver
A table with topic, minutes and decision needed. Leave the owner as ‘to agree’ where no name is supplied.

Permissions and limits
Draft the agenda here. Do not create calendar events, contact anyone or change files.

Before starting
If essential information or access is missing, ask me a short, specific question. Explain unfamiliar steps in plain language.

Completion check
Check that the timings total 20 minutes and both decisions in the notes are covered. Do not invent names or dates.
Report what you completed, what you checked and anything still needing my input. Do not claim an action succeeded unless you verified it.
Why these instructions work
  • Goal: gives the agent one concrete job.
  • Material: tells it which information to use instead of guessing.
  • Deliverable: describes the result you want back.
  • Limits: separates work it can do from actions needing your permission.
  • Check: makes completion something you can review.

What to check in this example: Check that the timings total 20 minutes and both decisions in the notes are covered. Do not invent names or dates.

2. Try it and check the result

  1. Open the AI tool you already use and start a new conversation or task.
  2. Click Copy on the example or your adapted instructions, then paste them into the message box.
  3. Read them before sending. Replace any example details and use only information you are allowed to share.
  4. For a file-building task, select the intended project in your agent tool. If you do not know how, ask it to explain the next step before proceeding.
  5. Send the instructions. Answer any clarification questions, then compare the result with the completion check.

If something is missing, reply: “Check your result against my instructions. Tell me what is missing, then correct it within the same permissions.”

Give the AI enough to work with

A useful prompt normally gives the AI 4 things.

The job

Say exactly what you want it to do, such as draft an email, compare 2 options or summarise a document.

The context

Explain who the result is for, what has happened and which details matter.

The boundaries

Set the length, tone, sources, exclusions or facts it must not invent.

The format

Ask for the result as an email, checklist, table, plan or another format you can use.

See the difference

The improvement is not a clever phrase. It is the missing information.

Too vague

Write an email.

No limit or tone is given.

More useful

Write a friendly follow-up email to a potential client who requested a proposal 5 days ago.

Keep it under 120 words, do not sound pushy and end with one clear question.

Let the AI ask before it answers

You will not always know which details the AI needs. Ask it to question you before it starts.

This is often more useful than writing a very long prompt yourself because it makes the missing information visible.
