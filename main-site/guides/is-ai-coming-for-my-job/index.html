Introduction

Any AI · 15 min read · Beginner

AI changes tasks, not whole jobs, at least at first. List what you actually do each week, sort each task honestly, and put your energy into the parts that need you.

A guide by Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

What you leave with: a list of your real tasks, an honest sort of what AI can and can't take on, and a 90-day plan for the one skill that matters most.

01

How to use this guideIn 30 seconds.

Look at last week's calendar and sent emails before you start. Describe your work in general terms, with no company or client names.

05

The honest partCheck before you use it.

Nobody can tell you exactly how your job will change, and any tool that gives you a precise score is guessing. What you can control is how well you use AI on your own work, and how clearly you can show the value of the parts only you do.

✦Your turn

Unlock the 3 prompts.

Your prompts ready to copy.

Job 1: The task list prompt

Help me list the tasks in my job. My role: [general job title and sector, no company names].

Ask me up to 5 questions about my typical week, one at a time. Then list my 8 to 10 main tasks, with roughly how many hours a week each one takes.

Job 2: The task sort prompt

Sort each task into one of 3 groups:
1. AI can draft it (I check it).
2. AI can help, but I lead.
3. Stays with me.

For each task, give a one-line reason. Be realistic about what today's AI tools can do. If it depends on my company's rules or tools, say so. Don't give me a risk score.

Job 3: The 90-day skill prompt

Looking at my "stays with me" tasks and the tasks AI can help with, suggest the one skill that would make me most valuable in my role.

Give me a 90-day plan: one step per month, using my real work, not a course. End with one piece of proof I could show my manager.

Before you trust the sort: would your manager agree with the "stays with me" list? Anything you're unsure about, test with AI on a real task this week.
