One practical prompt

From rough note to useful answer

Start with this made-up project note

We are preparing a website launch. The landing page is approved. The designer expects final graphics by Friday. Pricing still needs approval. The team meets on Tuesday. We have not decided if the launch should wait for pricing approval.

The jobMake a 15-minute meeting agenda.
The resultThree agenda items and one decision.
The checkPricing is still waiting for approval.
A useful answer would show
  • The approved landing page and graphics update.
  • Pricing approval as an open item.
  • A decision about launch timing, with no invented owner.
Copy the complete worked example
I need help with one work task. Use the information I provide and follow the requested result exactly.

THE JOB
Turn this rough project note into an agenda for a 15-minute team meeting.

WHAT YOU CAN USE
We are preparing a website launch. The landing page is approved. The designer expects final graphics by Friday. Pricing still needs approval. The team meets on Tuesday. We have not decided if the launch should wait for pricing approval.

WHAT I NEED BACK
Write three agenda items. For each, give the update or decision needed and one useful question. End with a single line headed ‘Decision to make’. Keep the whole answer under 130 words.

WHAT TO CHECK
Use only the note above. Do not invent people, owners, dates, approvals or promises. If an owner is needed but not named, write ‘Not specified’. Make the unresolved pricing approval visible as a decision, not as a completed fact.

Before you answer, check that the note contains enough information for the requested result. If a crucial detail is missing, ask me up to three specific questions and stop. Otherwise, produce the result without adding an introduction or extra task. Mark any detail you cannot confirm from the note as ‘Not specified’; do not fill it in yourself.

At the end, add a short ‘Check before use’ line naming the facts I should compare with my original note.
