PUT AI TO WORK

Three AI tools are enough if each one has a real job.

You do not need a folder full of AI apps. You need a small stack where every tool owns a clear piece of work.

What is an AI tool stack?

An AI tool stack is the small set of tools you use together to get work done.

The mistake is choosing tools because they are impressive. The better question is: what job does this tool own?

THE SIMPLE MODEL

Brain → megaphone → net.

Tool 1: the brain

The brain is the AI assistant you use for thinking, drafting, research and preparing work.

That could be ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.

The brand matters less than the job.

The brain should own tasks like:

  • Drafting a first version
  • Summarising long information
  • Comparing options
  • Preparing research
  • Turning raw notes into useful structure

PICTURE IT

The brain is the person at the desk turning messy information into something ready to use.

Tool 2: the megaphone

The megaphone gets finished work out to the right place.

That might be a social scheduler, email platform or publishing tool.

The megaphone should own tasks like:

  • Scheduling posts
  • Publishing across channels
  • Sending newsletters
  • Keeping a publishing queue moving

The AI does not have to publish by itself. A human approval step can stay at the end.

Tool 3: the net

The net catches the attention your content creates.

This is usually your CRM, form system or follow-up tool.

The net should own tasks like:

  • Saving new leads
  • Recording where they came from
  • Scheduling follow-up
  • Making sure nobody disappears

PICTURE IT

The brain makes the useful thing. The megaphone gets it seen. The net catches the people who raise their hand.

Why three tools can beat ten

Every extra tool creates another login, another bill, another place where information can get stuck and another workflow you have to maintain.

Overlap is the real waste.

If two tools do the same job and neither clearly owns it, you will keep switching between them.

How to choose your AI stack

Write down the three jobs before you choose the products.

BrainWhich tool helps me think, draft and prepare?
MegaphoneWhich tool reliably publishes or sends?
NetWhich tool captures people and keeps follow-up moving?

Then choose one tool for each job.

What should you cancel?

Look at every AI and automation subscription you pay for.

Ask:

  • What job does this tool own?
  • Did I use it in the last 30 days?
  • Would I notice if it disappeared tomorrow?
  • Does another tool already do the same job?

If you cannot explain its job in one sentence, it probably does not belong in the stack.

What order should you build the stack?

Start with the brain because it helps create useful work.

Add the net next because capturing existing demand is more important than creating more noise.

Add the megaphone once you have something worth publishing consistently.

THE ORDER

1. Brain
2. Net
3. Megaphone

A simple example

Brain: Claude prepares a useful guide.

Megaphone: your scheduler turns the guide into planned social posts.

Net: your CRM captures people who ask for the guide and reminds you to follow up.

Three tools. Three jobs. One flow.

The rule to remember

Do not collect AI tools.

Give every tool one job, and remove the tools that do not own anything.
