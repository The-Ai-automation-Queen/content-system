Introduction

Any AI · 20 min read · Beginner

AI can't decide for you, and it tends to agree with however you ask. It can help if you describe both options equally, ask five advisors with different jobs to weigh in, and turn the result into a small test.

A guide by Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

What you leave with: a fair way to describe your decision, a 5-advisor prompt, and a 30-day test that gives you real evidence before you decide.

01

How to use this guideIn 30 seconds.

Do job 1 on paper first. Leave out your employer's name and anything confidential. Then run jobs 2 and 3 in the same chat.

02

Job 1Describe both options fairly.

The job:
writing the decision down so it doesn't lean one way.
Why it works:
"I'm miserable and thinking of leaving" already contains the answer. Two equal descriptions, with the good and bad of each, give the AI nothing to agree with.
Leaning

I hate my job and want to go freelance. Should I?

→
Fair

"Option A: stay. Steady salary, good team, no growth for 2 years. Option B: freelance. More control, 4 months of savings, two possible first clients."

03

Job 2Hear 5 advisors.

The job:
looking at the decision from five angles you'd normally mix together.
Why it works:
one answer blends hope, fear and money into one paragraph. Five advisors keep them apart, and one of them is required to argue against you.
The 5 advisors

The Bank Balance (runway and the worst month) · The Tuesday Test (what an ordinary day looks like in each option) · The Hiring Manager (how each choice looks on your CV in 3 years) · The Person Who Knows You (what you've said you want before) · The Contrarian (the best case for the option you're leaning against).

→
What a good answer looks like

Five short, different views, then a summary of where they agree, where they clash, and what you'd need to know to decide.

Job 3Test before you decide.

The job:
turning the advice into something you can try without quitting yet.
Why it works:
a small test gives you facts instead of feelings: can you win a client, do you enjoy the work, does the money add up?
A test

Land one paid freelance project on evenings and weekends in the next 30 days.

→
What a good test looks like

One clear goal, a date, and the result that would change your mind agreed in advance ("no paid project in 30 days means I stay and try again in the spring").

The honest partCheck before you use it.

This is your life and your money. Use the advisors to see the decision clearly, then talk it through with someone who knows you, and with a qualified adviser for anything about tax, contracts or benefits.

Job 1: The fair description prompt

I'm deciding between two options. Help me describe them fairly before we discuss anything.

Option A: [describe]
Option B: [describe]

Rewrite both in the same format: what I gain · what I give up · what I don't know yet. Remove any wording that makes one sound better. Don't give me your opinion yet.

Job 2: The 5-advisor prompt

Now look at my decision as 5 different advisors. Keep each view under 80 words.

1. The Bank Balance: my money, my runway and the worst month I could face.
2. The Tuesday Test: what an ordinary day would look like in each option.
3. The Hiring Manager: how each choice would look on my CV in 3 years.
4. The Person Who Knows Me: what I've told you matters to me.
5. The Contrarian: the strongest case for the option I seem to be leaning against.

Then summarise: where they agree, where they clash, and the 2 facts I'd need to decide.

Job 3: The 30-day test prompt

Based on this, design a 30-day test I can run without quitting.

Give me: one clear goal · the steps, week by week · how much time it needs each week · the result that would tell me to stay, and the result that would tell me to go.

Keep it realistic for someone with a full-time job.

Before you decide: both options described fairly · the Contrarian taken seriously · a real test result, not just a feeling.
