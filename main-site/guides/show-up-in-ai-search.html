How to Get ChatGPT to Recommend My Business

Check what it says about you and fix it. Then make your services page say clearly who you help.

Start the guide ↓
Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen Free guide
Shift & Lead

Your AI visibility audit, done in one sitting

When a client asks ChatGPT who to hire, it answers from what it can find about you online: your website, your profiles, your reviews and anything written about you. This guide shows you how to check what AI tools say about your business today, fix the facts they get wrong or can't find, and rewrite your services page so it says plainly what you do. Nobody can promise you a place in the answers. You can make sure every fact about you is correct and easy to find.

What you end up with

  • A list of the places AI tools read about you, each one checked
  • Your client search results: who ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity suggest when a client describes their problem
  • Your AI visibility audit: every fact AI tools state about you, marked correct, wrong or missing
  • A fix list: the exact facts to correct on each profile
  • A rewritten services page that says who you help, with what, where, and the proof you actually have

You need a ChatGPT account. The free plan works. Gemini and Perplexity are optional and free to try. Every prompt below can be copied and pasted as it is; replace the parts in [square brackets].

01

Know where AI looks The places that describe you.

AI tools have no private list of businesses. When they search the web (look up live pages instead of answering from memory), they read the same public pages a client would find. If those pages are missing, out of date or disagree with each other, the answer about you will be too.

  1. Open a new document or spreadsheet and call it AI visibility audit.
  2. Copy the table below into it and add a last column called Status.
  3. Open each place and write in the Status column whether it exists and whether it is up to date. Leave the fixes for step 04.
PlaceWhat it tells AI toolsHow to check
Your websiteWho you help, what you offer, where you workOpen your home page and services page as a visitor would
Google Business ProfileYour name, category, area, contact details and reviewsSearch your business name on Google, or sign in at business.google.com ↗
LinkedInYour headline, About section, services and postsOpen your profile and read it as a stranger would
Directories and listingsYour entry with professional bodies, industry directories or booking sitesSearch your business name plus your industry or town
Reviews and articlesWhat clients and other people say about youSearch your business name and read the first two pages of results

No Google Business Profile? Google's profiles are for businesses that meet clients in person or serve a local area. If you work only online, skip that row. If you're not sure, read Google's guidelines ↗.

02

Search like a client See who gets suggested.

Your clients rarely search for your name. They describe their problem and ask who can help. This step shows you what they see.

  1. Open ChatGPT ↗ and start a new chat. If you see a Search option or a globe icon, switch it on so ChatGPT looks at the web. If you don't, the prompt asks it to search.
  2. Paste the prompt below with your details filled in.
  3. Run the same prompt in Gemini ↗ and Perplexity ↗.
  4. Copy each answer into your audit document, with the tool name and today's date.
Search the web and answer as if I were a client.

I'm a [type of client] in [place, or "online"]. I need help with [the problem you solve, in your client's words].

Which providers should I compare? For each one, give me:
- their name
- what they offer, in one line
- the public page you based that on, with the link

Use only what you can find on public pages. Never invent reviews, prices or results. If you can't find a source for something, say so.

Example of a filled-in first line: "I'm a café owner in Lyon. I need a bookkeeper who understands French VAT."

Do this before you change anything. It is your "before" picture. You'll run the same prompt again in step 06 and compare.

03

Ask what AI knows Correct, wrong or missing.

Now check what AI tools can find and confirm about your business. Wrong and missing facts are the part you can fix.

  1. Start a new chat in each tool you used in step 02.
  2. Paste the prompt below with your business name and place.
  3. Copy every fact from each answer into the audit table below, one row per fact.
Search the web for [your business name] in [place, or "online"].

List every fact you can find about this business in a table with these columns:
Fact | Source link

Cover: what the business does, who it helps, where it works, the services offered, prices if public, reviews, and the name of the person behind it.

Then list:
1. What you couldn't find or confirm
2. Anything that looks out of date, or where two sources disagree

Use only what you find. Never guess or fill gaps. Write [NOT FOUND] where you have nothing.

Fill in your audit table. Mark each fact yourself: you are the only one who knows what's true.

FactWhat AI saidToolCorrect / wrong / missingSource it used
Who I help[what it said]ChatGPT[mark][link]
What I do[what it said]Gemini[mark][link]

AI said something wrong? Open the source link it gave. The mistake usually sits on that page, which tells you exactly where to fix it.

04

Write your fix list One fact, one place.

The fix list turns your audit into small jobs: which fact to correct, and on which page.

Here is my AI visibility audit: [paste the table].

Here are the correct facts about my business:
- Business name: [name]
- Who I help: [type of client]
- What I do: [services, in plain words]
- Where I work: [place, or online]
- How to contact or book: [link]

Make me a fix list as a table with these columns:
Place (website, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, or the directory's name) | What it says now | What it should say | Priority (high, medium, low)

High priority: anything wrong. Medium: anything missing. Low: small wording differences.

Use only the facts I gave you. Write [CHECK] where you are not sure what the correct fact is. Never invent services, reviews, results or qualifications.

Work through the list, high priority first:

  1. Use the same words for your name, services and area everywhere. Small differences make it harder for AI tools to match the pages to one business.
  2. Google Business Profile: sign in at business.google.com ↗ and edit your profile. Menus change. If you can't find the field, search for it on Google's help pages ↗.
  3. LinkedIn: edit your headline and About section so they use the same words as your website.
  4. Directories: log in and update your entry. If you can't, email the directory with the correct details.
  5. Tick each row in your fix list when it's done.

Few or no reviews? Ask two or three happy clients to leave one on your Google Business Profile or LinkedIn. Never write reviews yourself or ask AI to write them.

05

Rewrite your services page Clear facts AI can repeat.

Your website is the one place you fully control. When it says plainly who you help, with what, where, and why someone should believe you, AI tools have something clear to repeat.

Before (example)After (example)
We offer tailored financial solutions for your business needs.Bookkeeping and VAT returns for cafés and restaurants in Lyon, for a fixed monthly fee.
  1. Open your services page in your browser, select the text and copy it.
  2. Start a new chat in ChatGPT and paste the prompt below, with your page text at the end.
  3. Answer the questions one at a time.
Help me rewrite my services page. Ask me these one at a time and wait for each answer:
1. Who do I help? (type of client, size, situation)
2. What exactly do I do for them? (each service, in plain words)
3. Where do I work? (town, region, or online)
4. What proof do I have? (results, testimonials, qualifications, examples of work: only what I can show)
5. How does a client start? (book a call, send an enquiry, something else)

Then rewrite the page with these sections:
- One opening sentence a client would search for: who I help, with what, where
- Each service: a heading and 2 to 3 plain sentences
- Proof: only what I gave you
- How to start: one clear next step

Rules: plain English, short sentences, no buzzwords. Use only what I tell you. Write [CHECK] where information is missing instead of guessing. Never invent clients, results, numbers, reviews or qualifications.

My current page: [paste your services page text]

Read it line by line. Delete any claim you can't back up, fill in every [CHECK], then update the page on your website. Use the same opening sentence on your Google Business Profile and LinkedIn, so every place tells the same story.

Only name clients on your page if they have said yes. Swap any other names for [client] before you paste.

06

Check again in a month Compare before and after.

AI tools take time to pick up changes, and their answers shift from week to week. A monthly check shows you what moved.

  1. Put a reminder in your calendar for one month from today.
  2. Run the step 02 and step 03 prompts again, in the same tools.
  3. Paste the new answers into your audit next to the old ones, with the date.
  4. Fix one new wrong or missing fact each month.

The honest part: nobody can promise ChatGPT will recommend you, and nobody can buy a place in its answers. What you control is whether every fact about you is correct, clear and the same everywhere. That is what this audit checks.
