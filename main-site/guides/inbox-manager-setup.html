PUT AI TO WORK

Save time by letting AI organise your inbox

A useful AI inbox manager should reduce what you have to read, not create a new pile of summaries and alerts.

What is an AI inbox manager?

An AI inbox manager is a workflow that reads incoming email, sorts messages into useful groups, prepares summaries and can draft replies for you.

The safest first version does not send anything.

PICTURE IT

Email arrives → AI sorts it → AI drafts if needed → you see one clear list → you decide what gets sent.

What should the AI do?

Start with a small job.

For example, ask the AI to sort each email into four groups:

  • Needs me today
  • Needs a reply, but not today
  • Information only
  • Noise

Those four buckets are easier to trust than a complicated scoring system.

Step 1: connect Gmail or Outlook with read access

The AI needs permission to read the messages you want it to process.

Start with the minimum access possible. If the workflow only needs to read and draft, do not give it permission to delete or send.

THE RULE

Give the AI only the permission needed for the job it has today.

Step 2: define what each inbox category means

The AI cannot know what “urgent” means in your business unless you tell it.

Write simple rules.

Example:

  • Needs me today if it comes from an active client and blocks work
  • Needs a reply if someone asked a direct question
  • Information only if no action is requested
  • Noise if it is a newsletter, promotion or automated notification I do not need

Step 3: ask AI to explain the classification

Do not only ask for a label.

Ask for one short reason too.

EXAMPLE

Needs me today. Client says the launch is blocked until you approve the final copy.

That makes mistakes easier to spot.

Step 4: let AI draft easy replies

For messages that clearly need a response, AI can prepare a draft.

Give it examples of how you normally write. Tell it what it must never promise and what information it cannot invent.

Keep the draft inside Gmail or Outlook for review.

Why draft-only is safer

A prompt saying “do not send” is only an instruction.

A system that does not have permission to send cannot accidentally send.

PICTURE IT

Removing the Send permission is like taking away the car keys, not leaving a note that says “please do not drive.”

Step 5: create one daily inbox summary

The point is to open fewer messages.

Ask for one summary at a time that shows:

  • The messages that need you today
  • The drafts waiting for approval
  • Anything the AI was unsure about
  • Anything it could not process

Everything else can stay out of your way.

What should an AI inbox manager never do?

  • Delete messages automatically when you are still testing
  • Send sensitive replies without approval
  • Make legal or financial promises
  • Assume a person is unimportant because the message is short
  • Hide errors or uncertain classifications

A simple AI inbox workflow

FLOW

New email → AI reads it → assigns one of four buckets → drafts a reply if needed → adds it to your daily summary → you review.

How to test the inbox manager safely

Run it in shadow mode first.

That means the AI sorts and drafts, but you keep using your inbox normally for a few days.

Compare what the AI did with what you would have done.

When the mistakes become predictable, improve the rules. Do not remove review just because the first ten emails looked good.

What a good result looks like

You should not be reading every message anymore.

You should be looking at a short list that tells you:

  • What needs your decision
  • What already has a draft
  • What can wait
  • What went wrong

The AI handles the sorting. You keep the judgment.
