How to Make ChatGPT Give Shorter Answers

One instruction, saved once, so it stops writing essays.

Start the guide ↓
Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen Free guide
Shift & Lead

ChatGPT's answers are way too long.

"Make it shorter" doesn't work because ChatGPT doesn't know what short means for you. Tell it the parts you need and a word limit, then check it didn't cut the truth.

What you leave with

a short-answer prompt you can reuse, plus a 4-point check.

How to use this guide: Read the example, try the prompt as it is, then swap in facts from your own work.

01

Say what "short" means Name the parts. Set a limit.

Instead of

"Make this shorter."

Try

"Write a meeting update in 3 bullets, under 60 words."

Name the parts (for example Status, Waiting on, Next action) and the limit. That's usually all it takes.

02

Short is only useful if it's still true Check what it cut.

Your facts

The onboarding checklist draft is ready. Team leads haven't reviewed it. No publication date has been agreed.

Too confident

"The checklist is ready to publish soon."

It added an approval and a timeline you don't have.
Short and accurate

"The draft is ready. Team lead review and a publication date are still open."

The full promptCopy the prompt ↓Write a short update using only these facts: [paste your facts] Give me exactly 3 bullets headed Status, Waiting on and Next action. Keep it to 60 words or fewer. No introduction
03

The honest part Check before you use it.

When AI shortens, it tends to round things up: "under review" becomes "approved". The shorter the answer, the more carefully you check each word.

✓

Your prompts Ready to copy.

Write a short update using only these facts:
[paste your facts]

Give me exactly 3 bullets headed Status, Waiting on and Next action. Keep it to 60 words or fewer. No introduction or closing offer. Don't say anything is approved or decided unless my facts say so. If the next action isn't clear, say what still needs to be agreed.

The 4-point check

  1. Can you find all 3 parts?
  2. Is it 60 words or fewer?
  3. Does every statement match your facts?
  4. Did you delete any intro, repetition or invented detail?
