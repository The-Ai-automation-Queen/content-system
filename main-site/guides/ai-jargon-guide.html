What Do AI Words Like "Agent" and "Hallucination" Mean?

6 words in plain English, and what each one means for your business.

Start the guide ↓
Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen Free guide
Shift & Lead

People keep saying 'hallucination', 'context window', 'agent'. I nod along. What do they actually mean?

You'll understand AI words faster by seeing them happen in a chat. Three short experiments show you six words, and what each one changes about how you work.

What you leave with

3 experiments, 6 words you can explain to a colleague, and the one habit each word should give you.

How to use this guide: Open any free AI chat. Run each experiment exactly as written, then read what happened. The words make sense after you've seen them.

01

Experiment 1 Hallucination and sources.

What you do:
ask the AI about a book that was never written.
What it shows:
a hallucination is the AI inventing something and saying it confidently. Grounding means tying an answer to a real source, which is how you catch it.
What you ask

Summarise the novel The Glass Orchard by Mara Quell.

→
What you might see

A confident plot summary (a hallucination), or "I can't find this book" (good). Then ask for a source link and watch what happens.

The habit: for any fact you'll repeat, ask where it came from.

02

Experiment 2 Context window and memory.

What you do:
tell the AI something in one chat, then ask about it in a new one.
What it shows:
the context window is everything the AI can see in this conversation. Memory is a separate setting that saves some details between chats, if it's switched on.
Chat 1

My project is called Bluebird and the deadline is Friday.

→
Chat 2 (new)

"What's my project called?" Usually: it doesn't know. If it does, memory is on.

The habit: put the important details in every new chat, or in a Project, instead of hoping it remembers.

Experiment 2 promptsCopy the prompt ↓Remember this: my project is called Bluebird and the deadline is Friday.
03

Experiment 3 Agent and connector.

What you do:
compare a request for an answer with a request for an action.
What it shows:
a chatbot answers. An agent takes steps on its own, like clicking, searching or sending. A connector is the link that lets AI into your email, files or calendar.
An answer

Write a reply declining the meeting.

→
An action

"Decline the meeting in my calendar and email the organiser." That needs a connector and permission, and it happens without you pressing Send.

The habit: before you connect anything, ask "what can it do without me?"

04

The honest part Check before you use it.

Knowing the words won't make an answer right. What they give you is the right question to ask: where did this come from, what can it see, and what can it do without me?
