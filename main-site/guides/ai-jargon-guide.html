10 AI words you need to know

What each word means, with simple examples that make it easy to remember.

People use AI words without explaining them. Here are 10 words you will hear often, what they mean and why they matter.

Term 1

LLM

Large Language Model

An LLM is the part of ChatGPT or Claude that reads your question and writes an answer.

Picture it

Think about autocomplete on your phone. It guesses the next word while you type. An LLM does something similar, but on a much larger scale.

Why it matters

An LLM can write a useful answer, but it does not know facts the way a person does. It can sound sure and still be wrong.

Term 2

Prompt

A prompt is what you ask or tell an AI to do.

Picture it

“Write something about marketing” is a prompt. “Write a short email inviting local shop owners to a free workshop” is a clearer prompt.

Why it matters

Clear instructions usually lead to a more useful answer. If your request is vague, the answer will often be vague too.

Term 3

Tokens

Tokens are the small pieces of text an AI reads and writes.

Picture it

A short word may be 1 token. A long word may be split into several tokens. Your question, your files and the answer all use them.

Why it matters

AI tools use tokens to measure limits and cost. Longer chats and larger files use more tokens.

Term 4

Context window

The context window is how much information an AI can use at the same time.

Picture it

Picture a desk. The AI can work with everything on the desk. When the desk gets too crowded, important details can get buried.

Why it matters

A very long chat can become less useful. Starting a fresh chat or removing old information can help.

Term 5

Hallucination

A hallucination is an answer that sounds real but contains made-up information.

Picture it

The AI may give you a book title, date or website that does not exist. It may still write the answer with complete confidence.

Why it matters

Check important names, dates, numbers and sources before you use them.

Term 6

Machine learning

Machine learning is a way for computers to learn from examples.

Picture it

Show a system thousands of emails marked “spam” or “not spam.” It starts to notice what spam emails have in common.

Why it matters

The computer is not given a rule for every situation. It learns patterns from the examples it receives.

Term 7

MCP and connectors

Model Context Protocol

Connectors let an AI reach information or tools outside the chat. MCP is a common way to make those connections.

Picture it

A connector can let an AI read your calendar, search a folder or create a task in another app.

Why it matters

A connected AI can do more, but it may also see or change more. Check what access you are giving it.

Term 8

Generative AI

Generative AI creates new content from your instructions.

Picture it

It can write an email, make an image, create music or produce a video.

Why it matters

This is the type of AI behind most chatbots and image tools people use today.

Term 9

AGI

Artificial General Intelligence

AGI is the idea of an AI that could learn and complete almost any thinking task a person can do.

Picture it

Today, different AI tools are good at different jobs. AGI would be able to move between many kinds of work without needing a new system for each job.

Why it matters

AGI does not exist today. When people talk about super-smart AI taking over, this is often what they mean.

Term 10

Vibe coding

Vibe coding means building software by telling an AI what you want instead of writing all the code yourself.

Picture it

You might ask for a simple booking app. The AI writes the code, shows you a version and changes it when you give feedback.

Why it matters

More people can now build small tools. You still need to test the result because working code is not always safe or ready to publish.

5 more words you will hear next

Agent

An AI system that can choose and complete several steps toward a goal.

RAG

A way for AI to search selected documents before it answers.

Fine-tuning

Extra training that helps a model follow a certain style or type of task.

Multimodal

AI that can work with more than text, such as images, audio or video.

Open-source model

A model people can download and run under the rules of its licence.
