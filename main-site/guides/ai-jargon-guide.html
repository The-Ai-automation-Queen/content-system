10 AI words you need to know

Understand the 10 terms that help you question an AI tool, follow a proposal and spot when someone is overselling it.

Use this guide when an AI tool, proposal or meeting uses a term nobody stops to explain.

01

Start with what AI is

These 3 terms explain what the technology does and what powers the tools you use.

01

AI

Artificial Intelligence

Computer software that can do tasks that normally need human judgment, such as recognising a photo, writing a summary or recommending what to do next.

AI is the broad category. ChatGPT, image generators and recommendation systems are different kinds of AI tools.

Remember: AI is software, not a person. A human is still responsible for the decision and the result.

02

Generative AI

AI that creates something new from your instructions, including text, images, audio, video or code.

When ChatGPT drafts an email or an image tool makes a poster, you are using generative AI.

03

LLM

Large Language Model

The part inside tools such as ChatGPT and Claude that reads your words and writes a response.

The LLM is the engine. The chat screen, saved files and buttons are the tool built around it.

Remember: A fluent answer is not proof that the answer is correct.

02

Know what happens in the chat

These 3 terms help you ask for better work and check the answer before you use it.

04

Prompt

The question, instruction or information you give an AI.

“Summarise this document in 5 bullets for a client” is a prompt. A clearer prompt usually produces a more useful first answer.

05

Context window

The amount of information an AI can consider at one time, including your request, earlier messages and attached files.

In a very long chat, the AI may lose track of an instruction from the beginning because too much information is competing for space.

06

Hallucination

Information an AI makes up but presents as if it were true.

A fake source, a wrong date or a product feature that does not exist can all be hallucinations.

Remember: Check names, dates, numbers, quotes and sources before you send, publish or act on the answer.

03

Understand how AI does work

These 4 terms explain how AI completes steps, uses your information and connects to other tools.

07

AI agent

An AI tool that works through several steps to complete a goal, instead of giving you only 1 answer.

An email agent could read new messages, identify the urgent ones, draft replies and add follow-up dates to your calendar.

Remember: Check which actions require your approval before an agent can send, delete, buy or publish anything.

08

Automation and workflow

A workflow is the order of steps in a job. Automation lets software complete some of those steps without you repeating them by hand.

A form can save a new contact, send the promised guide and start an email sequence automatically.

Remember: Keep human approval where a mistake could affect money, privacy, customers or your reputation.

09

Connector and MCP

Model Context Protocol

A connector gives an AI access to another tool, such as your email, calendar or files. MCP is 1 standard that tools can use to make those connections.

A calendar connector could let an AI check your availability and create an event instead of only telling you how to do it.

Remember: Approve only the access needed for the job. A connector may allow the AI to see or change private information.

10

RAG

Retrieval-Augmented Generation

A way for AI to search information you choose before it answers, instead of relying only on what it already knows.

If you connect your company handbook, RAG can find the relevant policy and use it to answer an employee's question.

Remember: The answer can still be wrong if the source is missing, outdated or misunderstood. Ask to see the source it used.
