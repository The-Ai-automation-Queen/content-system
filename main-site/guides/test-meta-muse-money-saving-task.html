Introduction

Any AI · 20 min read · Beginner

Yes. AI is good at spotting repeat payments in a list, working out the true yearly cost of a renewal, and drafting a firm, polite message. You keep the bank details private and make every decision.

A guide by Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

What you leave with: a subscription finder, a renewal cost check, a cancel-or-negotiate message, and a list of what never to paste.

01

How to use this guideIn 30 seconds.

Before you paste anything, remove account numbers, card numbers, your address and anything that identifies you. Keep only date, name of payee and amount.

02

Job 1Find the subscriptions you forgot.

The job:
three months of payments, and the small monthly charges hiding in them.
Why it works:
spotting the same payee every month is tedious for you and quick for AI.
What you paste (cleaned)

03/07 StreamBox 9.99 · 05/07 CloudDrive 2.99 · 03/08 StreamBox 9.99 · 12/08 FitApp 14.99 · 03/09 StreamBox 9.99 · 12/09 FitApp 14.99

→
What a good answer looks like

StreamBox, monthly, 9.99 · FitApp, monthly, 14.99 · CloudDrive, once so far, check it. Then a yearly total for each.

03

Job 2The real cost of a renewal.

The job:
a renewal letter for broadband, insurance or a phone plan.
Why it works:
the headline price often hides the end of an intro deal or extra fees. Adding up 12 months shows the real number.
The renewal

20 a month for the first 3 months, then 32 a month. Setup fee 25.

→
What a good answer looks like

3 × 20 + 9 × 32 + 25 = 373 for the year. Plus the questions to ask before you accept.

The renewal cost promptCopy the prompt ↓Work out the total cost of this deal over 12 months, including every fee mentioned. Show the sum step by step. Then list up to 5 questions I should ask before accepting, like what
04

Job 3The message you've been putting off.

The job:
the cancellation or "can you do better?" email.
Why it works:
AI writes a clear, polite, firm message in seconds. You decide what you're asking for.
Your facts

Customer for 3 years · the new price is 32 · you've seen 25 elsewhere · you'll leave if they can't match.

→
What a good message looks like

Under 100 words, your facts only, one clear ask, a date you need a reply by.

The cancel-or-negotiate promptCopy the prompt ↓Write a short email to [company] asking them to [lower my price / cancel my plan]. My facts: [bullet points] Polite and firm, under 100 words, one clear ask, and ask for a reply
05

The honest partCheck before you use it.

AI can add up wrong and can't see offers you haven't told it about. Check the maths once with a calculator, and confirm any price on the company's own page before you act. Never paste full bank statements, card numbers or login details.

✦Your turn

Unlock the 3 prompts.

Your prompts ready to copy.

Job 1: The subscription finder prompt

Here are my payments from the last 3 months (date · payee · amount). I've removed my account details.

Find anything that looks like a repeat payment. For each: payee · how often · amount · yearly total.

List one-off payments separately. If you're not sure something repeats, mark it "Check". Don't guess what the payee is.

Payments: [paste]

Job 2: The renewal cost prompt

Work out the total cost of this deal over 12 months, including every fee mentioned. Show the sum step by step.

Then list up to 5 questions I should ask before accepting, like what happens after the first year or if there's an exit fee.

Use only what's in the text. Deal: [paste the renewal wording]

Job 3: The cancel-or-negotiate prompt

Write a short email to [company] asking them to [lower my price / cancel my plan].

My facts: [bullet points]

Polite and firm, under 100 words, one clear ask, and ask for a reply by [date]. Use only my facts. Draft only, I'll send it myself.

Never paste: card or account numbers · passwords or security answers · full statements with your address.
