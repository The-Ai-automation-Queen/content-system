Can Mistral research in two languages without losing the source?

Ask one question in two languages, keep every claim tied to its original source and compare what each language reveals.

Translation can make two sources look more similar than they are. Keep the original wording, a short translation and the direct link beside every important claim.

Choose a fair research question

Start with a harmless topic covered by official sources in both languages.

  1. Name both languages

    Specify the language of your question and the second language to search.

  2. Set the date range

    Use a clear period so older sources do not appear current.

  3. Prefer original sources

    Ask for government, company, research or first-hand documents before summaries.

  4. Keep differences visible

    Do not merge claims when dates, definitions or local rules differ.

Check every useful claim

KeepCheck

Original title and language

The source is actually written in the named language.

Direct link and date

The page opens and the date supports the claim.

Original extract

The words appear in the source and are not taken out of context.

English translation

The translation preserves uncertainty, conditions and exceptions.
