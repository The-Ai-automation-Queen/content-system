Introduction

Copilot in Excel · 15 min read · Beginner

Give Copilot one small job at a time, on a copy of the file, and check each job the way it can go wrong: a tidy-up can lose rows, a formula can miss cells, a summary can invent a trend.

A guide by Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

What you leave with: 3 jobs worth handing to Copilot, a one-minute check for each, and a prompt that makes Copilot tell you exactly what it touched.

01

How to use this guideIn 30 seconds.

Make a copy of a real sheet, or use the example below. Never try a new job on the only copy of a file people rely on.

Job 1Tidy a messy column.

The job:
names in different formats, dates typed three ways, extra spaces.
Why it works:
tidying is slow and boring for people. The risk is lost or merged rows, so you count before and after.
The messy column

Jo SMITH · Smith, Amir · priya patel · LEE, SAM

→
What a good result looks like

Jo Smith · Amir Smith · Priya Patel · Sam Lee. Still 4 rows, in the same order, next to the same data.

Job 2Add a formula column.

The job:
a new column that works something out, like days overdue or price with tax.
Why it works:
Copilot writes the formula. You test it on one row you can work out in your head.
The request

What checking looks like

Pick one invoice due 10 days ago and still unpaid. The new column should say 10. Paid invoices should say 0 or stay blank.

Job 3Explain what the sheet shows.

The job:
a quick read of the numbers before a meeting.
Why it works:
a summary sounds convincing even when it's wrong. Asking for the cells behind each point lets you check in seconds.
Copilot says

Sales grew every month this quarter.

→
What checking finds

The cells it names show April 12, May 15, June 11. June went down. The sentence was wrong; the numbers were right there.

05

The honest partCheck before you use it.

Copilot can change more than you asked for, and a confident summary can be wrong. Work on a copy, one job at a time, and don't share the file until your one-minute check is done.

✦Your turn

Unlock the 3 prompts.

Your prompts ready to copy.

Job 1: The tidy-up prompt

In column [letter], make every name "First Last" with capital first letters, and remove extra spaces.

Change only column [letter]. Don't sort, delete or merge any rows.

When you're done, tell me how many rows there were before and after.

Job 2: The formula prompt

Add a new column called "[name]" that shows [what to work out].

Explain the formula in one plain-English sentence. Then tell me which row I should check by hand, and what the answer should be in that row.

Don't change any existing column.

Job 3: The explain-with-cells prompt

Give me 3 things this sheet shows, in plain English.

For each one, name the exact cells or range you used. If the data doesn't clearly show something, say so rather than guess a trend.

The one-minute check: row count the same · one row checked by hand · every claim matches the cells it names.
