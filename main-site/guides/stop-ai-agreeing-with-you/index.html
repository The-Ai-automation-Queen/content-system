How to Get ChatGPT to Tell Me What's Wrong With My Idea

Make ChatGPT show you the problems first.

Start the guide ↓
Fatiha Chikh Free guide
Shift & Lead

Get honest feedback first

ChatGPT tends to praise whatever you show it. This guide makes it show you the problems first. It takes 30 minutes.

What you end up with

  • One rule saved, so ChatGPT starts with the problems
  • The 5 biggest problems in your offer
  • The risks in your next plan, with warning signs
  • A saved honest critic for every new offer

You need a ChatGPT account. The free plan works. Copy each prompt as it is and change the [brackets].

01

See it agree Two questions.

AI learns to give answers people like. So it praises what you show it.

  1. Open ChatGPT ↗ and click New chat.
  2. Type: "Here is my offer: [one line]. Is it good?"
  3. Click New chat again.
  4. Type: "Here is my offer: [one line]. What would make a client say no?"

Compare the two answers. The second one is the one you need.

02

Save the honest rule Paste once.

Save one rule in ChatGPT. Every new chat then puts problems first.

  1. Click your name or picture, then Settings, then Personalization.
  2. Open Custom instructions.
  3. Paste the text below at the end of the box, then click Save.
When I share an idea, offer, page or plan:
Start with the problems, most serious first. Then what works.
Don't praise it unless you can say exactly why it works.
If you disagree with me, say so plainly.
Tell me what my client would doubt.
If you're not sure, say so. Never make up facts, figures or examples.

Open a new chat and ask: "I'm thinking of raising my prices. Good idea?" It should start with questions or risks.

Can't find it? OpenAI moves this menu. Search "custom instructions" on OpenAI's help pages ↗.

03

Get the problems first Read it as your client.

Ask ChatGPT to read your offer as your buyer would.

Read this as my ideal client: [describe them, for example: a coach who tried two programmes and was let down].

My offer, page or email:
[paste it, with client names swapped for brackets]

Give me:
1. PROBLEMS: the 5 biggest, most serious first. Quote the exact line for each.
2. QUESTIONS: what this client would still ask before buying.
3. WHAT WORKS: up to 3 things, and why.

Use only what I give you. If you need details, write [CHECK].
Never make up facts about my business or my clients.
  1. Copy 4 or 5 words of each quoted line.
  2. Press Ctrl + F (Mac: Cmd + F) in your offer and paste them.
  3. Nothing lights up? It made the line up. Cross that problem off.
  4. Save the rest in a note called Weak spots. Fix the ones you agree with.

The honest part: ChatGPT has never met your clients. Its list is questions to check, not answers. What clients say decides.

04

Find what could go wrong Before you spend money.

Use this before a launch, a new offer or a new price.

Imagine it is [3 or 6] months from now and this plan has failed.

The plan:
[what, when, the price range, who it is for]

What I know so far:
[what worked before, what clients said, my time and money]

Give me:
1. The 5 most likely reasons it failed. For each, one early warning sign.
2. The one guess that breaks everything if it is wrong.
3. The cheapest way to test that guess in 2 weeks.

Use only what I give you. Never make up figures, results or client stories.
  1. Put each warning sign in your calendar.
  2. Run the cheapest test first.
  3. Only then spend more time or money.
05

Save your honest critic Both checks in one place.

Save both checks in a ChatGPT Project (a folder that keeps its own rules).

  1. In the sidebar, click New project. Name it Honest critic.
  2. Open the project's Instructions.
  3. Paste the text below, fill in the brackets, then click Save.
You are my honest critic for [business name]. I sell [what you sell] to [who your clients are].

When I paste an offer, page, email or plan:
1. Read it as my ideal client: [describe them].
2. List the 5 biggest problems, most serious first. Quote the exact line for each.
3. List the questions the client would still have.
4. Imagine it failed in 3 months. Give 3 likely reasons and a warning sign for each.
5. Then up to 3 things that work.

Quote only lines that are really in what I paste.
Use only what I give you. If something is missing, write [CHECK].
Never make up facts, figures, names or client stories.
  1. Pick the 2 or 3 weak spots that worry you most.
  2. Ask two past clients: "When you first saw this offer, what made you unsure?"
  3. Add what they say to the critic's instructions.

Can't find it? Some plans don't have Projects. Search "projects" on OpenAI's help pages ↗, or keep the text in your notes.
