What Can I Use AI For in My Business?

A list of the jobs AI can do for you, and the 3 to try first. Then you turn it into a menu for your own business.

Start the guide ↓
Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen Free guide
Shift & Lead

Your AI job menu, built from your own week

Most lists of AI uses are written for someone else's business. This guide starts with 12 jobs AI can do for a small business that sells its expertise, then turns that list into your own job menu: the jobs in your week, ranked, each with a prompt you can reuse.

What you end up with

  • A clear picture of the 12 jobs AI can help with, and what stays with you in each one
  • Your AI job menu: the jobs in your business AI can help with, ranked, with your top 5 marked
  • A ready prompt for each of your top 5 jobs, written for your business
  • Job number 1 done on real work, and checked by you
  • Your menu saved in your notes or in a project, with one new job added each week

You need a ChatGPT or Claude account. The free plans work. Every prompt below can be copied and pasted as it is; replace the parts in [square brackets].

02

Build your job menu Your business, your tasks.

The table above is general. This prompt interviews you about your own week and turns it into a job menu that fits your business.

  1. Open a new chat in ChatGPT or Claude.
  2. Paste the prompt below and send it.
  3. Answer each question in a sentence or two. Use [client] instead of real names.
I run a small business. I want a list of the jobs in my week that AI could help with.

Here is a list of jobs AI can help a small business with:
Admin: routine email replies, call notes into actions, long documents
Clients: follow-up after a call, welcoming a new client, sorting feedback
Sales: replying to enquiries, first draft of a proposal
Content: posts from rough ideas, reusing what I made
Planning: my weekly plan, launch checklist

Interview me about my business. Ask me one question at a time and wait for each answer, up to 8 questions, about:
- what I sell and who my clients are
- the tasks I repeat every week, and how long each one takes
- the tasks I put off or dread
- which of the jobs above I do, and any jobs I do that are not on the list

Then give me my AI job menu as a table with these columns:
Job | What I paste in | What AI gives back | What I check before using it | Hours per week now | Risk if AI gets it wrong (low, medium, high)

Rank the table: most hours saved and lowest risk first. Mark the top 5 with a star.

Rules: use only what I tell you. Write [CHECK] where information is missing instead of guessing. Never invent names, prices, dates or promises. Plain English, no em dashes.

Read the table. If a job is missing, tell it: "Add [job]. I do it [how often]." If a risk looks too low to you, change it. You know your clients, it doesn't.

03

Write a prompt per job One for each of your top 5.

A job on a list saves you nothing until you have a prompt ready for it. This step writes one reusable prompt for each of your top 5 jobs.

  1. Stay in the same chat, so it can see your job menu.
  2. Paste the prompt below and send it.
Take the 5 starred jobs from my AI job menu.

For each one, write a reusable prompt I can paste into a new chat every time the job comes round. Each prompt must:
1. Start with one line saying what the job is
2. Say what I will paste in, as [square brackets], for example [paste the call notes]
3. Say exactly what to give back: format, length and tone
4. End with these rules: use only what I paste; write [CHECK] where information is missing instead of guessing; never invent names, prices, dates or promises

Keep each prompt under 800 characters.
Give me the 5 prompts under headings: Job 1, Job 2, Job 3, Job 4, Job 5, each with the job name.

Read each prompt. If it asks for something you never have to hand, cross that line out. If it misses something you always include, add it.

04

Try job 1 on real work Then check it.

One real job, done and checked, tells you more than any list. Use this week's material, not an invented example.

  1. Open a new chat.
  2. Paste the prompt for Job 1.
  3. Replace the [square brackets] with this week's real material. Swap names for [client], prices for [price] and dates for [date].
  4. Send it.

Before you use what comes back, check these 4 things:

  1. Names, numbers and dates. Every one must be in what you pasted. If not, fix it or delete it.
  2. Promises. Delete anything you didn't agree to.
  3. [CHECK] marks. Fill in every one yourself.
  4. Your voice. Would you send this with your name on it?

If one part is wrong, tell it what to change in the same chat:

Change this part: [paste the line].
Why it's wrong: [what's actually true].
Keep everything else the same.

The same fix twice? Add it to that job's prompt as a new rule, so you never correct it again.

05

Save your menu Add one job a week.

Your job menu only works if you can find it next week. Save it in one place with its prompts.

  1. If you see Projects in the left sidebar (in ChatGPT or Claude): click New project, name it AI job menu, and add the menu table and the 5 prompts to the project's instructions or files. Start every job chat inside the project.
  2. If you don't: copy the menu table and the 5 prompts into one note in your notes app, called AI job menu. Paste the right prompt into a new chat each time.
  3. Put a reminder in your calendar for the same time each week: "Add one job."
  4. Each week, take the next job on the menu, write its prompt with step 03, and try it with step 04.

Menus change. If you can't find Projects, search "projects" on OpenAI's help pages ↗ or Claude's help centre ↗. A note works just as well.

The honest part: AI won't know which jobs matter most to your clients, and it won't notice when a draft is wrong. It prepares the work. You check it, decide and send it.
