# The 4-Line Prompt That Works in Any AI

**Reader's question:** "I never know what to type into AI."

**One-line answer:** You don't need clever wording. Tell the AI the job, what it can use, what you want back and what to check. That's the whole method.

Beginner · 5 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a 4-line prompt you can reuse for any task, a worked example, and one line that makes the AI ask you questions before it guesses.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Read the 4 lines, look at the before-and-after, then try the full prompt with the example note. Swap in your own task once you've seen a good answer.

### 02 · The 4 lines *(Job · material · result · check.)*
1. **The job:** what you want done. *Draft an email, compare 2 options, summarise a report.*
2. **What it can use:** the notes, facts or document it should work from.
3. **What you want back:** the format and length. *3 bullets, a table, under 100 words.*
4. **What to check:** what must stay accurate and what it must not invent.

### 03 · See the difference *(The missing piece is information.)*
Paper cards:
- **Too vague:** "Make an agenda."
- **Useful:** "Turn this project note into an agenda for a 15-minute team meeting. Give 3 items and flag the pricing decision. Don't invent owners or approvals."

▒ locked ▒ *The full 4-line prompt*

### 04 · Let the AI ask first *(Questions before guesses.)*
You won't always know what the AI needs. Add one line asking it to check for missing details and ask you up to 3 questions before it answers. The gaps become visible instead of being filled with guesses.

### 05 · The honest part
A good prompt makes a good answer more likely, not certain. Always compare the answer with what you gave it: names, dates and anything that sounds decided.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *2 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**The 4-line prompt (with the example filled in)**
```
THE JOB
Turn this rough project note into an agenda for a 15-minute team meeting.

WHAT YOU CAN USE
We are preparing a website launch. The landing page is approved. The designer expects final graphics by Friday. Pricing still needs approval. The team meets on Tuesday. We have not decided if the launch should wait for pricing approval.

WHAT I NEED BACK
Three agenda items. For each, the update or decision needed and one useful question. End with one line headed "Decision to make". Under 130 words.

WHAT TO CHECK
Use only the note above. Don't invent people, owners, dates or approvals. If an owner is needed but not named, write "Not specified". Show the pricing approval as an open decision.
```

**The "ask me first" line (add it to any prompt)**
```
Before you answer, check whether you have enough information. If something important is missing, ask me up to 3 specific questions and stop. Otherwise, answer and mark anything you can't confirm as "Not specified".
```

**Check before you use it:** it followed the format · every fact is in your note · nothing undecided became decided.

# Teach Claude a Job Once, Reuse It Every Week

**Reader's question:** "I write the same weekly update every Friday and start from scratch every time."

**One-line answer:** Write the job down once (what goes in, the steps, what comes out, where Claude must stop), test it on two real weeks, then save it.

Intermediate · 20 minutes · Claude · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a tested weekly-update instruction, the 4-part template to build your own, and a 2-week test that proves it works.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Run the example with last week's notes (remove anything confidential). Fix what goes wrong, test with a second week, and only then save it.

### 02 · Describe the job in 4 parts *(Input · steps · output · stop.)*
- **Input:** what you give it each time (notes, a file, fields).
- **Steps:** what Claude does, in order.
- **Output:** the exact headings or table you need.
- **Stop rule:** what Claude must leave to you instead of guessing or acting.

### 03 · Test before you save *(Two weeks, not one.)*
1. Run the full instruction in a new chat.
2. Every time you fix something, turn the fix into a clearer rule.
3. Run it again with a different week's notes.
4. Save the version that works in a Claude Project's instructions. [VERIFY current menu names]

### 04 · The honest part
A saved instruction doesn't mean Claude remembers last week. Each run only knows what you paste in. That's a good thing: it can't carry old facts into this week's update.

# Turn a Screen Recording Into a Step-by-Step Guide

**Reader's question:** "Nobody writes down how we do things, and training new people takes forever."

**One-line answer:** Record one task, upload it to ChatGPT, and ask for only the steps it can actually see. Then have someone new follow them.

Intermediate · 20 minutes · ChatGPT · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a prompt that turns a recording into a checked process guide, a recording checklist, and a fallback when video upload isn't available.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Practise with a short task in a demo account first. No names, customer data or passwords on screen.

### 02 · Record something ChatGPT can use *(One task, start to finish.)*
1. Start on the screen where the task begins. Say why you make each choice. Show the finished result.
2. Use demo data or remove names, emails and notifications before uploading.
3. In ChatGPT, use **+ → Add photos & files** and choose the video. [VERIFY] If it isn't accepted, try **Files**.
4. No video upload on your account? Use a corrected transcript and a few clean screenshots instead.

### 03 · What a usable guide contains *(Four parts.)*
- **Before you begin:** what must already be open or approved.
- **Numbered steps:** one action each, with the real button names.
- **You should see:** the result after each step.
- **Needs clarification:** a flag where ChatGPT couldn't see or hear something, instead of a guess.

ChatGPT can miss a click or mishear the audio. Treat the recording as evidence and the draft as a first version. The real test is someone new following it.

# 4 Limits to Set Before an AI Agent Works Alone

**Reader's question:** "Is it actually safe to let AI do tasks on its own?"

**One-line answer:** An agent only has the power you connect. Give it one job, the smallest access, a clear approval point and a way to stop.

Beginner · 10 minutes · Any AI agent · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** the difference between a chat, an automation and an agent, the 4 limits, and a prompt that checks an agent setup before you switch it on.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Read the three types, then apply the 4 limits to one real task you'd like to hand off.

### 02 · Chat, automation or agent? *(Three different jobs.)*
Paper cards:
- **Chat:** you ask, it answers, it waits. You stay in charge of every step.
- **Automation:** the steps are fixed in advance and happen the same way each time.
- **Agent:** you give it a job and tools; it decides the next permitted step as it goes.

### 03 · The 4 limits *(Before you switch it on.)*
1. **One exact job.** Not "help with customer service" but "sort new requests by topic and draft a reply".
2. **The smallest access.** Only the files, inbox or apps the job needs.
3. **An approval point.** Anything touching money, customers, access or privacy waits for a person. Restrict the permission; a polite "please ask first" is not enough.
4. **A way to stop.** It pauses when information is missing, and you can see and undo what it changed.

### 04 · Start one step before automatic *(Prepare · propose · act.)*
Let it **prepare** first (research, sort, draft). Then **propose** (it waits for your OK). Only let it **act** alone once you've tested it and know how to reverse it.

▒ locked ▒ *The agent setup check prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
More access doesn't make an agent smarter. It just gives its mistakes more places to go.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *prompt.*

### After sign-up
**The agent setup check (new prompt, based on the existing guide's steps)**
```
I want to hand this task to an AI agent: [describe the task without private details].

It would have access to: [list the tools, files or apps].
It would be allowed to: [read / draft / update / send / delete / spend].

Check my setup against 4 limits and tell me for each: OK, too broad, or missing.
1. Is the job exact enough to test?
2. Is the access the smallest it needs?
3. Which actions should wait for my approval, and is that enforced by a permission, not just an instruction?
4. When should it stop, and how would I see and undo what it changed?
Then suggest the safest first version: prepare only, or propose and wait for approval.
```
**Before you switch it on:** you can name its one job · you know what it can change · you know how to stop it and undo.
