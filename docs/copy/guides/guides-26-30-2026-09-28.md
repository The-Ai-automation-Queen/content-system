# Stop AI Agreeing With Everything You Say

**Reader's question:** "Why does AI always agree with me?"

**One-line answer:** AI tools are built to be helpful, and agreeing feels helpful. Ask for problems before praise, give it a job that includes disagreeing, and set that rule once in your settings.

Beginner · 10 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a feedback prompt that ranks problems first, a plan stress test, and a one-paragraph "no flattery" rule for your settings.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Take something you wrote or planned this week. Run job 1 on it, then job 2. Set up job 3 once, and every chat after that starts honest.

### 02 · Job 1: Feedback that starts with the problems *(Praise comes last.)*
**The job:** real feedback on a draft: an email, a proposal, a post.
**Why it works:** when you ask "is this good?", the easy answer is yes. Asking for the 3 biggest problems first, ranked, gives it a job where agreeing doesn't count.
Paper cards:
- **Your draft:** a 200-word proposal to a new client.
- **What a good answer looks like:** "1. The price appears before the benefit. 2. Two paragraphs say the same thing. 3. No clear next step." Each with the line it's about. One strength at the end, not the start.
▒ locked ▒ *The problems-first prompt*

### 03 · Job 2: Stress-test a plan *(What would make it fail?)*
**The job:** a plan you're about to commit time or money to.
**Why it works:** imagining the plan has already failed makes it easier to name the reasons. You get risks you can act on instead of encouragement.
Paper cards:
- **The plan:** "Run a paid workshop in 6 weeks, promoted only on Instagram."
- **What a good answer looks like:** the 4 most likely reasons it didn't work (too little time to fill seats, one channel only, no price tested, no reminder emails), and one early sign for each that you'd see by week 2.
▒ locked ▒ *The stress-test prompt*

### 04 · Job 3: Switch off the flattery for good *(One paragraph, in your settings.)*
**The job:** stopping every future chat from opening with "Great question!"
**Why it works:** most AI tools let you save standing instructions. Put your honesty rule there once, instead of typing it every time.
Paper cards:
- **Where it goes:** ChatGPT custom instructions, Claude's personal preferences, Gemini saved info.
- **What changes:** answers start with the point, disagree when you're wrong, and say "I'm not sure" instead of guessing.
▒ locked ▒ *The no-flattery rule*

### 05 · The honest part
An honest AI is still not a person who knows your clients, your team or your history. Use it to find the weak spots, then decide with people who do.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**Job 1 · The problems-first prompt**
```
Give me feedback on this [email / proposal / post] for [who will read it].

Start with the 3 biggest problems, most serious first. For each one, quote the line and say why it's a problem for this reader.

Then give me one thing that works. Don't rewrite it, and don't soften the problems.

Draft: [paste]
```
**Job 2 · The stress-test prompt**
```
Here's my plan: [describe it in 3 to 5 lines].

Imagine it's [3 months] from now and the plan didn't work. Give me the 4 most likely reasons, most likely first.

For each reason, tell me the early warning sign I'd notice in the first [2 weeks], and one change I could make now to prevent it.
```
**Job 3 · The no-flattery rule** (paste into your AI's settings)
```
Be direct with me. Skip compliments and start with the answer.

If I'm wrong, or my plan has a weak spot, say so plainly and explain why. If you're not sure about something, say "I'm not sure" rather than guessing.

When I ask for feedback, give me the problems first.
```
**Before you act on the feedback:** do the problems match what you know about the reader? Would a trusted colleague agree?

# Catch AI Making Things Up in 30 Seconds

**Reader's question:** "Can I trust what AI tells me?"

**One-line answer:** Trust it like a fast new colleague: useful, but check before it goes out under your name. Three 10-second checks catch most mistakes before anyone else sees them.

Beginner · 10 minutes · Any AI chat · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** 3 checks you can run on any answer, a prompt for each, and a rule for when to check by hand.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Take an AI answer you were about to use today. Run the three checks on it, in order. Each one takes about 10 seconds.

### 02 · Check 1: Names, numbers and dates *(Where mistakes hide first.)*
**The check:** pull out every specific detail and mark where it came from.
**Why it works:** a made-up detail looks exactly like a real one. Listing them separately makes each one easy to check.
Paper cards:
- **The AI answer:** "The event is on 14 March at the Riverside Hall, and 120 people have signed up."
- **What a good check looks like:** 14 March · from your notes · 120 people · not in anything you gave it. That second one is your problem.
▒ locked ▒ *The details check prompt*

### 03 · Check 2: Open the source *(A link is not proof.)*
**The check:** click the link and find the sentence that supports the claim.
**Why it works:** AI can give a real link that says something different, or a link that doesn't exist. Only opening it tells you.
Paper cards:
- **The claim:** "Most small businesses now use AI for marketing," with a link.
- **What checking finds:** the page exists, but it's about large companies in one country. The claim goes beyond the source, so it stays out or gets rewritten.
▒ locked ▒ *The source check prompt*

### 04 · Check 3: Ask where it's least sure *(It will often tell you.)*
**The check:** ask the AI to mark its own weak spots.
**Why it works:** asked directly, AI tools will often flag which parts are guesses. It doesn't catch everything, but it tells you where to look first.
Paper cards:
- **Your question:** "Which parts of your last answer are you least sure about?"
- **What a good answer looks like:** "The date of the rule change and the second statistic. I'd check both." Now you know what to check by hand.
▒ locked ▒ *The confidence check prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
None of these checks makes an answer true. For anything legal, medical, financial or going to a client, check the original source yourself, every time.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 checks.*

### After sign-up
**Check 1 · The details check prompt**
```
List every name, number, date and place in your last answer.

For each one, tell me where it came from: something I gave you, a source you can link to, or your general knowledge. If you can't say, write "Unsure".
```
**Check 2 · The source check prompt**
```
For each claim in your last answer that has a source, give me the exact sentence from that source that supports it.

If the source only partly supports the claim, say so. If you can't find the sentence, tell me to remove the claim.
```
**Check 3 · The confidence check prompt**
```
Which parts of your last answer are you least sure about? List up to 3, and say what I should check to confirm each one.
```
**The by-hand rule:** if a mistake would cost money, trust or a relationship, check the original yourself. The AI's own check is not enough.

# Make a Quit-or-Stay Decision With 5 AI Advisors

**Reader's question:** "Should I quit my job? Can AI help me think it through?"

**One-line answer:** AI can't decide for you, and it tends to agree with however you ask. It can help if you describe both options equally, ask five advisors with different jobs to weigh in, and turn the result into a small test.

Beginner · 20 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a fair way to describe your decision, a 5-advisor prompt, and a 30-day test that gives you real evidence before you decide.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Do job 1 on paper first. Leave out your employer's name and anything confidential. Then run jobs 2 and 3 in the same chat.

### 02 · Job 1: Describe both options fairly *(Your wording leaks your answer.)*
**The job:** writing the decision down so it doesn't lean one way.
**Why it works:** "I'm miserable and thinking of leaving" already contains the answer. Two equal descriptions, with the good and bad of each, give the AI nothing to agree with.
Paper cards:
- **Leaning:** "I hate my job and want to go freelance. Should I?"
- **Fair:** "Option A: stay. Steady salary, good team, no growth for 2 years. Option B: freelance. More control, 4 months of savings, two possible first clients."
▒ locked ▒ *The fair description prompt*

### 03 · Job 2: Hear 5 advisors *(Each one has a different job.)*
**The job:** looking at the decision from five angles you'd normally mix together.
**Why it works:** one answer blends hope, fear and money into one paragraph. Five advisors keep them apart, and one of them is required to argue against you.
Paper cards:
- **The 5 advisors:** The Bank Balance (runway and the worst month) · The Tuesday Test (what an ordinary day looks like in each option) · The Hiring Manager (how each choice looks on your CV in 3 years) · The Person Who Knows You (what you've said you want before) · The Contrarian (the best case for the option you're leaning against).
- **What a good answer looks like:** five short, different views, then a summary of where they agree, where they clash, and what you'd need to know to decide.
▒ locked ▒ *The 5-advisor prompt*

### 04 · Job 3: Test before you decide *(30 days, real evidence.)*
**The job:** turning the advice into something you can try without quitting yet.
**Why it works:** a small test gives you facts instead of feelings: can you win a client, do you enjoy the work, does the money add up?
Paper cards:
- **A test:** land one paid freelance project on evenings and weekends in the next 30 days.
- **What a good test looks like:** one clear goal, a date, and the result that would change your mind agreed in advance ("no paid project in 30 days means I stay and try again in the spring").
▒ locked ▒ *The 30-day test prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
This is your life and your money. Use the advisors to see the decision clearly, then talk it through with someone who knows you, and with a qualified adviser for anything about tax, contracts or benefits.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**Job 1 · The fair description prompt**
```
I'm deciding between two options. Help me describe them fairly before we discuss anything.

Option A: [describe]
Option B: [describe]

Rewrite both in the same format: what I gain · what I give up · what I don't know yet. Remove any wording that makes one sound better. Don't give me your opinion yet.
```
**Job 2 · The 5-advisor prompt**
```
Now look at my decision as 5 different advisors. Keep each view under 80 words.

1. The Bank Balance: my money, my runway and the worst month I could face.
2. The Tuesday Test: what an ordinary day would look like in each option.
3. The Hiring Manager: how each choice would look on my CV in 3 years.
4. The Person Who Knows Me: what I've told you matters to me.
5. The Contrarian: the strongest case for the option I seem to be leaning against.

Then summarise: where they agree, where they clash, and the 2 facts I'd need to decide.
```
**Job 3 · The 30-day test prompt**
```
Based on this, design a 30-day test I can run without quitting.

Give me: one clear goal · the steps, week by week · how much time it needs each week · the result that would tell me to stay, and the result that would tell me to go.

Keep it realistic for someone with a full-time job.
```
**Before you decide:** both options described fairly · the Contrarian taken seriously · a real test result, not just a feeling.

# Check How Exposed Your Job Is to AI in 5 Minutes

**Reader's question:** "Is AI coming for my job?"

**One-line answer:** AI changes tasks, not whole jobs, at least at first. List what you actually do each week, sort each task honestly, and put your energy into the parts that need you.

Beginner · 15 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a list of your real tasks, an honest sort of what AI can and can't take on, and a 90-day plan for the one skill that matters most.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Look at last week's calendar and sent emails before you start. Describe your work in general terms, with no company or client names.

### 02 · Job 1: List what you really do *(Tasks, not your job title.)*
**The job:** turning "I'm a marketing coordinator" into the 10 tasks that fill your week.
**Why it works:** a job title tells AI nothing. Tasks, with the hours each one takes, show where change would actually land.
Paper cards:
- **The title:** "Office manager."
- **The real week:** supplier emails (5 hrs) · booking rooms and travel (3 hrs) · onboarding new starters (4 hrs) · handling complaints (2 hrs) · monthly budget report (3 hrs).
▒ locked ▒ *The task list prompt*

### 03 · Job 2: Sort each task honestly *(Draft · help · human.)*
**The job:** placing each task in one of three groups, with a reason.
**Why it works:** three plain groups are more useful than a single risk score. They show what to hand over, what to speed up and what to protect.
Paper cards:
- **AI can draft it:** supplier emails, the first version of the budget report.
- **AI can help, you lead:** onboarding plans, travel bookings.
- **Stays with you:** handling complaints, knowing which supplier to call when it goes wrong.
▒ locked ▒ *The task sort prompt*

### 04 · Job 3: Pick one skill for the next 90 days *(Build on the human part.)*
**The job:** choosing where to put your learning time.
**Why it works:** one skill, practised on real work, beats a pile of courses. Build it where the "stays with you" tasks and your AI use meet.
Paper cards:
- **The skill:** "Using AI to draft the budget report, then being the person who explains it to leadership."
- **What a good plan looks like:** one skill, 3 monthly steps, and one piece of proof to show your manager at the end.
▒ locked ▒ *The 90-day skill prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
Nobody can tell you exactly how your job will change, and any tool that gives you a precise score is guessing. What you can control is how well you use AI on your own work, and how clearly you can show the value of the parts only you do.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**Job 1 · The task list prompt**
```
Help me list the tasks in my job. My role: [general job title and sector, no company names].

Ask me up to 5 questions about my typical week, one at a time. Then list my 8 to 10 main tasks, with roughly how many hours a week each one takes.
```
**Job 2 · The task sort prompt**
```
Sort each task into one of 3 groups:
1. AI can draft it (I check it).
2. AI can help, but I lead.
3. Stays with me.

For each task, give a one-line reason. Be realistic about what today's AI tools can do. If it depends on my company's rules or tools, say so. Don't give me a risk score.
```
**Job 3 · The 90-day skill prompt**
```
Looking at my "stays with me" tasks and the tasks AI can help with, suggest the one skill that would make me most valuable in my role.

Give me a 90-day plan: one step per month, using my real work, not a course. End with one piece of proof I could show my manager.
```
**Before you trust the sort:** would your manager agree with the "stays with me" list? Anything you're unsure about, test with AI on a real task this week.

## Guide 30 · `/guides/test-business-idea-with-ai/`

**Search wording:** How to test a business idea with AI
**Kit:** AI for small business owners (coming soon)
**Hero:** "Put your business idea *in front of 5 AI critics.*"
**Hero line:** Write the idea down in one paragraph, let five critics find the holes, and run a one-week test before you spend anything.

# Put Your Business Idea in Front of 5 AI Critics

**Reader's question:** "Is my business idea any good?"

**One-line answer:** Asking AI "is this a good idea?" gets you encouragement. Ask five critics with different jobs to find the problems, then let real people answer the only question that counts: will they pay?

Beginner · 20 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a one-paragraph idea brief, a 5-critic prompt, and a one-week demand test with the pass mark set in advance.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Write your idea in one paragraph before you open any AI. If you can't, that's the first thing to fix.

### 02 · Job 1: The one-paragraph brief *(Who, what, why now, how much.)*
**The job:** putting the idea into a form a critic can judge.
**Why it works:** a vague idea gets vague feedback. Four clear lines give the critics something to push against.
Paper cards:
- **Vague:** "Something to help older people with technology."
- **Clear:** "A one-hour home visit that sets up a new smartphone for someone over 70: contacts, photos, video calls. For their grown-up children who live too far away to help. Because the phone was a gift and it's still in the box. A fixed price per visit."
▒ locked ▒ *The idea brief prompt*

### 03 · Job 2: Five critics *(Each one looks for a different hole.)*
**The job:** finding the weak spots while changing the idea is still free.
**Why it works:** each critic has one narrow job, so nobody can hide behind "overall it's promising".
Paper cards:
- **The 5 critics:** The Stranger (would someone who's never met you care?) · The Rival (what do people already use instead?) · The Accountant (does the price cover your time?) · Month-6 You (what will be hard once the excitement goes?) · The Sceptical Friend (the one question you're avoiding).
- **What a good answer looks like:** 2 sharp points from each critic, then the 3 problems that matter most, ranked.
▒ locked ▒ *The 5-critic prompt*

### 04 · Job 3: A one-week demand test *(Real people, no spending.)*
**The job:** finding out whether anyone will say yes, before you build anything.
**Why it works:** AI can only guess what customers will do. A simple offer to real people gives you an answer.
Paper cards:
- **The test:** message 20 people with a parent over 70, describe the offer in 3 lines, and ask if they'd book a visit.
- **Pass mark, set before you start:** 3 people say yes and agree a date. Fewer than 3: change the idea, not the pass mark.
▒ locked ▒ *The demand test prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
AI critics have never run your business, met your customers or seen your market up close. Use them to find questions, then answer those questions with real people.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**Job 1 · The idea brief prompt**
```
Help me turn my business idea into a one-paragraph brief with 4 parts:
Who it's for · What they get · Why they need it now · Roughly what it costs them.

Ask me questions one at a time until each part is specific. Don't tell me whether the idea is good.

My idea: [describe it in your own words]
```
**Job 2 · The 5-critic prompt**
```
Review my business idea as 5 critics. Give 2 sharp points from each. No encouragement.

1. The Stranger: would someone who's never met me care?
2. The Rival: what do people already use or do instead?
3. The Accountant: does the price cover my time and costs?
4. Month-6 Me: what will be hard once the excitement wears off?
5. The Sceptical Friend: what's the question I'm avoiding?

Then rank the 3 problems that matter most.

My brief: [paste]
```
**Job 3 · The demand test prompt**
```
Design a one-week test of my idea that costs nothing and involves real people.

Give me: who to contact and how many · a 3-line message describing the offer · the question to ask · a pass mark I should set before I start.

Don't suggest building a website, an app or anything else first.
```
**Before you build anything:** the brief is clear · the top 3 problems have an answer · real people said yes.
