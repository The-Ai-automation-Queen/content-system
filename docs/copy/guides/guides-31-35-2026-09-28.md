# Weekend Projects: 7 Useful Things Claude Can Build

**Reader's question:** "What can I actually build with Claude on a free weekend?"

**One-line answer:** Claude can build small interactive tools, with buttons and lists you can click, straight from a description. Start with three for your home, then try the four after sign-up.

### 02 · Build 1: The family chore rota *(Fair, and it rotates.)*
**The build:** a weekly chore chart that rotates jobs between people.
**Why it's a good first build:** simple lists and buttons, and you'll know straight away if it works.
Paper cards:
- **You describe:** 4 people · 6 chores · jobs rotate each week · tick a job when it's done.
- **What a good first version looks like:** this week's chart with names and chores, a Done button on each, and a "Next week" button that moves everyone along one job.

### 03 · Build 2: The renewal calendar *(No more surprise charges.)*
**The build:** a list of subscriptions and renewals, sorted by date, with the next 30 days highlighted.
**Why it's useful:** the insurance, the domain, the streaming service. All in one place, soonest first.
Paper cards:
- **You add:** name · cost · how often · next renewal date.
- **What a good first version looks like:** a sorted list, anything renewing within 30 days in a different colour, and a yearly total at the top.
▒ locked ▒ *The renewal calendar prompt*

### 04 · Build 3: The gift and birthday planner *(Ideas saved when you have them.)*
**The build:** a list of people, dates and gift ideas you jot down through the year.
**Why it's useful:** the best gift ideas come in March, and the birthday is in November.
Paper cards:
- **You add:** name · birthday · budget · ideas as they come.
- **What a good first version looks like:** the next 3 birthdays at the top, a place to add ideas under each person, and a "bought" tick.
▒ locked ▒ *The gift planner prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
These are handy personal tools, not secure apps. Don't put bank details, passwords, health information or anyone's address into them, and check whether your tool keeps its data before you rely on it.

# Build a Money Plan From Your Payslip With AI

**Reader's question:** "Where is my money going, and can AI help me plan?"

**One-line answer:** AI is good at explaining, adding up and organising. Give it your take-home pay and your regular costs (never your account details), and it can help you build a simple plan and check it each month. The decisions stay yours.

Beginner · 20 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a payslip explainer, a one-page monthly plan, and a 10-minute monthly check-in.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Have your latest payslip and a list of your regular monthly costs ready. Type the numbers in yourself. Don't upload the payslip: it carries your name, employer and ID numbers.

### 02 · Job 1: Understand your payslip *(Line by line.)*
**The job:** knowing what each deduction is and why your take-home is what it is.
**Why it works:** payslips use codes and short names. AI can explain each one in plain words, and tell you what to ask your payroll team.
Paper cards:
- **What you type:** "Gross pay 3,000 · Tax 400 · National Insurance 200 · Pension 150 · Net pay 2,250."
- **What a good answer looks like:** one plain line per deduction, a check that the numbers add up, and "ask payroll" next to anything that looks unusual.
▒ locked ▒ *The payslip explainer prompt*

### 03 · Job 2: A one-page monthly plan *(Needs · goals · everyday.)*
**The job:** deciding in advance where your take-home pay goes.
**Why it works:** three simple pots are easier to stick to than twenty categories. AI does the maths; you choose the numbers.
Paper cards:
- **What you give it:** take-home 2,250 · rent 900 · bills 250 · travel 120 · goal: an emergency fund.
- **What a good plan looks like:** Needs 1,270 · Goals 400 · Everyday spending 580, about 135 a week. One line on the emergency fund: how long to reach 3 months of needs at 400 a month.
▒ locked ▒ *The monthly plan prompt*

### 04 · Job 3: The monthly check-in *(10 minutes, same day each month.)*
**The job:** comparing what you planned with what happened.
**Why it works:** a plan you never look at again is a wish. One short check each month shows what to adjust.
Paper cards:
- **What you type:** planned vs actual for each pot.
- **What a good check-in looks like:** where you were over or under, the one change for next month, and no judgement.
▒ locked ▒ *The check-in prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
AI isn't a financial adviser and can get sums wrong. Check the totals once with a calculator. For pensions, debt, tax or investing decisions, speak to a qualified adviser or a free, official money guidance service in your country.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**Job 1 · The payslip explainer prompt**
```
Explain my payslip line by line in plain English. I've typed the numbers in myself, with no names or ID numbers. I live in [country].

[Gross pay, each deduction and net pay]

For each deduction, say what it is in one line. Check that the numbers add up. If anything looks unusual, tell me what to ask my payroll team. Don't give me tax advice.
```
**Job 2 · The monthly plan prompt**
```
Help me build a simple monthly plan.

Take-home pay: [amount]
Regular costs: [list with amounts]
My goal: [for example an emergency fund]

Split my pay into 3 pots: Needs · Goals · Everyday spending. Show the sums. Then tell me how many months it would take to reach [my goal amount] at this rate.

Use only my numbers. Ask me if anything is missing.
```
**Job 3 · The check-in prompt**
```
Here's my plan and what actually happened this month.

Planned: [pot amounts]
Actual: [pot amounts]

Show where I was over or under. Suggest one change for next month. Keep it short and practical.
```
**Never type in:** account or card numbers · your employee or tax ID · login details.

**Hero line:** Work out what they'll really test, practise one question at a time, and walk in with five stories ready.

# Rehearse Any Job Interview With AI

**Reader's question:** "I have an interview next week and I'm nervous."

**One-line answer:** Nerves drop when you've practised out loud. AI can read the job ad for what they'll test, act as your interviewer one question at a time, and help you prepare stories that answer most questions.

Beginner · 30 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (voice mode works well) · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a job-ad decoder, a one-question-at-a-time mock interview, and a bank of 5 stories you can adapt on the day.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Have the job ad open. Do job 1 today, job 3 tomorrow, and job 2 (the mock interview) twice before the day, out loud if you can.

### 02 · Job 1: Decode the job ad *(What they'll really test.)*
**The job:** turning a long job ad into the few things the interview will focus on.
**Why it works:** most ads list 15 things but care about 4 or 5. Knowing which ones tells you what to prepare.
Paper cards:
- **The ad says:** "Fast-paced environment · manage multiple stakeholders · excellent written communication · experience with CRM systems · proactive."
- **What a good decode looks like:** "They'll test: juggling competing requests, writing clearly to senior people, and using a CRM. Expect a question about a time priorities clashed."
▒ locked ▒ *The job ad decoder prompt*

### 03 · Job 2: The mock interview *(One question at a time.)*
**The job:** practising answers with feedback before it counts.
**Why it works:** one question, your answer, specific feedback, then the next. It feels like the real thing and you improve as you go.
Paper cards:
- **The question:** "Tell me about a time you had to say no to a senior colleague."
- **What good feedback looks like:** "Clear situation, but you didn't say what you did or how it ended. Add the outcome in one sentence." Then the next question.
▒ locked ▒ *The mock interview prompt*

### 04 · Job 3: Your 5-story bank *(Prepared once, used all day.)*
**The job:** preparing a handful of real stories that answer most behavioural questions.
**Why it works:** five well-told stories can cover dozens of questions. You pick the story; AI helps you tell it tightly.
Paper cards:
- **Your 5 stories:** a problem you solved · a conflict you handled · a mistake you learned from · a time you led · a result you're proud of.
- **What a good story looks like:** under 90 seconds out loud, in four parts: what was happening, what you did, what changed, what you learned.
▒ locked ▒ *The story bank prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
Practise with AI, but don't memorise its words. Interviewers notice scripted answers. Use your own stories, told in your own way, and check any fact about the company on its own website.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**Job 1 · The job ad decoder prompt**
```
Here's a job ad. Tell me the 4 or 5 things this interview will most likely test, in plain words.

For each one, give me a likely interview question and what a strong answer would need to show.

Use only the ad. Don't guess facts about the company.

Job ad: [paste]
```
**Job 2 · The mock interview prompt**
```
Act as the interviewer for this role: [job title]. Ask me the questions from the list we made, one by one, and wait for my answer each time.

After each answer, give me 2 lines of feedback: what worked and the one thing to improve. Then ask the next question.

After 5 questions, give me my strongest answer and the one to practise again.
```
**Job 3 · The story bank prompt**
```
Help me build 5 interview stories: a problem I solved, a conflict I handled, a mistake I learned from, a time I led, and a result I'm proud of.

For each one, ask me questions until you have the details. Then write it in 4 short parts: situation · what I did · what changed · what I learned. Under 150 words each, in my words, not yours.
```
**The morning of:** read your 5 stories once · check the company website for anything new · 3 questions ready to ask them.

## Guide 34 · `/guides/launch-your-product-in-30-days/`

**Kit:** AI for small business owners (coming soon)
**Hero:** "The 30-day checklist that gets your product *out the door.*"
**Hero line:** Cut the product to its smallest useful version, plan 4 weeks backwards from launch day, and check every Friday what's blocking you.

# The 30-Day Checklist That Gets Your Product Out the Door

**Reader's question:** "I keep polishing and never launch."

**One-line answer:** Most products don't launch because they keep growing. Decide what the first version doesn't include, plan backwards from a fixed date, and let AI do the planning and the Friday check so you can spend your time making.

Beginner · 15 minutes to set up · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** a "smallest version" prompt, a 4-week plan built backwards from launch day, and a Friday check that keeps it moving.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Pick your launch date before you start and write it down. It doesn't move. What goes into the first version can.

### 02 · Job 1: Cut to the smallest useful version *(What's out matters most.)*
**The job:** deciding what version 1 is, and what waits for version 2.
**Why it works:** a "not in version 1" list is the fastest way to stop polishing. AI is good at spotting what a first customer actually needs.
Paper cards:
- **The full idea:** an online course with 12 modules, a community, worksheets, a certificate and bonus live calls.
- **A good version 1:** 3 modules that get one clear result, plus one live call. Out for now: the community, the certificate, 9 modules.
▒ locked ▒ *The smallest version prompt*

### 03 · Job 2: Plan backwards from launch day *(4 weeks, 4 milestones.)*
**The job:** a week-by-week plan that ends on your fixed date.
**Why it works:** starting from the launch date shows what must be done by when. One milestone per week keeps it manageable.
Paper cards:
- **Launch date:** a Tuesday, 4 weeks from now.
- **A good plan:** Week 1 · content outlined and one module made. Week 2 · all 3 modules made. Week 3 · sales page, payment and emails set up. Week 4 · 10 people told, launch. About 45 minutes a day.
▒ locked ▒ *The backwards plan prompt*

### 04 · Job 3: The Friday check *(10 minutes, every week.)*
**The job:** a short weekly look at what's done, what's stuck and what to cut.
**Why it works:** when a week slips, the answer is to cut scope, not to move the date. AI can suggest what to drop.
Paper cards:
- **What you type:** "Planned: all 3 modules. Done: 2. Stuck: editing the videos takes too long."
- **What a good check looks like:** keep the date · record module 3 as a simple talking-head video · move editing polish to version 2.
▒ locked ▒ *The Friday check prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
AI can plan, but it can't make the thing or find your first customers. The plan only works if the date is real and the daily 45 minutes happen. If you miss a week, cut, don't delay.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**Job 1 · The smallest version prompt**
```
Here's my product idea in full: [describe everything you imagine it including].

Help me define version 1: the smallest thing a first customer would pay for that gets them one clear result.

Give me two lists: In version 1 · Not in version 1 (yet). For each item you cut, one line on why it can wait.
```
**Job 2 · The backwards plan prompt**
```
My launch date is [date], [4] weeks from now. I have about [45 minutes] a day.

Version 1 includes: [paste your list].

Plan backwards from the launch date: one milestone per week, and the daily tasks for each week. Keep every task under [45 minutes]. Include telling at least 10 people before launch day.
```
**Job 3 · The Friday check prompt**
```
It's Friday. Here's my week:
Planned: [what I planned]
Done: [what I did]
Stuck: [what's blocking me]

The launch date doesn't move. Tell me what to cut or simplify to stay on track, and give me next week's 3 priorities.
```
**On launch day:** it's live · 10 people know · your version 2 list is saved for later.

# 3 Email Rules That Make People Click Buy

**Reader's question:** "Why don't people buy from my emails?"

**One-line answer:** Most sales emails talk to everyone, hide what they're about and make big promises. Write to one real customer, say what's inside in the subject line, and show proof instead of promises. AI helps with drafts; you bring the customer and the proof.

Beginner · 15 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini · By Fatiha Chikh | The AI Automation Queen

**What you leave with:** 3 rules, a prompt for each, and a 4-point check to run before you press send.

### 01 · How to use this guide *(In 30 seconds.)*
Pick one product and one email you're about to send. Apply the three rules to that email, in order.

### 02 · Rule 1: Write to one person *(Not "everyone on my list".)*
**The rule:** picture one real customer and write to them.
**Why it works:** an email written to everyone sounds like an advert. One written to one person sounds like a note, and gets read.
Paper cards:
- **To everyone:** "Dear valued customers, we're excited to announce our new range of candles."
- **To one person:** "You told me the lavender candle was the only thing that helped you switch off after work. There's now a bigger one."
▒ locked ▒ *The one-reader prompt*

### 03 · Rule 2: Say what's inside *(The subject line is the promise.)*
**The rule:** the subject line tells the reader exactly what they'll get by opening.
**Why it works:** clever subject lines get skipped. Clear ones get opened by the people who actually want the thing.
Paper cards:
- **Clever:** "You won't believe this..."
- **Clear:** "The big lavender candle is back (40 hours)" · "New: the lavender candle, twice the size"
▒ locked ▒ *The subject line prompt*

### 04 · Rule 3: Proof, not promises *(Their words beat yours.)*
**The rule:** use a real customer's words, a real detail or a real result instead of adjectives.
**Why it works:** "the best candle ever" is a claim. "I light it every night at 9" from a real customer is proof.
Paper cards:
- **A promise:** "Our candles are amazing and long-lasting."
- **Proof:** one short real review, with permission · "burns for about 40 hours" (only if you've tested it) · a photo of it on a real customer's shelf.
▒ locked ▒ *The proof prompt*

### 05 · The honest part
AI can write a smooth email, but it can't invent your proof. Never let it make up reviews, results or numbers. If you don't have proof yet, ask your last five customers for a sentence before you send.

**Sign-up band:** Your turn · Unlock the *3 prompts.*

### After sign-up
**Rule 1 · The one-reader prompt**
```
Write a short sales email for [product] to one specific customer: [describe them: who they are, why they bought before, what they said].

Write as if to that one person. Under 120 words. One link, one clear reason to click. Use only the facts I've given you.
```
**Rule 2 · The subject line prompt**
```
Give me 5 subject lines for this email. Each one must say exactly what's inside. No teasers, no "you won't believe", no false urgency. Under 50 characters each.

Email: [paste]
```
**Rule 3 · The proof prompt**
```
Here's my email and the real proof I have: [paste reviews, tested details or results, with permission].

Replace any vague claims in the email with this proof. Don't invent any reviews, numbers or results. If a claim has no proof, mark it [NO PROOF] so I can cut it.

Email: [paste]
```
**Before you press send:** written to one person · the subject says what's inside · every claim has proof · one link, one ask.
