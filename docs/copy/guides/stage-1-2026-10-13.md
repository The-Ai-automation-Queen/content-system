# 3 jobs to hand AI *in your business today.*

**Hero line:** A client reply, a long contract and a pile of enquiries. One prompt for each.

### Everyone says AI will help my business. Where do I actually start?

Start with work you already do. You know what a good result looks like, so you can judge the AI's answer straight away. This guide gives you three everyday jobs, a worked example for each and the prompt to copy.

**What you leave with**
- 3 prompts for jobs you do every week
- A worked example for each, so you know what good looks like
- The 30-second check before anything reaches a client

> **How to use this guide:** do job 1 with the example first. Then try it on a real email of yours, with the client's name swapped for [client].

### 01 · Get set up *Two minutes.*

You need one free AI chat. Any of these works for this guide.

1. Open [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com), [Claude](https://claude.ai) or [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com) and sign in. A free account is enough. [VERIFY]
2. Start a new chat.
3. The box at the bottom of the screen is where you paste every prompt in this guide.

> **What AI is, in one line:** a tool that writes the most likely answer from what you give it and what it learned in training. That is why the material you give it matters, and why you check what comes back.

> **Before you paste:** swap client names, emails and prices for [brackets]. The guide *Is ChatGPT Safe for Business?* shows you how.

### 02 · The client reply *The email you keep putting off.*

An email with three questions in it sits in your inbox because answering properly takes thought. AI can lay out the reply. You bring the facts.

**The email (example):**
> Hi, I saw your workshop page. Is the date on the 21st confirmed, can I pay in two instalments, and do I get the slides afterwards? Thanks, Sam

**What a good reply looks like:**
> Three short answers in Sam's order. Only the facts you gave it. [CHECK] wherever you haven't decided yet. Under 100 words.

`▒ sign-up card ▒`

**Prompt: The client reply**
```
Help me reply to this email from a client.

What I know:
[your answers, as short bullet points]

Rules:
- Answer each question in the order they asked it.
- Use only my facts. Where I haven't given an answer, write [CHECK] instead of guessing.
- Friendly and clear, under 100 words.

The email:
[paste the email, with names swapped for [client]]
```

**Next:** read it once, replace every [CHECK] yourself, then send it from your own inbox.

### 03 · The long document *The 3 points that matter.*

A supplier contract, a terms page or a report you meant to read before a call. AI can pull out what matters and show you the exact sentence each point came from, so you can check it in seconds.

1. Copy the text of the document, or attach the file if your chat has a paperclip button. [VERIFY]
2. Paste the prompt below and fill in your situation.
3. Find each quoted sentence in the document before you rely on it.

**What you paste (example):** a 4-page agreement for your booking software.
**What a good answer looks like:** 3 points (notice period, price changes, who owns your client list), each with the sentence it came from, plus one line on what the agreement doesn't cover.

**Prompt: The 3 points**
```
Read this [contract / report / terms page] and give me the 3 points that matter most for [my situation, for example: a one-person business deciding whether to sign].

For each point:
- say it in one plain sentence
- quote the exact sentence it came from

Then add one line: what does this document not tell me that I should ask about?

Document:
[paste]
```

> **For anything legal or financial:** the quotes help you find the right clause fast. The decision, or a professional's advice, is still yours.

### 04 · Sort the pile *Enquiries, feedback, ideas.*

A list you need to make sense of: questions from a launch, answers from a client survey, ideas in your notes app. Grouping is slow by hand and fast for AI. You decide whether the groups are right.

**What you paste (example):**
> wants a payment plan · asks if beginners can join · price too high · when is the next date · can I get a refund if I miss it · do you do 1:1 · is there a recording · loved the free guide

**What a good answer looks like:**
> Price and payment (3) · Dates and access (2) · Who it's for (2) · Other (1). Every item in exactly one group.

**Prompt: Sort the pile**
```
Sort these items into 3 to 5 groups. Give each group a short name and count the items in it.

Put every item in exactly one group. If something doesn't fit, put it under "Other" instead of forcing it.

Then tell me which group I should deal with first, and why, in one sentence.

Items:
[paste, one per line]
```

**Next:** the biggest group tells you what to answer first: on your sales page, in an FAQ or in your next email.

### 05 · The honest part *Check before it goes out.*

> AI sounds just as sure when it's wrong. Before anything reaches a client, put the answer next to what you gave it and check three things: names, numbers and anything that now sounds decided.

- [ ] You sent one real reply, with every [CHECK] filled in by you
- [ ] You know the 3 points of a document you'd been avoiding
- [ ] One list is sorted, and you know which group comes first
- [ ] The three prompts are saved where you'll find them

## Guide 2 · `/guides/ai-jargon-guide/`

**Library title:** AI Terms Explained for Business Owners, in Plain English
**Browser title:** AI Terms Explained for Business Owners, in Plain English | Shift & Lead

**Info row:** Beginner · 10 minutes · Any free AI chat

# 6 AI terms *in plain English.*

**Hero line:** Six words you keep hearing, what each one means for your business, and a quick test to see it happen.

### People say hallucination, context window and agent. I nod along. What do they mean for my business?

Each term describes something you'll run into in your first week with AI. Here they are in plain words, with what each one changes about your work. Three short tests then let you see them happen in your own chat.

**What you leave with**
- A plain-English table of 6 terms
- 3 tests you can run in 2 minutes each
- The question each term should make you ask

> **How to use this guide:** read the table first. Run the tests when you have a chat open.

### 01 · The 6 terms *In plain English.*

| Term | What it means | What it means for your business |
|---|---|---|
| Hallucination | The AI makes something up and says it confidently. | A wrong price, date or quote in a client email. Check before you send. |
| Source | A real document or web page an answer is tied to, that you can open. | Ask "where did this come from?" for any fact you'll repeat. |
| Context window | Everything the AI can see in this conversation. | It can't see what you didn't give it. Paste the brief into the chat. |
| Memory | A setting that saves some details between chats, when it's switched on. | Don't rely on it for client details. Give them in each chat, or save them in a Project. |
| Agent | An AI that takes steps on its own: clicks, searches, sends. | Decide what it may do without you before you switch it on. |
| Connector | The link that lets AI into your email, files or calendar. | Give it the least access the job needs. |

### 02 · Test 1: Hallucination *Ask about a book that doesn't exist.*

You ask about a made-up business book, then ask for a source. You'll see whether the AI invents an answer or admits it can't find it.

**What you ask (example):**
> Summarise the business book "The Velvet Ledger" by Orla Penrith. [VERIFY: no such book found on 13/10/2026; re-check on build day]

**What you might see:**
> Either a confident summary of a book that doesn't exist (a hallucination), or "I can't find this book" (good). The source question shows you which one you got.

`▒ sign-up card ▒`

**Prompt 1: Ask about the book**
```
Summarise the business book "The Velvet Ledger" by Orla Penrith in 5 bullet points.
```

**Prompt 2: Ask for the source**
```
Give me a link to a real source that shows this book exists. If you can't, tell me which parts of your last answer you made up.
```

**What it means for you:** any name, number or quote you put in front of a client gets checked against a source you can open.

### 03 · Test 2: Context window and memory *Tell it once, then start a new chat.*

You give the AI a detail in one chat, then ask for it in a brand-new chat.

**Prompt 3: In your first chat**
```
Remember this for later: my client is called [Client A] and their project is due on [date].
```

**Prompt 4: In a brand-new chat**
```
What's my client called, and when is their project due? If you don't know, say so.
```

**What you might see:** usually it doesn't know, because a new chat can't see the old one. If it does know, memory is switched on. Check what it has saved in your settings. [VERIFY: memory settings path in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini]

**What it means for you:** keep a short client brief you paste into new chats, or save it once in a Project. See *How to Use ChatGPT Projects for Your Business*.

### 04 · Test 3: Agent and connector *An answer or an action?*

Asking for a draft gives you text to check. Asking for an action does something in your accounts.

| An answer | An action |
|---|---|
| "Write a reply turning down this client's meeting request." You read it, then you press Send. | "Decline the meeting in my calendar and email the client." That needs a connector and your permission, and it happens without you pressing Send. |

**Prompt 5: Answer or action**
```
Explain the difference between these two requests in 3 lines:
1. "Write a reply turning down this meeting request."
2. "Decline the meeting in my calendar and email the client."

Then tell me what access an AI would need for the second one, and two things that could go wrong.
```

**What it means for you:** before you connect anything, ask "what can it do without me?"

### 05 · The honest part *Words are a start.*

> Knowing the terms won't make an answer right. It gives you three questions to ask every time: where did this come from, what can it see, and what can it do without me?

- [ ] You caught a made-up answer, or saw the AI refuse to make one up
- [ ] You know whether memory is on in your chat
- [ ] You know which of your tools could act without you

## Guide 3 · `/guides/what-should-you-never-share-with-ai/`

**Library title:** Is ChatGPT Safe for Business? What Never to Paste In
**Browser title:** Is ChatGPT Safe for Business? What Never to Paste In | Shift & Lead
**Google description:** Is ChatGPT safe for business? A 3-question check before you paste, what to keep out, what needs a client's OK, and the privacy setting to change today.
**Info row:** Beginner · 10 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot

# What never to paste *into ChatGPT.*

**Hero line:** Three questions before you paste, a keep-out list for client data, and the setting to change today.

### I want to use AI with client work. What's actually safe to paste in?

Most of your work is fine to use with AI once the private details are out. A few things should never go in, and some need your client's OK first. This guide gives you a 3-question check, a sorted list and the privacy setting to change in each tool.

**What you leave with**
- The 3-question check before you paste
- A keep out / ask first / fine list with business examples
- The training setting to switch off in 4 tools
- A way to do the task without the real details

> **How to use this guide:** do steps 01 to 03 now. They take five minutes and protect every chat after them.

### 01 · The 3-question check *Before anything goes in.*

1. **Does the task need the real details?** A reply can be drafted with [client] instead of a name.
2. **Am I allowed to share it?** Check your client contract and any confidentiality agreement you signed.
3. **Is this account right for the job?** A personal free account and a business workspace can handle your data differently. [VERIFY]

If any answer is "no" or "not sure", swap the details for [brackets] or leave them out.

### 02 · Keep out · Ask first · Fine *Sort it before you paste.*

| Keep out | Ask first | Usually fine |
|---|---|---|
| Passwords and login codes | A client's emails and documents | Your public website and sales page |
| Card and bank details | Client names, project details and results | Your own notes, with names removed |
| Health or other sensitive personal information | Contracts and proposals | Invented examples |
| Anything covered by a confidentiality agreement | Details about your freelancers or team | Published articles and guides |

> **"Ask first"** means ask whoever owns the information. For client material, that's your client. This is a practical check, not legal advice.

### 03 · Switch off training *One setting, two minutes.*

Many AI tools can use your chats to improve their models unless you switch it off. Do this once in each tool you use. Menu names change; if yours looks different, open the official page.

| Tool | Where | Official page |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | **Profile → Settings → Data Controls**, then switch off **Improve the model for everyone**. Temporary Chat is another option for a one-off conversation. [VERIFY] | [OpenAI help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-chatgpt-data-controls-faq) |
| Claude | **Your name → Settings → Privacy**, then switch off **Help improve our AI models** (personal Free, Pro and Max accounts). [VERIFY] | [Claude privacy](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/12109829-how-do-i-change-my-model-improvement-privacy-settings) |
| Gemini | **Settings & help → Activity**, then under **Keep Activity** choose **Turn off**. Work accounts may be managed by an administrator. [VERIFY] | [Gemini help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13278892) |
| Copilot | Personal account: **Profile → your name → Privacy**, then switch off the training options. Microsoft 365 Copilot at work follows your organisation's settings. [VERIFY] | [Microsoft support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot/microsoft-copilot-privacy-controls) |

> **The honest part:** switching training off gives you more control over your chats. It doesn't give you permission to share a client's confidential work. That permission comes from the client.

`▒ sign-up card ▒`

### 04 · Swap the real details out *Same task, no private data.*

Most tasks work just as well with placeholders. Do the swap yourself, before anything goes into the AI.

1. Copy the text into a blank document, not into the AI.
2. Replace each name, email, price and date with a bracket: [client], [email], [price], [date].
3. Keep a note of what you replaced.
4. Paste the cleaned text into the AI and do the task.
5. Put the real details back into your final version.

**Before:**
> Hi Priya, following our call, the website package for Bloom Studio is £2,400, due on 3 March.

**After:**
> Hi [client], following our call, the website package for [business] is [price], due on [date].

### 05 · Check a task without sharing the data *Ask about the kinds of information.*

Not sure whether a task is safe? Describe the kinds of information involved, never the information itself.

**Prompt: The safe check**
```
I want to use AI for this task: [describe the task, with no names, files or private details].

It involves these kinds of information: [kinds only, for example: client emails, invoices, my public sales page].

Don't ask me to paste the real information. For each kind, tell me:
1. Keep it out, ask the owner first, or fine to use?
2. Why, in one sentence.
3. A safer stand-in I could use instead.

If you can't know whether I have permission, say who I should ask.
```

- [ ] Training is switched off in every AI tool you use
- [ ] You've sorted what you were about to paste into keep out, ask first or fine
- [ ] You have a [bracket] version of one real client email
- [ ] You know who to ask for each "ask first" item

## Guide 4 · `/guides/catch-ai-making-things-up/`

**Library title:** How to Fact-Check ChatGPT Before It Reaches a Client
**Browser title:** How to Fact-Check ChatGPT Before It Reaches a Client | Shift & Lead
**Google description:** How to fact-check ChatGPT before a client sees it: three quick checks for names, numbers, dates and sources, with a copy-ready prompt for each.
**Info row:** Beginner · 10 minutes · Any AI chat

# Fact-check ChatGPT *before your client does.*

**Hero line:** Three quick checks that catch made-up names, numbers and sources.

### Can I trust what AI writes for my business?

Treat it like a fast new assistant: useful, but nothing goes out under your name unchecked. Three short checks show you where a made-up detail is hiding, before a client finds it.

**What you leave with**
- 3 checks you can run on any AI answer
- A prompt for each check
- The cases where you always check by hand

> **How to use this guide:** use an AI answer you were about to send today. Run the checks in order.

### 01 · Get ready *One answer, one source.*

1. Keep the chat with the AI's answer open. The prompts below refer to "your last answer".
2. Have your own notes or the original document next to it.
3. Mark anything going to a client. That's what you check first.

### 02 · Check 1: Names, numbers and dates *Where did each one come from?*

A made-up detail looks exactly like a real one. Listing every detail on its own line makes each one quick to check.

**The AI's draft (example):**
> Your coaching package starts on 6 May, includes 8 sessions, and 40 clients have already completed it.

**What a good check finds:**
> 6 May: from your notes. 8 sessions: from your notes. 40 clients: not in anything you gave it. That third one comes out.

`▒ sign-up card ▒`

**Prompt: The details check**
```
List every name, number, date, price and place in your last answer.

For each one, say where it came from:
- something I gave you
- a source you can link to
- your general knowledge

If you can't say, write "Unsure". Don't change the answer yet.
```

**Next:** verify or delete every "Unsure" and every "general knowledge" detail before it reaches a client.

### 03 · Check 2: Open the source *Find the sentence.*

AI can give a real link that says something different, or a link that doesn't work. Only opening it tells you.

**The claim (example):**
> "Most clients prefer video calls to phone calls," with a link.

**What checking finds:**
> The page exists, but it's a survey of one industry in one country. The claim goes further than the source, so rewrite it or leave it out.

**Prompt: The source check**
```
For each claim in your last answer that has a source, quote the exact sentence from that source that supports it.

If the source only partly supports the claim, say so.
If you can't find a supporting sentence, tell me to remove the claim.
```

**Next:** open each link yourself and find that sentence. If it isn't there, the claim goes.

### 04 · Check 3: Ask where it's least sure *Let it point to the weak spots.*

Asked directly, AI will often say which parts are guesses. It won't catch everything, but it tells you where to look first.

**Prompt: The weak-spot check**
```
Which parts of your last answer are you least sure about? List up to 3. For each one, tell me what I should check to confirm it, and where.
```

**What a good answer looks like:**
> The renewal date and the price of the second plan. Check both on the supplier's pricing page.

### 05 · The honest part *Checks reduce risk.*

> No check makes an answer true. For anything legal, financial, medical or going to a client, check the original source yourself, every time.

### ✓ You're done when
- [ ] Every name, number and date has a source, or it's gone
- [ ] You opened every link and found the sentence
- [ ] You checked the AI's 3 weak spots by hand
- [ ] The version going to the client is one you'd sign

# The 4-line prompt *for any business task.*

**Hero line:** The job, what it can use, what you want back and what to check. That's the whole template.

### I never know what to type into ChatGPT for my business.

You don't need clever wording. A good prompt tells the AI four things: the job, what it can work from, what you want back and what it must not get wrong. Fill those in and you get an answer built on your business, ready to check.

**What you leave with**
- A fill-in 4-line template
- A filled-in example for a client proposal
- One line that makes the AI ask before it guesses

> **How to use this guide:** fill in the table below with a task from your week. Then copy the template and paste your answers in.

### 01 · The 4 lines *Fill in the blanks.*

| Line | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The job | What you want done | Draft a short proposal for a new client. |
| 2. What it can use | Your notes, facts or document | My call notes and my price. |
| 3. What you want back | Format and length | 3 short sections, under 200 words. |
| 4. What to check | What must stay accurate, what it must not invent | Use only my notes. Don't invent results or dates. |

### 02 · See the difference *Same task, more information.*

| Too vague | Useful |
|---|---|
| "Write a proposal." No material, no format, nothing to check. | "Using my call notes below, draft a 3-section proposal for a new client, under 200 words. Use only my price. Don't invent results or dates." |

The second version gives the AI what the first one leaves out: your material, the shape of the answer and the rules.

`▒ sign-up card ▒`

### 03 · The template *Copy it, fill it in.*

**Prompt: The 4-line template**
```
THE JOB
[what you want done]

WHAT YOU CAN USE
[paste your notes, facts or document]

WHAT I WANT BACK
[format and length, for example: 3 bullet points, under 100 words]

WHAT TO CHECK
Use only what I've given you. Don't invent [names, prices, dates, results]. If something is missing, write [CHECK] instead of guessing.
```

**Prompt: Filled in for a client proposal**
```
THE JOB
Draft a short proposal for a new client who wants help with their website copy.

WHAT YOU CAN USE
Call notes: the client runs a small yoga studio. They want their homepage and class pages rewritten before their summer timetable launches. They haven't decided whether they need blog posts too.
My price: [price] for the homepage and up to 5 pages.

WHAT I WANT BACK
Three short sections: what I understood, what I'll deliver, next step. Under 200 words. Warm and plain.

WHAT TO CHECK
Use only the notes and my price. Don't invent dates, results or extras. Show the blog posts as an open question.
```

**What a good answer looks like:** three sections, your price, the blog posts as a question, no promised results.

### 04 · Let it ask first *Questions before guesses.*

Sometimes you don't know what the AI needs. Add this line to the end of any prompt and it asks before it fills the gaps with guesses.

**Prompt: The ask-me-first line**
```
Before you answer, check whether you have enough information. If something important is missing, ask me up to 3 specific questions and wait. Otherwise, answer and mark anything you can't confirm with [CHECK].
```

### 05 · Save it *So you never start from blank.*

1. Save the blank template in your notes app.
2. For a job you repeat, keep a filled-in copy.
3. When you're ready, save it in a ChatGPT or Claude Project so you stop pasting it. See *How to Use ChatGPT Projects for Your Business*.

> **The honest part:** a good prompt makes a good answer more likely, not certain. Check every answer against what you gave it.

- [ ] You filled in the 4 lines for one real task
- [ ] The answer used only your material
- [ ] The template is saved where you'll find it

## Guide 6 · `/guides/stop-ai-agreeing-with-you/`

**Library title:** How to Get Honest Feedback From ChatGPT on Your Business
**Browser title:** How to Get Honest Feedback From ChatGPT on Your Business | Shift & Lead
**Google description:** Does ChatGPT agree with everything? Get problems-first feedback on your offer, stress-test a plan and save a no-flattery rule once in your settings.
**Info row:** Beginner · 10 minutes · ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini

# Get honest feedback *from ChatGPT.*

**Hero line:** Ask for problems before praise, then save the rule so every chat starts honest.

### Why does ChatGPT say every idea I have is great?

AI tools are built to be helpful, and agreeing with you feels helpful. You can change that. Give it a job where finding problems is the point, then save one honesty rule in your settings.

**What you leave with**
- A problems-first feedback prompt for your offer, page or email
- A stress test for any plan
- A no-flattery rule to save in your settings

> **How to use this guide:** take something you wrote or planned this week: an offer, a sales page, a launch plan. Use it all the way through.

### 01 · Why it agrees *And how to change it.*

"Is this good?" has an easy, pleasing answer: yes. Give the AI a job where agreeing doesn't count.

- Ask for problems before strengths.
- Say who the reader is, so it judges from their side.
- Ask it to imagine your plan has already failed.

### 02 · Feedback on your offer *Problems first.*

**What you paste (example):** the sales page for your 6-week group programme.

**What a good answer looks like:**
> 1. The price comes before the reader knows what they get. 2. The page says "transform" three times and never says what changes. 3. Nothing answers "is this for me?" Each problem quotes its line. One strength comes at the end.

`▒ sign-up card ▒`

**Prompt: Problems first**
```
Give me feedback on this [sales page / offer / email / proposal]. The reader is [who they are and what they want].

Start with the 3 biggest problems for this reader, most serious first. For each one, quote the line and say why it's a problem.

Then give me one thing that works. Don't rewrite it, and don't soften the problems.

Text:
[paste]
```

**Next:** fix the first problem, then run the prompt again on the new version.

### 03 · Stress-test a plan *Imagine it already failed.*

Before you put time or money into a plan, ask the AI to imagine it didn't work. Reasons for a failure are easier to name than risks in a plan that still looks good.

**The plan (example):**
> Launch a paid workshop in 4 weeks, promoted only on Instagram.

**What a good answer looks like:**
> Four likely reasons it didn't fill (a short run-up, one channel, an untested price, no reminder emails), and one early warning sign for each by week 2.

**Prompt: The stress test**
```
Here's my plan: [describe it in 3 to 5 lines].

Imagine it's [3 months] from now and the plan didn't work. Give me the 4 most likely reasons, most likely first.

For each reason, tell me:
- the early warning sign I'd see in the first [2 weeks]
- one change I could make now to prevent it

Don't reassure me. I want the risks.
```

### 04 · Make it stick *Save the rule once.*

Most AI tools let you save standing instructions that apply to every new chat. Paste the rule below there.

| Tool | Where |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT | **Settings → Personalization → Custom instructions** [VERIFY] |
| Claude | **Settings → Profile**, in the personal preferences box [VERIFY] |
| Gemini | **Settings → Saved info** [VERIFY] |

**Prompt: The no-flattery rule**
```
Be direct with me. Skip compliments and start with the answer.
If I'm wrong or my plan has a weak spot, say so plainly and explain why.
If you're not sure, say "I'm not sure" instead of guessing.
When I ask for feedback, give me the problems first.
```

**What changes:** answers start with the point, and it tells you when you're wrong.

> An honest AI still doesn't know your clients or your market. Use it to find the weak spots, then check them with real customers.

# 3 AI skills *every business owner needs.*

**Hero line:** Brief it, check it, and decide what stays yours.

### My AI answers are average. What am I doing wrong?

Before you switch tools, look at three skills that change every answer you get: how you brief the AI, how you check what comes back, and what you keep for yourself. Practise each one on a real task from your week.

**What you leave with**
- 3 skills, with a 10-minute exercise each
- A prompt for each exercise
- One task split into AI work and your work

> **How to use this guide:** pick one task you do every week (a post, a proposal, a client update) and use it for all three exercises.

### 01 · Pick your task *One real job.*

1. Choose a task you do every week.
2. Write one sentence on what a great result looks like.
3. Open any AI chat.

### 02 · Skill 1: Brief it *Let it interview you.*

Most weak answers come from thin requests. You don't need to write the perfect brief: let the AI ask what it needs.

**The thin request (example):**
> Write a LinkedIn post about my new service.

**What a good brief covers:**
> Who it's for · the problem it solves · one real example · the tone · the length · what not to say.

`▒ sign-up card ▒`

**Prompt: Interview me first**
```
I need [what you want, for example: a LinkedIn post about my new bookkeeping package].

Before you write anything, ask me up to 5 questions you need answered to do this well. Ask one at a time and wait for my answer. Then write it, using only what I told you.
```

### 03 · Skill 2: Check it *Wrong things that sound right.*

Anyone can get a draft from AI. Checking it properly is what keeps your name safe.

**The AI's summary (example):**
> Your client survey shows most clients want weekly calls.

**What checking finds:**
> The survey says 4 of 11 clients asked for weekly calls. That isn't most.

**Prompt: The claim check**
```
Check this answer against the source I've pasted.

For each claim, label it: supported (quote the source), not supported, or contradicted. Be strict: if the source doesn't say it, it's not supported.

Answer: [paste the AI's answer]
Source: [paste the original]
```

### 04 · Skill 3: Decide what stays yours *Draft or decide.*

Some parts of your work should stay with you: judgement, relationships and anything you're responsible for. Being clear about the split is what makes AI safe to use with clients.

**The task (example):**
> Reply to a client who's unhappy about a late delivery.

**A good split:**
> AI drafts the apology and the new timeline. You decide what you offer, whether to call instead, and the tone with this client.

**Prompt: The task split**
```
Here's a task from my business: [describe it].

Split it into two lists:
1. What AI could draft or prepare for me.
2. What I should decide or do myself, and why.

Flag any part where getting it wrong could cost me a client, money or trust.
```
