# Shift & Lead site · the honest review (29/09/2026)

Read as a senior web designer and a conversion copywriter, from the point of
view of a business owner who lands on the site cold. Every point is about what
that visitor thinks, not about taste.

---

## The verdict in five lines

1. **The site sells nothing yet, and it shows.** I count 11 "Coming soon"
   labels across Home and Work with me. A 20-year professional with nothing
   to buy looks like a start-up still testing ideas.
2. **Three promises, two audiences.**
   - The promises: "AI training & guidance" (Home), "From AI ideas to real
     work" (Work with me), and "Think you have nothing special?" (Workbooks).
   - The audiences: business owners, and individuals reflecting on their own
     life.
   - The visitor can't tell who the site is for.
3. **The mascot and the colour carousel make it look junior.** The first
   thing below the hero is four cartoon covers on four different colours.
   That reads as a children's app, not a consultant.
4. **Every section shouts.** Every heading is a two-part slogan, every section
   has its own background and a wave, and every card has a heavy offset
   shadow. When everything is loud, nothing feels premium.
5. **The same things are said three times.** The logos, the numbers, the kits,
   the workbooks and the offers all repeat across pages and even within one
   page.

**What professional looks like here:** one promise, one audience, one path,
and everything shown is real, few colours, calm type, real proof, and fewer
sections.

## 1 · Positioning and products

### One audience: business owners
The guides, kits and Work with me all speak to business owners. The workbooks
speak to individuals going through a personal transition. That audience is
valid, but it belongs to a different brand. This ties in with your domain
question: the workbooks fit a personal brand, and the rest fits AI Queen or
Shift & Lead.

**Recommendation:** take the workbooks out of the nav and off Home now. Keep
the page online, linked only from the footer, until you decide the brand split.

### One promise, everywhere
"From AI ideas to real work" is the strongest line you have: it's specific,
active, and about the result. "AI training & guidance" names a category, not a
result.

**Recommendation:** use one promise on every page.

Home. I recommend replacing it. Your call.]`

### A product ladder a buyer understands in 5 seconds

| Step | Product | Status | Why it's there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | **The guides** (42) | Live | Proof you know your stuff. It earns the email. |
| Do it yourself | **Kits**, one per topic | Launch **one** first | The first thing people can buy. One kit, clearly shown, beats three "coming soon" kits. |
| Done with you, in writing | **Your AI Plan** (today's "business and marketing transformation") | Waitlist, then open | A named product with a clear deliverable sells. A long category name doesn't. |
| For events and teams | **Speaking and team sessions** | **Available now** | You've already done this: stages, panels, podcasts. Today the page calls it "coming soon", which undersells real proof. |

**Park for now** (not on the site until each one is real):

  problems
- Workbooks, pending the brand split

Each one adds a "coming soon" and a decision for the visitor.

## 2 · The customer journey

A business owner should move through these steps without friction:

1. **In 5 seconds:** "This is for my business, and she's credible." (promise and logos)
2. **In 30 seconds:** "I can try her method now, free." (one guide)
3. **Then:** "I'll leave my email for more." (one sign-up)
4. **Later:** "I'll buy the kit, or ask for my plan, or book her to speak."

**Where the current site breaks this path:**
- **Home** jumps from free guides to kits (coming soon), then to workbooks (a
  different audience, also coming soon). Step 4 never feels reachable.
- **Work with me** gives three offers, a comparison table, then each offer
  again in full, then do-it-yourself products, then the FAQ "When does it
  open? All three are coming soon". The page ends by admitting nothing is
  available.
- **The sign-up form** has to explain the product names ("Kits = AI for your
  work. Workbooks = what only you bring.") in small print. When a form needs a
  legend, the product structure is too complicated.
- **The buttons** use 8 different verbs:
  - Free practical guides
  - Get the free guides
  - Find a free guide
  - Send me the guides
  - Start free with the guides
  - Hear first when it opens
  - See how we can work together
  - Work with me

  Two are enough: **Get the free guides** and **Work with me**.

## 4 · Visual style: from playful to premium

| Now | Why it reads amateur | Change to |
|---|---|---|
| A cartoon mascot on every guide cover, filling the Home carousel and the library | It sets a childish tone before anyone reads a word | Typographic book covers built in code: title, number and topic on a solid brand colour, like Saadia's book spines. No image generation needed. `[FATIHA: keep the mascot inside the guides only, or remove it from the covers too?]` |
| 5 to 6 background colours per page, with a wave between every section | A template look, and the eye never rests | Two backgrounds (white and ink) plus one blue band per page. One wave at the most, under the hero. |
| Heavy black offset shadows on every card | Playful, "neo-brutalist" | Flat cards with a thin border, or no card at all |
| Every card in a different colour (pink, blue, ink) | A toy-box rhythm | One card colour. Colour only for the one thing to click. |
| Every heading built the same way: Impact capitals plus a Playfair italic accent | A gimmick once repeated 8 times per page | Impact capitals for the page headline only. Section headings in plain sentence case. One italic accent per page, at the most. |
| Slogan headings ("THE FULL SETUP. One topic at a time.", "GOOD questions.", "RATHER DO IT yourself?") | Copywriting tricks that feel cute | Plain, specific headings that say what the section is ("The kits", "Speaking and team sessions", "Questions") |
| 13 to 14px grey text in cards | Hard to read, looks cheap | 17 to 18px body text everywhere |
| Eyebrow labels with a pink star (About, Workbooks, guides) | A common template signature | Removed, except the hero |
