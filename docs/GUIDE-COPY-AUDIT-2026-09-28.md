# Guide copy audit — Shift & Lead vs reference (28 September 2026)

Written from two roles: a guide auditor (would a reader sign up?) and an AI
partner marketer (would a tool vendor share this guide?). The topics are good.
The copy is the problem. Internal document.

Sample read in full: `/guides/claude/`, `/guides/which-ai-tool-for-what/`,
`/guides/what-should-you-never-share-with-ai/`. Reference: Saadia Karam's
"5 AI tools. One job each.", "The bedtime story" and the Project bank.

## Scorecard (1–5)

| Criterion | Shift & Lead | Reference | What makes the difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promise in the title | 2 | 5 | Hers is a statement of the result. Yours are questions ("What can you actually do with Claude at work?"). |
| Specific numbers and names | 2 | 5 | She gives "5 tools", "60 seconds", "10 pages", "€0", "45 languages", and names the model ("Claude Sonnet 5"). You give "one small task", "a useful result". |
| Author voice and opinion | 1 | 5 | She says "my 5 prompts" and "this is the right default". Your guides have no "I", no byline and no recommendation. |
| Visible value before the form | 2 | 5 | She shows all of module 1 (the job, why this tool, the setup) and blurs only the prompt. Your content hides behind choosers and "+" buttons. |
| Perceived value (feels like something to download) | 2 | 5 | Her guides have a book-cover mock-up, "the rest is yours", "free, no card". Yours read like help-centre pages. |
| Consistent skeleton | 2 | 5 | Every module of hers follows the same four parts: job, why, setup, prompt. Yours change shape from guide to guide. |
| Pull of the email gate | 2 | 4 | Yours: 3 fields, "a link back to this guide". Hers: one account, "the rest is yours", free, and you can see the blurred prompt. |
| Would a tool vendor share it? | 1 | 4 | Yours is cautious about each tool; hers explains why each tool is the right choice. |

## The seven problems, with evidence

1. **Questions instead of promises.** "Which AI tool should you try first?" and
   "What can you actually do with Claude at work?" make the reader do the work.
   The reference title "5 AI tools. One job each." answers before you read.
2. **The payoff is undersold.** "You will finish one small task in Claude."
   "Try one small task." A small promise gets a small click.
3. **Caution leads, value follows.** Your judgement theme has become the
   headline: "Compare Claude's answer with the original facts before using it",
   "non-confidential", "Before you paste it into AI, check this". The reference
   handles caution in one honest line ("Two honest things...") after it has
   delivered the value.
4. **No author.** There is no "I", no "my prompt", no "I use this every week",
   and no name or date. Your story is your biggest asset and it's absent from
   the guides.
5. **Vague nouns.** "A useful result", "the instruction", "Get the next
   practical part of What should you never share with AI?" (that last line is
   template text leaking onto the page).
6. **Interaction hides the substance.** Choosers and accordions mean the reader
   sees about 1,000 characters before the form, mostly labels. The reference
   shows one complete module and blurs only the prompt, so the reader knows
   exactly what they will get.
7. **Examples without emotion.** "The team agreed to run a pilot..." is generic
   office text. The reference uses a child as the hero of a bedtime story, and
   money you are paying for things AI does free: situations the reader can see
   and feel.

## The copy formula to use for every guide

```
TITLE          THE RESULT IN CAPS.            (3–5 words, a statement)
               Italic line with the twist.    ("One job each.")
COVER          Book-style mock-up using the existing illustration
INTRO          One paragraph: the exact result, the tools by name, what you get
               ("my 3 prompts, the setting to change first, the 30-second check")
BYLINE         By Fatiha Chikh · date · read time
PROMISE        "What you leave with:" one concrete line
01             How to use this guide (20 seconds)
02–0N          One module per job, always 4 parts:
                 The job · Why this tool · Setup (numbered) · The prompt (blurred until sign-up)
HONEST NOTE    One short paragraph of limits and checks. Once, not everywhere.
GATE           "The rest is yours." Free · no card · the blurred prompt stays visible above it
KEEP READING   2–3 cover cards
```

Voice rules: speak to one person ("you"), write in the first person ("I use"),
give a clear recommendation, include real numbers (time, cost, count), name
product versions, link to the official source, and use no em dashes.

## Partner-marketing note

Guides that name a tool, explain why it is the right choice for a specific job
and link to the official page are the kind vendors reshare, and they qualify
for affiliate and partner programmes. Write each tool module so the vendor
would be proud to share it, and put the honest limits in the one "honest note".

## Recommendation

Keep the topics, covers and URLs. Rewrite the copy with this formula in batches
of 5, as plain text for approval first. Suggested batch 1: Claude at work, 4 AI
tools, The paste test, The 4-line prompt, Short answers only.
