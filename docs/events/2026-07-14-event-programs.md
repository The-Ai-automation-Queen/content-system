## The Cordiner formula (the 7 mechanics to replicate)

1. **Learn 20%, build 80%.** Her bootcamps are facilitated "get it done"
   sessions: the thing gets MADE in the room, so it is off the to-do list
   before people leave. Never lecture past 15 minutes without a build block.
2. **The live counter.** A running total of what the room produced (her
   "58,000 pieces created" number came from one cohort). The counter is the
   marketing asset for the next cohort. Screenshot it at the end, always.
3. **Templates preloaded.** Nobody starts from a blank page. Every build
   block opens with the relevant playbook or template already in their hands
   (the Fast Forward bonus stack does this job).
4. **Homework between days.** One small assignment overnight keeps day 2
   starting hot.
5. **The recording is the product.** Replays sold after with 12-month access.
   For us this is double duty: bootcamp recordings become the 54 unrecorded
   Whop course videos.
6. **Fixed pitch moments.** She sells at the end of each day, not
   continuously. The pitch is always the same shape: "you just did one piece.
   The complete system is [next tier]."
7. **Hot seats and show-and-tell.** Attendee results demonstrated live become
   testimonials on the spot. Ask permission to screenshot in the moment.

**What NOT to copy:** her promise inflation. She sells "12 months of content
in 2 days." We promise a month, deliver a month, and let the counter
over-deliver. Facts trace or die applies on stage too.

## Door 1: "Hire Your First AI Employee" — 90-minute masterclass

Promise: you leave with one AI employee running. Cap: none needed (webinar
format works). Counter: "employees hired live today."

| Time | Segment | What happens | Source lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | **Watch it work** | Live demo of the machine doing a morning of busywork: inbox summary, drafted posts, keyword DM firing. No slides. | M0-L00 (Shock and Awe) |
| 10-20 | **Pick the right task** | The 3-question filter (repeats on a schedule? same steps? zero judgment?). Everyone writes their task down. Wrong task = why people quit. | M0-L00 + the 061 method |
| 20-30 | **The safety brief** | The Traffic Light Rule: green/amber/red data. Everyone marks their task's data color before we build. | M0-L04 |
| 30-70 | **THE BUILD** | Hire the inbox employee together, step by step on their accounts: write the job description in plain English, connect Gmail, run it twice, fix what's off. Screen-share hot seats for stuck people. | M2-L02 + the Employee #004 playbook |
| 70-80 | **Make it stick** | The 5-minute weekly check habit; how to judge output without redoing it. | M7-L05 (lite version) |
| 80-90 | **The door** | Counter screenshot. "That was one lesson. Fast Forward is all 54, in order." Founding offer + Q&A. | — |

Assets needed, all existing: the M0-L00 demo (her real dashboard), the
inbox playbook PDF, the Traffic Light card, the Fast Forward checkout link.

recordings become the course videos.

## Door 3: The Get It Done Day — max 10 seats

Promise: the half-finished thing runs before dinner. Menu model: each seat
picks ONE build at intake, a week before.

**The build menu** (each maps to an existing playbook):
1. Inbox manager (M2-L02 + playbook)
2. Lead follow-up machine (M2-L07 + FOLLOW UP setup)
3. Content engine: voice + 1-into-5 + scheduling (M1-L02, M3-L07, M3-L08)
4. Lead magnet + capture page (M4-L01, M4-L02)
5. Proposals and invoices flow (M4-L05, M4-L07)

**The day:**

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 09:00 | Stand-up: each person states their one build, out loud. It becomes the room's contract. |
| 09:15-10:45 | Build block 1 (templates pre-loaded per person; Fatiha rotates) |
| 11:00-12:30 | Build block 2 |
| 13:30-15:00 | Build block 3 |
| 15:15-16:15 | Fix-it hour: whatever is not running yet gets swarmed |
| 16:30-17:00 | Show-and-tell: every system runs LIVE in front of the room. Photos, testimonials, done. |

Rule announced at 09:00 and enforced: **nobody leaves with a to-do list.**
Pitch: none needed during the day; alumni get the community invite and a
Fast Forward code at show-and-tell.
