## Follow the reference's reading order

1. **Hero.** A strong, specific title and existing guide cover. The first line makes the topic obvious; the second promises the practical angle. Tell readers what they can do after this guide.
2. **In this guide.** A short contents map with section names that describe the actual work. On mobile, each item must take the reader to the corresponding nearby section or gate.
3. **Introduction.** A concise statement of the reader's problem, the result and who it is for. Include an honest level or reading time only when verified. Avoid background prose.
4. **The promise.** One concrete line: what the reader will leave with. If the promise says a checklist, comparison, prompt or working result, that exact thing must be delivered.
5. **01 · How to use this guide.** In a few sentences, say what to have ready, what to do in order and what to check. A beginner must be able to begin without guessing.
6. **02 onward · Practical modules.** Each module has a numbered heading and a clearly named task or job. For a tool guide, provide **what this tool does for the job, why it fits, the first settings or actions in numbered steps, a realistic example and what to check**. For a decision, diagnosis or build guide, keep the same level of specificity but choose steps that fit that task. Do not force five tools or identical interactions.
7. **Earn the email request.** Show a useful explanation and worked example before capture. Place the compact inline Lumail form at the point where the reader wants the next practical resource. Name that resource exactly. **The complete prompt and its Copy button remain after a successful submission**, never above the form. Do not use a page-load popup, full-page gate, login or reader account.
8. **Deliver the rest.** Give the complete uncollapsed prompt, checklist, instructions or file promised by the form. Keep the Copy action inside the prompt block. Prompts may be detailed; surrounding prose stays lean. Give the reader a clear check of the output.
9. **Keep reading.** End with varied, relevant next guides using existing covers and working clean URLs. A separate site-wide newsletter form may sit in the footer; it does not replace the guide's inline capture.

## Copy quality check for each page

- Can the reader say what they will get after reading the hero and introduction?
- Does every numbered section answer a real question or move the task forward?
- Are the example and first actions specific enough to try, without inventing a client outcome?
- Are changeable product steps verified against official sources?
- Is there useful public teaching before the Lumail form, and is the full copyable prompt strictly after it?
- Does the content remain readable and interactive on a phone?

The approved Instagram walkthrough and *What AI actually is* provide Shift & Lead's own visual and interaction references. This screenshot supplies the **copy hierarchy and content depth**, not a licence to paste the same five-tool article into every topic.

## Keep production instructions off the public page

Rules about layout, approval, review status, source checking, capture placement, SEO, prompt visibility and editorial quality belong in this internal document and code only. Do not render them as reader-facing copy, headings, disclaimers or notes. The reader sees only the task, useful explanation, exact actions, the email offer and the promised resource. Review the rendered page for leaked production language before approval.

Never paste this brief, a build prompt, a checklist for editors, or any instruction addressed to the website team into the page copy. Do not mention the reference website, template, review mode, publication status, the email provider, or why the form is positioned where it is. Instructions *for the reader's own exercise* are welcome when they help the reader act; production instructions are never part of the guide.

This also applies inside copyable prompts and emails. A reader may copy task instructions for an AI tool, but must never copy editorial, implementation, approval, testing or publishing directions meant for the site team.
