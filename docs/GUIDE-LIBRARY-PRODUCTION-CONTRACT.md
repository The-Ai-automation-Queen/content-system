# Shift & Lead guide library production contract

This contract records the approved scope for the guide library and every guide produced from it. A guide is not complete when any required function below is missing.

## Scope for this phase

- Organize the public library by Beginner, Intermediate and Expert.
- Let readers bypass the learning sequence by searching or choosing an outcome.
- Preserve every existing guide and keep all 10 AI tool guides visible.
- Consolidate overlapping source material into 7 public hubs.
- Use the existing AI jargon guide as the quality baseline for visual direction and reading density.
- Do not add the Lead or governance curriculum in this phase.
- Do not publish borrowed sentences, distinctive examples or lightly paraphrased source copy.

## Public navigation

Readers must be able to enter the library through any of these routes:

1. Search for a topic, task or tool.
2. Select Beginner, Intermediate or Expert.
3. Select an outcome:
   - Understand AI
   - Choose an AI tool
   - Get better answers
   - Create content
   - Automate a task
   - Build an agent
   - Run business operations

The 7 public hubs are:

1. AI essentials
2. Better prompts and answers
3. AI tools
4. Content and creative work
5. Workflows and automation
6. AI agents
7. Business operations

## Required AI tool coverage

The library must keep visible guide entries for:

- ChatGPT
- Claude
- Gemini
- Copilot
- DeepSeek
- Grok
- Kimi
- Manus
- Meta AI
- Mistral

The cornerstone tool chooser links to the individual guide for each tool. No tool is hidden because it is less popular or requires more maintenance.

## Library card contract

Every guide card must:

- use a 16:9 cover;
- publish every image requested by a guide page as WebP;
- keep each published guide image at 250 KB or less;
- size standard covers at 1280 by 720 pixels and smaller inline illustrations to their real display need;
- show only the useful level over the image as live HTML;
- place the guide title below the image in compact, readable type;
- show the useful level, not `FREE` or reading-time metadata;
- include a maximum 2-line outcome statement;
- make the complete card one accessible link;
- show a prominent `Open guide` action;
- avoid fixed blank height and decorative empty space;
- work at 3 columns on desktop, 2 on tablet and 1 on mobile;
- preserve keyboard focus and reduced-motion behavior.

## Guide page contract

The shared React guide system must support glossary, explainer, tutorial, workflow, decision and tool-verdict compositions. The page composition can change, but each guide brief must account for these functions:

1. Branded illustrated hero with a concrete title and 1 short outcome sentence.
2. Primary email capture directly below the promise when the guide has a deliverable.
3. Immediate answer that earns the next scroll.
4. Essential framework or shortest reliable method.
5. A recognisable Shift & Lead business example.
6. A practical prompt, checklist, decision tool, worksheet or template.
7. A clear result check, limitation or failure condition where relevant.
8. A guide-specific deliverable that is different from the page.
9. Exactly 3 useful next-guide cards.
10. One relevant commercial action, or a clean ending when no action belongs.

Do not force decorative sections, repeated component labels or unnecessary summaries into a guide simply to make every page look identical.

## Brand ownership

- Display `The AI Automation Queen` as simple live HTML in the open-guide hero. The mascot represents the Queen on cards.
- Do not place a creator-name box on library, hub, featured or related-guide card images.
- Keep `Shift & Lead` in the site identity, metadata and creator footer, not in the image-area label.
- Add `Created by The AI Automation Queen · Shift & Lead` to the guide footer.
- Apply the signature to downloadable assets and social-sharing artwork.
- Keep the generated illustration itself free of baked-in text. The overlay remains editable, accessible and responsive.
- Use canonical URLs and author metadata on every guide.

## Hero clarity

Every guide hero uses exactly 4 reader-facing elements: creator label, title, 1 short outcome sentence and 1 capture button. Do not repeat the deliverable name, format or use case beneath the button. That explanation belongs inside the capture popup. Keep the promise to 20 words or fewer and the button label to 5 words or fewer.

## Mascot identity

- The recurring character is the exact small blue robot mascot already established in the approved AI jargon, prompt and tool artwork.
- Preserve its round cobalt helmet and body, ivory face, large dark eyes, small silver crown with a blue diamond, white and silver jointed limbs and glowing blue chest symbol.
- Make the robot an active operator, guide, investigator or decision-maker. Keep it large enough to recognise on a guide card.
- Never depict a human queen, realistic woman, generic princess, generic humanoid or a different robot.
- `The AI Automation Queen` is the creator brand shown in live HTML. It is not a human character to illustrate.
- No character or brand name is baked into the image itself.
- PNG files may remain as working sources, but public page markup, metadata and catalogue data must reference only the optimized WebP file.

## Cover colour rhythm

- Use cobalt, deep blue, signal red, teal and warm ivory as the approved cover fields. Red is an equal part of the system, not an error or an exception.
- Vary the background across adjacent library cards so the page does not become one uninterrupted blue wall.
- Choose colour to strengthen the visual metaphor and collection rhythm. Do not assign a colour because of a tool logo or copy another brand's palette.
- Keep enough calm negative space and contrast for the live title and creator signature. White live text is permitted on cobalt, deep blue, teal and signal red. Use charcoal live text on warm ivory.
- Keep the mascot's cobalt body unchanged on every background. On blue fields, add an ivory or brass separation zone when needed so the robot remains distinct.

## Source transformation

For each guide, create a source brief before drafting. It records:

- every relevant workbook source;
- the concepts and useful prompt mechanics to retain;
- facts that need current verification;
- overlapping material to consolidate;
- dated or weak material to exclude;
- the new Shift & Lead example and operating point of view;
- the reader level, outcome, hub and next guides;
- the practical and downloadable assets.

Use source material closely for coverage, completeness and practical value. Write the published explanation, examples and transitions independently. Do not claim ownership of copied source wording by adding Shift & Lead branding.

## Email capture contract

Each lead-magnet guide must have:

- one popup capture near the top;
- a specific button label describing the deliverable;
- a unique Lumail tag and guide identifier;
- a working immediate download after submission;
- the same asset delivered by email;
- no duplicate bottom capture;
- a clear failure message when email delivery is unavailable.

## Copy rules

- Write for a capable non-specialist.
- Use digits for numbers.
- Do not use em dashes.
- Define an AI term without requiring another undefined AI term.
- State direct instructions as instructions.
- Keep paragraphs short without turning every sentence into a slogan.
- Use concrete Shift & Lead examples involving real tasks, customers, evidence, cost, access, reputation or approval.
- Never imply that an AI system is a person or employee. Use `AI teammate` unless correcting the phrase `AI employee`.

## Completion gate for each guide

A guide is complete only when all of these exist and have been checked:

- approved source brief;
- original structured copy;
- relevant 16:9 small blue robot mascot cover;
- branded hero overlay;
- guide-specific downloadable asset;
- Lumail guide mapping;
- related-guide mapping;
- canonical metadata;
- desktop visual check;
- mobile visual check;
- interaction and download check;
- passing production build;
- published route verification.
