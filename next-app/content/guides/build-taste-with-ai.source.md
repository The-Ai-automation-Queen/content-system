`content-and-creative-work` owns the full content path. It moves an allowed source through a reader change, main piece, channel versions, review and publish decision.

`research-to-content-workflow` owns the detailed source-to-publish workflow. It captures evidence, adds a point of view, drafts, verifies, publishes and learns.

`better-prompts-and-answers` owns briefing the task, asking for clearly different options when the direction is unclear, scoring answer quality, improving the named weak part and choosing Use, Revise or Stop.

`build-taste-with-ai` owns creative judgment. It makes the reader's standard visible, compares real options against that standard, records why an option wins, finishes the selected work and saves the pattern for later briefs.

- the hub's 6-step source-to-publish learning path or planner;

The boundary is simple: the content hub routes the content job. The research workflow controls evidence and publishing. The prompt hub improves the brief and answer. ## Critical claim correction

Do not claim that taste is the only skill AI cannot replace, uniquely human, impossible for AI to support or a permanent defence against automation.

The public position is narrower:

- AI can suggest options, compare patterns, describe differences and surface possible weaknesses.
- A person sets the purpose, chooses the standard, decides which tradeoff fits the situation and owns approval.
- Good judgment depends on context, practice, evidence and feedback. It is not proven by confidence or personal preference alone.
- The same option can be strong for 1 reader and wrong for another. Taste must be tied to a real job and standard.

## Locked 5-step method

### 1. Define the job and the standard

- Action: name the reader, channel and result the work must support. Write up to 5 observable standards, such as the main point is clear, the action is visible, the tone matches an approved example or the cover reads at card size.
- Finish line: another reviewer can use the standards without asking what `better` means.

### 2. Compare 3 real options

- Action: put exactly 3 genuinely different directions side by side. Use the same source facts, brief and non-negotiable rules for all 3.
- Finish line: the options differ in a meaningful choice, not only colour, wording or decoration.

### 3. Choose and explain

- Action: record what each option gets right, where it fails and which tradeoff it makes. Select the option that best serves the job and write the reason without referring only to personal liking.
- Finish line: another person can understand why the selected option fits and why the other 2 were rejected.

### 4. Finish the selected work

- Action: correct what the chosen option still misses. Check detail, tone, order, hierarchy, format, edge cases and unnecessary material. Keep facts, rights, privacy and approval as separate hard stops.
- Finish line: every manual change has a reason tied to the reader, job or non-negotiable rule.

### 5. Save the rule for next time

- Action: write 1 short rule from the final change and add it to the next brief or review checklist. Keep the before and approved versions when rights allow.
- Finish line: the next person can apply the saved rule before creating new options.

## Result check and stop rules

The practice is complete when:

- the real job, reader, channel and decision are named;
- up to 5 standards are observable rather than vague;
- exactly 3 meaningful options were compared against the same brief;
- the selected option has a written reason that explains what it gains and gives up;
- every finishing change connects to the reader, job or hard rule;
- important claims have a separate source check;
- references guide the quality without being copied;
- exactly 1 useful rule is saved for the next brief;
- the person responsible approves the final work.

Stop or ask for review when:

- the option contains a factual claim that has not been checked;
- a reference, image, quote, voice sample or design may not be used or adapted;
- personal, private or client material lacks approval;
- a legal, financial, medical, security or other expert point cannot be judged by the reviewer;
- the score conflicts with a non-negotiable fact, right, privacy or brand rule;
- the team cannot explain the choice beyond `I like it`;
- the work is being polished before the reader job or intended result is clear.
