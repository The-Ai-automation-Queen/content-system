## Critical claim corrections

Do not claim that this method makes an AI system recommend, rank or cite a business.

The public position is narrower:

- A search or AI answer system can use only information it can reach through its own sources and rules.
- Clear, current and consistent public facts reduce avoidable confusion. They do not control which source a system selects.
- A website is the business's strongest owned source, but an answer system may also use search indexes, profiles, reviews, publishers and other public pages.
- A crawler permission is an access choice, not a visibility guarantee.
- A sitemap is a discovery hint, not an indexing order.
- Structured data can help a supported search system understand page meaning or make a page eligible for a feature. It does not guarantee a ranking, answer or citation.
- A public profile must follow its current eligibility and content rules.
- A manual AI search test is a dated sample. Results may change by system, location, account, history and run.
- An AI citation is not proof that the answer is correct. The cited page must be opened and checked.

## Locked 4-step method

### 1. Map each question to the strongest owned source

- Action: write 6 questions a real customer asks before choosing, contacting or trusting the business. For each question, write the short answer the business can prove, choose the strongest page controlled by the business and name the person who approves it.
- Finish line: every question has a clear, current answer and 1 owned source page, with no claim the owner cannot support.

### 2. Make each source clear and reachable

- Action: state the useful fact in visible words, give the page a clear title and heading, link to any proof the reader needs and record who updates it. Ask the site owner or developer to check the page response, login status, canonical URL, `noindex`, sitemap, internal links and current crawler rules. Add supported structured data only when it matches the visible page. Check current vendor documentation before editing `robots.txt`, a firewall or a bot setting.
- Finish line: a customer can find the answer on a public, connected page, and the technical owner has ruled out an accidental block. No claim of indexing or citation is made.

### 3. Align the public record

- Action: update the public profiles and legitimate sources the audience actually uses. For a local business, check the approved name, location or service area, phone, hours, category, services and website. For an online business, check the About page, official profiles, partner pages and earned coverage. Follow the current rules for every profile, review and mention.
- Finish line: the main public sources state the same approved facts, and every mismatch has an owner and fix.

### 4. Test the answer and fix the source gap

- Action: run the 6 questions across at least 2 current AI search systems for 12 dated checks. Record the system, account or location context, answer, cited URL and accuracy. Open each citation. Mark Seen accurately, Seen with an error, Not seen or Cannot test. Fix the strongest source of the error and schedule a retest.
- Finish line: all 12 tests are recorded, each wrong or missing fact has a source fix and no sample result is presented as a stable ranking.

## Result check and stop rules

The practice is complete when:

- exactly 6 real customer questions are recorded;
- every question has a current answer the business can prove and approve;
- every answer has 1 strongest owned source;
- public pages are useful to people and reachable without a login;
- the canonical URL, sitemap, internal link and index rule have been checked;
- crawler and firewall changes use current official instructions for the chosen system;
- search discovery, training and user-request access are treated as separate choices where the vendor supports them;
- structured data matches visible page content;
- local profiles are used only when the business is eligible and follow current rules;
- legitimate outside sources do not conflict with the approved facts;
- exactly 12 dated tests record the system, context, answer, cited URL and accuracy;
- every citation was opened before the answer was marked accurate;
- each wrong or missing fact has a source fix, owner and retest date;
- the business owner approves public facts and the qualified site owner or developer approves technical changes.

Limitations:

- Crawl access, indexing, a sitemap, structured data, a public profile and outside coverage do not guarantee a ranking, recommendation or citation.
- AI answers can change by system, date, location, account, history and run.
- A manual test is a useful sample, not complete visibility measurement.
- A cited page can still fail to support the answer.
- A public profile can have different eligibility and content rules by platform, country and business type.
- Search systems may use sources the business does not own or know about.
- A source fix may take time to be crawled, indexed or reflected, and no fixed delay should be promised.

Stop or ask for review when:

- the proposed source contains private, paid, personal, client or confidential information;
- the team is using `robots.txt` as the only protection for private material;
- the current official crawler, firewall, index or profile rule cannot be confirmed;
- a technical change could weaken security, expose an admin path or block a required search crawler;
- the business fact, offer, location, right, testimonial, review or public claim is not approved;
- an old page and current page conflict and no source owner has decided which version wins;
- a local profile is ineligible, unclaimed or managed without the business owner's permission;
- the plan includes fake reviews, paid undisclosed mentions, copied competitor wording or misleading structured data;
- a vendor or consultant promises guaranteed placement, citation share or ranking;
- the test depends on 1 answer, 1 account or a result that was not dated;
- the cited page was not opened and checked.
