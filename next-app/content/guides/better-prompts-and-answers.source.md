## Editorial job

This page is the Better prompts and answers routing hub. It helps a Beginner turn a real task into a brief, improve the named weak part of an answer and verify the result before use. Steps 1 to 4 are Beginner. Steps 5 and 6 are Intermediate.

The child guides retain their full jobs:

- `what-is-a-prompt` owns the complete 4-step prompt lesson and reusable prompt brief.
- `check-ai-answers` owns the claim-to-source check after an answer exists.
- `make-ai-clear-and-concise` owns the revision of a long or vague answer for a real reader.
- `chatgpt` owns the real-work tool fit test.
- `research-to-content-workflow` owns the full source-to-publish workflow.
- `claude` remains a separate source-heavy tool verdict and is not used as a related route on this hub.

This hub does not publish a prompt dump or reproduce any child guide. Its job is the decision layer across briefing, evidence, rules and limits, improvement and a separate fact-check.

## Concepts retained

- Start from the finished result, audience and decision the answer must support.
- Add only context that changes the result.
- Give the AI evidence you are allowed to use and state which source wins when material conflicts.
- Use 1 quality example when the desired standard is hard to describe.
- Set the required output, limits, missing-information rule and human approval point before the run.
- Ask for clearly different options when direction is unclear.
- When a weakness repeats, repair the brief, source or example that caused it.
- Let AI checking its own answer point to possible problems, then check important claims against the original sources yourself.
- Use a scorecard to choose Use, Revise or Stop instead of trusting polished language.

## Material excluded

- Lists of ready-made prompts.
- Any claim that a role instruction or longer prompt guarantees expert output.
- Current product features, modes, model names, plans, prices or usage limits.
- Claims that a new chat, a named mode or a particular tool automatically improves accuracy.
- Cost and speed claims about generating several versions.
- Claims that a fixed prompt can stop invented facts.
- Unsupported explanations of why a model invents facts.
- Creator examples, wording, slogans and visual treatments.

## Direct answer

Heading: `A better answer starts with a better job and ends with a real check`

Paragraph 1: `Tell AI what result the task needs, add only the context that changes that result and provide the evidence it must use. Then set the limits before it starts.`

Paragraph 2: `Do not trust the answer because it sounds polished. Improve the weak part, reopen the sources and check every important claim before you use, send or publish it.`

Key line: `Brief the job. Give it evidence. Check the result.`

## 5 task-led pathways

1. A Beginner with a vague request opens `what-is-a-prompt` to build a clear brief.
2. A reader with a long or vague answer opens `make-ai-clear-and-concise`, moves the point to the top and protects the detail that changes the decision.
3. A reader testing ChatGPT on real work opens `chatgpt` and runs the same draft, file and research tests against a scorecard.
4. A reader turning sources into publishable content opens `research-to-content-workflow` and follows the approval workflow.
5. A reader who needs to check facts before using an answer opens `check-ai-answers`, compares every important claim with original information and makes a use decision.

## 6-step learning sequence

1. Beginner: brief the job.
2. Beginner: add useful context.
3. Beginner: provide evidence.
4. Beginner: set rules and limits.
5. Intermediate: improve the weak part.
6. Intermediate: verify separately.

Every step names an action and an observable finish line. The sequence avoids repeating the child guide's full prompt formula.

## Shift & Lead example

A potential client asks whether the Shift & Lead Build Sprint includes strategy, implementation or both. AI prepares a reply under 120 words using the current service page and scope template. The brief bans new deliverables, deadlines, guarantees and prices. The 1st draft is stopped because it invents a turnaround time. The promise that is not backed by a source is removed, every service claim is checked against the sources and Fatiha approves the final reply.

This example is distinct from the child-guide examples. It shows that AI may shape the wording but cannot invent the scope or own a customer promise.

The prompt builder contains all 9 required fields:

1. Task and finished result.
2. Audience and decision supported.
3. Context that changes the answer.
4. Evidence the reader is allowed to use and which source wins when sources conflict.
5. Quality example.
6. Required format.
7. Rules, limits and claims to avoid.
8. Missing-information rule.
9. Human approval point.

The scorecard uses 0, 1 or 2 for task fit, evidence, completeness, rules and limits, usability and uncertainty. The decision thresholds are 10 to 12 for Use after human review, 7 to 9 for Revise and rescore, and 0 to 6 for Stop and rebuild. Important claims that are not backed by a source, private information the reader is not allowed to use, unresolved source conflicts and actions without approval are automatic stops regardless of the total.

## Result check

The reader succeeds when the brief has a clear result, only useful background, evidence they are allowed to use, a rule for which source wins, rules and limits, a response for missing information and a named approval point. The reader must also find the weak part, revise it, check important claims against the original sources, score the result and choose Use, Revise or Stop.

Stop when the result is unclear, facts cannot be checked, the task needs private information the reader is not allowed to use, sources conflict without a rule for which source wins or the answer would act without human approval.
