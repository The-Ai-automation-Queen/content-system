## Original Shift & Lead point of view

Clear writing puts the point, the useful reason and the next action where the reader can find them. Length only matters when it gets in the way.

Cutting words is not the whole job. A short answer can still be vague, and a neat answer can hide the limit that changes the decision. The revision is finished when the reader understands what is true, why it matters and what happens next without losing a fact, risk or condition they need.

## Durable mechanics locked for the public guide

1. Name the reader and what the writing should help them understand, decide or do.
2. Write the point in 1 sentence before editing the draft.
3. Move the answer, decision or request to the opening.
4. Keep only support that helps the reader trust, understand or act on the point.
5. Remove restatement, repeated ideas, empty setup and summaries that add no decision.
6. Replace vague nouns and formal verbs with the specific thing and action.
7. Use the same word for the same thing throughout the piece.
8. Preserve uncertainty, evidence, limits, permission and risk when they change the reader's decision.
9. Send factual claims to `check-ai-answers` instead of treating clear wording as proof.
10. Name the person who approves customer-facing, public or lasting copy.

## Locked 4-step method

### 1. Name the point and the reader

- Action: state who will read the answer, what they need to understand and what they should decide or do next.
- Finish line: the point and reader action each fit in 1 clear sentence.

### 2. Put the answer at the top

- Action: move the answer, decision or request above the background. If the question can be answered yes or no, start with Yes, No or Not yet. Use the next sentence for the reason the reader needs most.
- Finish line: the opening gives the answer and the useful reason before the background.

### 3. Cut and clarify

- Action: remove question restatements, empty openings, repeated ideas and closing summaries that add no meaning. Replace vague nouns and formal verbs with the specific thing and action. Keep the same term for the same thing.
- Finish line: every sentence adds a new fact, reason, instruction, limit or decision. Use full, natural sentences. Do not cut words until the answer sounds clipped or becomes harder to understand.

### 4. Protect the meaning

- Action: add back any fact, limit, uncertainty, permission, risk or tradeoff the reader needs. Name the next action and the person who approves the final use.
- Finish line: making the answer shorter has not changed the promise, hidden a condition or removed information needed for a safe decision.

## Result check and stop rules

The revision is complete when:

- the reader and next action are named;
- the opening gives the point and useful reason;
- each later sentence adds a fact, reason, instruction, limit or decision;
- repeated ideas, empty setup and unnecessary summaries are gone;
- full, natural sentences remain and the edit does not sound clipped;
- specific nouns and direct verbs replace vague wording;
- the same term means the same thing throughout;
- facts, uncertainty, limits, permissions and risks that change the decision remain;
- important claims have been checked separately;
- the person responsible approves public, customer-facing or lasting copy.

Stop or ask for review when:

- cutting a sentence changes a promise, scope, price, date, right or approval;
- an important claim has not been checked against original information;
- personal, private or confidential information is not approved for the tool or task;
- permission to quote, copy, credit or publish source material is unclear;
- legal, financial, medical, security or other expert meaning could be changed by the edit;
- the reader needs a tradeoff or warning that the shorter version hides;
- the edit is chasing a word count instead of helping the reader understand or act.
