## Editorial job

This page is a routing hub, not another lesson about AI. It helps a Beginner choose the next useful guide, complete 1 low-risk task and learn only what that task requires.

The child guides retain their full jobs:

- `what-is-ai` explains where AI, automation, normal software and human judgment fit.
- `ai-jargon-guide` explains the 10 terms the reader is likely to meet.
- `what-is-a-prompt` teaches the complete brief for a useful answer.
- `get-better-at-ai` begins after 1 checked result and teaches repeated practice on new examples.

The hub does not repeat their definitions, term entries, examples or prompt formula. It provides the order, the decision map and a 7-day practice plan.

## Concepts retained

- Start with 1 real task instead of trying to learn every tool.
- Choose low-risk work the reader already understands and can check.
- Use real information the reader is allowed to use instead of asking AI to invent from nothing.
- Learn a term only when it blocks the current task.
- Make an attempt, compare the result, improve the brief and repeat with a new example.
- Record what changed so the reader develops 1 useful capability rather than collecting tutorials.
- Keep facts, private information and decisions about customers, money, access, rights or reputation with a person.

## Material excluded

- Full definitions already owned by the 2 AI essentials child guides.
- Prompt formulas already owned by `what-is-a-prompt`.
- Named thinking-mode lists and claims that a role instruction guarantees a better result.
- Tool rankings, tool stacks, free-plan claims, prices and product-specific setup.
- Model names, token limits, context-window sizes and changing feature descriptions.
- Ongoing practice after the 1st checked result. `get-better-at-ai` owns that work and its 4-week tracker. This hub stays focused on the 1st 7 days.
- Language that treats an AI system as a person, colleague or employee.

## Direct orientation

The opening uses 2 short paragraphs. It tells the reader to start with work they understand, give AI information they are allowed to use, ask it to prepare a result and keep the final decision. It then tells the reader to use the decision map and open only the guide needed now.

The Shift & Lead principle is: learn at the moment of use. Do not collect terms, prompts or tools before the task asks for them.

## 4-option decision map

1. If the reader does not know whether a task needs AI, open `what-is-ai`.
2. If unfamiliar language blocks the reader, open `ai-jargon-guide`.
3. If the task is clear but the result is vague, open `what-is-a-prompt`.
4. If the reader already has 1 useful result, open `get-better-at-ai` and improve the same kind of task on new examples.

## Numbered Start here sequence

1. Choose 1 real low-risk task and define the finished result.
2. Decide where AI belongs by using the task sorter in `what-is-ai`.
3. Check a term only when it blocks the task by using `ai-jargon-guide`.
4. Turn the task into a usable brief with `what-is-a-prompt`.
5. Run, check and repeat with a new example, then record Keep, Change or Stop.

## Shift & Lead example

Fatiha has 20 reader questions with names removed and needs evidence for the next guide decision. AI groups the questions and returns a table with each group, its count, question numbers and unclear items. Every group must link back to the original questions. AI must not invent demand or guess what a reader meant. Fatiha checks the groups and decides which guide belongs in the library.

This example shows AI preparing evidence without making the editorial decision. It is distinct from the examples in all 3 child guides.

## Result check

The reader succeeds when they can name 1 task and finish line, explain what AI prepares and what a person decides, open the right child guide, use information they are allowed to use, check the result, improve the brief, repeat with a new example and record Keep, Change or Stop.

Stop and review when the task has no clear finish line, important facts cannot be checked, private information is not allowed, the result would act on a customer, money, access, rights or reputation without approval, or the reader cannot judge whether the answer is useful.
