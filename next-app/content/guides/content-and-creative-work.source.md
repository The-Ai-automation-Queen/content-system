## Editorial job

This page is the Content and creative work routing hub. It helps a Beginner move 1 source they are allowed to use into 1 useful main piece, then create versions for other channels and complete a separate publish check. Steps 1 to 4 are Beginner. Steps 5 and 6 are Intermediate.

The child guides retain their full jobs:

- `research-to-content-workflow` owns the detailed source-to-publish workflow.
- `better-prompts-and-answers` owns brief repair and answer-quality checks.
- `which-ai-tool-for-what` owns task-led tool selection across text, images, audio and video.

The hub does not repeat the child workflow, prompt method or tool table. It provides the task routing, the ordered learning path and the source-to-publish planner shared across formats.

## Concepts retained

- Start with 1 trusted source, the right to use it and a clear reader change.
- Keep only facts and examples that serve the main point.
- Add a human judgment that goes beyond summarising the source.
- Build the main piece before creating versions for other channels.
- Use voice and visual examples you are allowed to use as direction, not material to copy.
- Change the hook, length, order and format for each channel without adding claims.
- Check facts, source rights, voice, links and the next action in a separate pass.
- Keep a named human approver and record the final URL.
- Use audience questions and responses to improve the next brief, not to prove a factual claim.

## Material excluded

- Copied creator prompts, examples, slogans, agent roles and design references.
- Claims that higher output volume means better content.
- Claims that 1 tool or agent can own research, taste, writing, design and publishing.
- Current product features, connectors, modes, model names, plans, prices or release dates.
- Speed, cost-saving, performance and income claims.
- Advice to copy another creator's wording, website, design or voice.
- Publishing automation before the manual source, draft and approval path works.

## Direct answer

AI can prepare notes, drafts and versions for other channels. The human owns the main point, the right to use the source, the voice and the publish decision.

The key line is: `AI prepares the work. You decide what is true, what is yours and what is ready to publish.`

## 4 task-led pathways

1. Saved research but nothing published routes to `research-to-content-workflow`.
2. A published answer that AI search misses, misstates or sends to an old page routes to `show-up-in-ai-search`.
3. Choosing a tool for text, images, audio or video routes to `which-ai-tool-for-what`.
4. Several polished options without an explainable choice route to `build-taste-with-ai`.

## 6-step learning sequence

1. Beginner: choose 1 trusted source and the reader change.
2. Beginner: pull only the facts and examples worth keeping.
3. Beginner: add the human point of view.
4. Beginner: build the main piece.
5. Intermediate: create a version for each channel without adding claims.
6. Intermediate: separately check facts, rights, voice, links and the next action, then approve publishing.

Every finish line is observable through a recorded source, a 1-sentence reader change, facts that link back to the source, a clear human judgment, a complete main draft, channel-fit versions and a recorded Publish or Stop decision.

## Result check and stops

The reader succeeds when the source and rights can be checked, the reader change is clear, facts serve the main point, the human judgment is distinct, the main piece is complete before other versions, every channel version adds no claim the sources do not support, and a named person approves publishing after a separate check.

Stop when the source cannot be reopened, rights are unclear, an important fact or link cannot be checked, the point of view is missing, a version for another channel adds a new claim, private information or client results lack approval, or the named approver has not recorded Publish.
