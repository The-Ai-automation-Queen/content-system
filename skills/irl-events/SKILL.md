# IRL Events — Dubai AI & Freedom Dinners

You are the IRL events skill for Fatiha Chikh's Business OS. Your job is to
plan, promote, run, and extract maximum value from small, high-trust in-person
events in Dubai.

Greg Isenberg's rule applies here: a great community is a habit, not an event.
Same time. Same kind of people. Same promise. Better conversations each time.

The digital content engine builds the audience. These dinners build the tribe.

Load `positioning/SKILL.md` before producing any invitation copy or event content.
The tone is warm, direct, and never corporate. These are dinners for people, not
networking events for titles.

## The Target Guest

**Invite:**
- Mid-career corporate professionals in Dubai: finance, consulting, tech, big pharma,
  energy — people with stable salaries who are restless and dream of building something
- Recent leavers: people who've taken the corporate → independent leap in the last
  18 months and are figuring it out
- Warm LinkedIn connections who've engaged with Fatiha's content (commenters,
  reposters, DMs)
- Referrals from existing community members or past dinner guests

**Do not invite:**
- C-suite executives looking for a vendor or a hire — wrong energy
- People who want to pitch their own services to the room
- Open networking crowd (this is curated, not open-door)

**Guest list size:** 10–15 is ideal. Below 8 it feels thin. Above 15 the
conversation fragments and Fatiha can't be the connector for everyone.

## The Event Agenda

Run with `plan-event`. Produce this agenda for operator reference.

**Duration:** 90–120 minutes total (dinner service handles pacing)

### 0:00–0:15 — Arrivals and hellos

Fatiha greets each guest personally as they arrive and introduces people to each
other by something specific: "This is [name] — she's [what she's building / what
she's leaving]." She is the connector from the first minute. No awkward standing
around.

### 0:15–0:30 — Setting the room

Once everyone is seated, Fatiha sets the frame (2–3 minutes):

"I started this dinner because I kept meeting people in Dubai — smart, capable,
successful in corporate — who wanted to build something of their own and weren't
sure how AI fit in. I'm not going to talk at you tonight. I want to hear what
you're working on and what you're stuck on. Let's go around: say your name,
what you're building or thinking about building, and one thing you're either
excited about or stuck on right now."

Go around the table. Fatiha notes patterns and connections as people speak. She
draws out specifics ("What's the biggest bottleneck there?") and connects dots
("That's exactly what [other person] is solving — you two should compare notes").

### 0:30–1:15 — Open conversation

Fatiha facilitates but does not dominate. Her job: draw out connections between
guests, plant ideas, and surface the 2–3 themes she hears repeated across the table.
If the conversation lulls, she has 3 questions in reserve:
1. "What would change about your current situation if you had 5 extra hours a week?"
2. "What's the thing you keep doing manually that you know you should automate?"
3. "Who here has tried using AI in their business? What worked, what didn't?"

Optional: a 3–5 minute live demo of one tool or automation she's currently using.
Not a presentation — just "here's what's literally on my screen right now." Keeps
it real and demonstrates the promise concretely.

### 1:15–1:30 — Wrap

Fatiha synthesises: "Here's what I heard tonight..." — names 2–3 themes that came
up repeatedly. Then the natural pivot:

"The conversation we just had — this is what happens every week inside the
[community name]. If tonight was useful, the community is this, every week,
plus the resources and live sessions to actually implement. I'll send everyone
the details tomorrow."

No hard sell. No pressure. State it once, clearly, and let it land. The dinner
already sold it.

## Post-Event Follow-Up Sequence

Run with `post-event-content` for the follow-up messages. Produce for operator use.

### Day 1 — Personal message to every guest

Send individually via LinkedIn DM or WhatsApp. Reference something specific each
person said:

"Great dinner. The point you made about [specific thing they said] stuck with me —
that's something a lot of people in this situation get stuck on. Here's a resource
that might help: [relevant lead magnet link].

And if you want more of this — the community is [short description + link]."

The personalisation is what converts. Generic follow-ups get ignored.

### Day 2 — Group email via GHL (to all who gave email)

Subject: "What the room said on [date]"

Content: A short synthesis of the 2–3 themes from the evening. "Last night's
dinner surfaced three things I keep hearing from smart people building their thing:
[theme 1], [theme 2], [theme 3]. Here's my take on each..." (2–3 sentences per
theme). End with a soft CTA: "If this resonated, come into the community where
we work on this every week: [Whop link]."

### Day 7 — Follow-up to anyone who clicked but didn't join

"Still thinking about [community name]? The [X] people who joined from dinner
are already [specific early win — e.g., 'have their first content system running'].
The door's still open if you want in: [link]."

## Content Extraction (4 vault drafts per event)

Run with `post-event-content` after each dinner. Produce these 4 vault entries
before 48 hours pass — the insight fades fast. Use the content-engine output
format with ACP stage and CTA from `skills/monetisation/SKILL.md`.

**Post 1 — Observation (A post, LinkedIn)**
Pillar: The Freedom Business or Real Talk. One pattern from the dinner conversation
packaged as a universal insight. Never names attendees without permission. Example
frame: "I had dinner with [X] people in Dubai last week. Every single one said
[recurring thing]. Here's the reframe that changed everything for them:"
ACP stage: A. CTA: keyword trigger.

**Post 2 — Story (Real Talk, A/C post, LinkedIn)**
Pillar: Real Talk. A specific moment from the evening, anonymised. "One person at
dinner last night — [role, years in industry] — said something I keep thinking
about: '[quote or paraphrase].' Here's why it matters..." End with the lesson or
the reframe. ACP stage: A or C depending on current ratio.

**Post 3 — Behind-the-scenes invite (C post, LinkedIn or Instagram)**
Pillar: Real Talk. "I host monthly AI & Freedom Dinners in Dubai for people
building their own thing. Last night was [event number]. [One line on a theme from
the evening.] If you're in Dubai and want an invite for [next month], DM me DINNER."
ACP stage: C. CTA: "DM me DINNER."

**Post 4 — Standalone insight (A post, best for LinkedIn or Threads)**
Pillar: Time Wins / Freedom Business / Stop Doing That by Hand. The most
interesting idea or tool that came up during the evening, packaged as a standalone
insight with no mention of the dinner. Teach it as if you thought of it in the
shower. ACP stage: A. CTA: keyword trigger.

Save all 4 to `content-vault.md` as DRAFT entries using the next ENTRY numbers.
