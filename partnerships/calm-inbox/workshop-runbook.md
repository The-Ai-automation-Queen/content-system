# Workshop Runbook — "Hack Your Inbox. Calm Your Mind."

**Format:** 90 minutes · max 20 participants · laptops open · interactive
**Facilitators:** Fatiha (AI) + Tessa (nervous system)
**One clear outcome:** every participant leaves with their own AI-powered email
workflow they can run tomorrow morning — plus a way to stay calm while running it.

> Angle (say it early, repeat it at the end): **AI organizes the work. Your
> nervous system decides how you respond to it.**

---

## §0 Before the room — logistics checklist

**Pre-event email to participants (send W−1, GHL template):**
- Bring your laptop, charged.
- Have a working AI assistant you can log into: ChatGPT, Claude, or Copilot —
  free tier is fine. Test the login *before* the workshop.
- Bring **3 real emails** from your inbox: one long thread, one you've been
  avoiding, one routine one. (If work email is sensitive, pick ones you can
  anonymize — we'll show you how.)
- Come with the number in your head: how many unread emails right now? No
  judgement. We've seen five digits.

**Room prep (Fatiha):** wifi tested with 20 devices · projector + demo laptop
with a prepared demo inbox (never demo a real client inbox) · printed
one-pagers (participant-handout §1) on every seat · 3 QR codes printed at exit ·
timer visible to facilitators.

**Demo inbox prep:** 6 fake-but-realistic emails staged in a doc, ready to
paste: (1) a 14-message thread about a slipping project deadline, (2) a vague
"quick call?" request from a senior person, (3) a client complaint with three
buried action items, (4) a newsletter, (5) a meeting-reschedule chain,
(6) an FYI-cc storm. Realism is what makes the room lean in.

## §1 Welcome — 10 min (both, Tessa leads)

**0:00–0:03 — Frame.** One sentence each on who you are. No bios, no slides of
logos. Then the promise: *"In 90 minutes you'll build an email workflow that
works tomorrow at 9am. Half of it is AI. The other half is you."*

**0:03–0:09 — Icebreaker.** Ask the room:
> **"What's the one thing your inbox steals from you?"**

Take 6–8 answers out loud. Cluster them live on a flip chart into: **Focus ·
Time · Energy · Evenings · Peace of mind.** (They will land in these — the
brief predicts it, and it always happens.)

**0:09–0:10 — The bridge (Tessa, one line):**
*"Notice that only one of those five is about time. The rest are about
capacity. That's why there are two of us up here — Fatiha fixes the volume,
I'll show you what to do about the capacity."*

## §2 Hack Your Inbox — live demo, 20 min (Fatiha)

Style: fast, funny, real. Type live, let the AI answer live, react honestly if
an answer is mediocre ("see, that's an 80% draft — the 20% is your job").
Every demo = one slide with the prompt, then live screen.

**0:10–0:12 — The 60-second safety rules (do NOT skip):**
1. Company email is company data. If your employer has an approved AI tool
   (Copilot, enterprise ChatGPT/Claude), use that at work.
2. If you're on a personal account: strip names, numbers, and anything
   confidential before pasting. The prompts today work fine on anonymized text.
3. AI drafts, **you** decide and you send. Nothing today auto-sends anything.

**The five demos** (~3 min each; full prompt text also in participant-handout §2):

**Demo 1 — Untangle a thread** (prompt #1). Paste the 14-message thread.
Punchline: *"Fourteen emails, three minutes of reading, or one prompt. Where
things stand, who wants what, and what happens if I do nothing."* The
'if-I-do-nothing' line is the calm-maker — most emails survive being ignored,
and now you know which ones.

**Demo 2 — Draft the reply you've been avoiding** (prompt #2). Use the client
complaint. Show the trick: you give AI the *decision* in one blunt line ("we
messed up, we'll refund the March invoice, no we won't rebuild for free") — AI
gives you the diplomacy. *"AI is not deciding for you. You decide in ten
seconds; it types for two minutes."*

**Demo 3 — Turn emails into a to-do list** (prompt #3). Paste 3 emails, get a
checklist with owners, deadlines, effort scores — sorted. *"Your inbox is a
to-do list other people wrote for you. This turns it back into one you
control."*

**Demo 4 — Triage: what needs you, what can wait** (prompt #4). Paste the
subject lines of all 6 demo emails. AI sorts into 🔴 today / 🟡 this week /
🟢 later / ⚪ archive — and challenges one fake urgency. Land the beat:
*"Notice it just told me the 'quick call?' from a VP is not actually urgent.
That feeling that it is? That's the part Tessa handles."*

**Demo 5 — Build your reusable prompt** (prompt #5). The meta-move: AI
interviews you (role, priorities, who never waits, how you sound) and writes
your personal "Inbox Rules" block you paste at the start of any session.
*"This is the difference between using AI and having a system. You'll build
yours in the next thirty minutes."*

Transition: *"Laptops open. Your turn."*

## §3 Exercise — Design Your Personal Inbox Workflow, 30 min (Fatiha leads, both circulate)

**Goal:** a workflow each person can run tomorrow morning. Map on the handout:

```
INCOMING EMAIL  →  AI SUPPORT  →  HUMAN DECISION
```

**0:30–0:35 — Setup (5 min).** Everyone writes down:
- The 3 types of email that eat most of their time (e.g. status threads,
  client requests, internal cc-noise).
- Their honest number: minutes per day in the inbox.

**0:35–0:50 — Build (15 min).** For each of their 3 email types, fill one row
of the blueprint: *What AI does* (which of the 5 prompts, adapted) → *What I
decide* (the one human judgement in the chain). Then run prompt #5 for real:
AI interviews them and writes their personal Inbox Rules block. **Everyone
saves that block somewhere they'll find it tomorrow** (notes app, draft email
to self — check this happens).

**0:50–0:58 — Test on real email (8 min).** Take one of the 3 real emails they
brought, run it through their new workflow end to end. Facilitators circulate;
the most common fix is prompts that are too polite — push them to put the
decision in the prompt.

**0:58–1:00 — One share-out (2 min).** Ask for one before/after out loud
("this thread would've taken me 20 minutes; it took 90 seconds"). That
sentence is tomorrow's testimonial — note who said it.

## §4 Calm Your Mind — 15 min (Tessa)

*(Tessa's segment, her delivery; this outline matches the brief so the
handoff and handout stay consistent.)*

**1:00–1:08 — The teach.** The hidden cost of an overloaded inbox isn't time —
it's **nervous system activation**:
- Why email triggers urgency (every unread is an open loop + a social demand;
  your brain treats both as mild threat).
- Decision fatigue and cognitive overload — 200 emails is 200 micro-decisions
  before lunch.
- **Busy vs. activated:** busy is a schedule state; activated is a body state.
  You can be calm and busy. Most of the room is the opposite: not that busy,
  chronically activated.
- How high performers accidentally *train* themselves into constant
  reactivity — every instant reply teaches your nervous system (and your
  colleagues) that you're an always-on service.

**1:08–1:15 — The framework + practice:** **Pause → Prioritize → Proceed.**
- **Pause** — one physiological sigh / breath before opening the inbox. You're
  choosing to respond, not react. (Practice it once, whole room, 30 seconds.)
- **Prioritize** — one question: *"What actually needs me today?"* (Your AI
  triage from Demo 4 just handed you the answer — this is where the two halves
  of the workshop click together.)
- **Proceed** — act on that list only. Everything else has a place (this
  week / later / archive), and a place is not your attention.

## §5 Integration — Your Calm Inbox Blueprint, 15 min (both)

**1:15–1:27 — Everyone completes the one-pager** (handout §1), combining both
halves:
1. **What can AI handle?** (their 3 workflow rows from §3)
2. **What requires my attention?** (their human-decision column, in one list)
3. **How will I protect my mental bandwidth?** (pick concrete rules: inbox
   opens at set times, not on waking · PPP before every session · reply-speed
   expectations reset · notification settings changed *right now, in the room*)

**1:27–1:30 — Commitment line.** Each person writes one sentence at the bottom:
*"Tomorrow at ___ I will run my workflow on ___."* Pairs read them to each
other — a witnessed commitment survives the week; a private one doesn't.

## §6 Closing — 5 min (both)

**Final reflection (Tessa asks, silence is fine):**
> **"If your inbox no longer dictated your day — what would you do with the
> energy you get back?"**

**The landing line (Fatiha):**
> *"AI handles the volume. Your nervous system handles the response. When both
> improve, work feels lighter — not because there's less work, but because
> there's less friction."*

**Next steps (30 seconds, no hard sell):** *"Tomorrow morning you'll get one
email from us: the 5 prompts, your workflow template, and the Pause →
Prioritize → Proceed card. At the door there are three QR codes — the free
Calm Inbox Kit for a colleague who needs it, and details on the 4-week pod for
the ten people who want to go further than the inbox."*
