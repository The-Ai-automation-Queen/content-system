# 4-Week AI Integration Pod — Full Curriculum

**"From Knowing AI to Working Better with AI"**
Max 10 participants · USD 150 pp · 4 × 90-min live sessions (weekly, same
slot) + light homework · runs online or hybrid (Dubai room + Zoom).

**Objective (from the brief):** build the **judgement, confidence and habits**
to integrate AI into day-to-day work *without becoming dependent on it*.
Every session = practical AI application (Fatiha) + reflection/coaching
(Tessa). Successful adoption isn't a technology change; it's a change in how
you work and think.

**Session skeleton (all four weeks):**
- 0:00 Check-in circle (Tessa, 10 min) — one word for the week + one AI moment (win or faceplant)
- 0:10 Teach (20 min)
- 0:30 Practice lab (35 min) — laptops, real work, facilitators circulating
- 1:05 Coaching circle (Tessa, 15 min) — the week's reflection questions
- 1:20 Commit + homework (10 min) — witnessed, written, specific

**Pod rules (say in week 1):** real work only, no demo theater · what's shared
in the pod stays in the pod · "I didn't do the homework" is data, not shame ·
company-confidential material goes in company-approved tools or gets
anonymized.

## Week 1 — Where AI Adds Value

**Promise:** stop using AI for everything; start using it where it pays.
**Outcome:** a personal **AI Opportunity Map**.

**Pre-work (sent at enrollment):** for 3 workdays, keep a dirty log of what
you actually do in 30-min blocks (a notes app is fine). Don't optimize
anything yet — we need the honest picture, not the LinkedIn version.

**Teach (Fatiha):** the four-quadrant sort for every recurring task —
1. **Automate** — repetitive, rule-based, low judgement (AI/automation does it,
   you spot-check): status summaries, first-draft replies, meeting notes,
   formatting, research collection.
2. **Assist** — judgement stays yours, AI compresses the labor: drafts you
   edit, options you choose between, analyses you interrogate.
3. **Human** — deliberately NOT AI: real decisions, sensitive conversations,
   relationships, anything where *you thinking* is the actual work.
4. **Delete** — the plot twist: tasks the log reveals nobody needs. AI can't
   fix a task that shouldn't exist. Kill it instead of automating it.
Plus the honesty rule: **where traditional beats AI** — if the manual way
takes 2 minutes and prompting takes 5, the manual way wins. Speed is measured,
not assumed (that's Week 3's lab).

**Practice lab:** everyone maps their 3-day log onto the quadrants, then picks:
- 2 **Automate** candidates (highest frequency × lowest judgement)
- 2 **Assist** candidates (highest pain)
- 2 tasks they hereby declare **Human** — written down, protected on purpose
Facilitators pressure-test: "is that Human because it needs you, or because
it's comfortable?" — and the reverse: "you put decisions in Automate; why?"

**Coaching circle (Tessa):** What did the log make you feel — where's the
discomfort? Which task are you protecting because it's your identity, not
because it needs you? What are you afraid AI adoption says about your value?

**Homework:** implement ONE Automate candidate using the workshop prompts
(or prompt #5's Inbox Rules pattern applied to the new task). Log minutes
saved — real numbers, we'll use them in Week 3.

## Week 2 — Working With AI Without Losing Yourself

**Promise:** AI becomes a thinking partner, not another source of overwhelm.
**Outcome:** a personal **guardrails list** — attention rules that survive contact with a busy week.

**Check-in focus:** what happened with the Week-1 automation — and how did it *feel*?

**Teach (Tessa leads, Fatiha seconds):** the five new failure modes that
appear precisely *because* AI removed the effort:
- **Decision fatigue** — AI gives you 5 options where you used to have 1; options are a cost.
- **Prompt perfectionism** — 40 minutes iterating a prompt for a 10-minute task. (Fatiha demos the countermove live: timeboxed prompting — 2 attempts, then ship or do it manually.)
- **Information overload** — AI makes producing input infinite; your attention is still finite.
- **Over-reliance** — the day you can't draft the email yourself anymore. Keep "manual reps" on purpose.
- **The "good enough" problem** — AI makes endless iteration possible; define done *before* you start, or the tool defines it as never.
Then the regulation layer: attention is a budget, activation is spend. Three
practical moves: single-tab AI sessions with a written goal · PPP applied to
AI outputs (pause before accepting, prioritize what you'll actually use,
proceed — don't re-prompt for sport) · a daily hard stop where AI tools close.

**Practice lab:** each participant drafts their guardrails list —
- "I use AI for ___ only after deciding ___ myself first."
- "Prompting timebox: ___ attempts / ___ minutes, then ship or go manual."
- "Done means ___ (defined before I open the tool)."
- "I keep doing ___ manually, on purpose, to keep the muscle."
Then a live stress test: everyone runs one real task under their own rules
while facilitators watch for the exact failure mode they named.

**Coaching circle:** Where this week did AI *add* cognitive load instead of
removing it? What does "good enough" cost you to say out loud? Who taught you
that faster always equals better?

**Homework:** run the guardrails for a full week. Bring one honest violation
to Week 3 — the moment the rules broke and what broke them.

## Week 3 — Building Confidence Through Practice

**Promise:** confidence through experience, not theory.
**Outcome:** 2 tested real-work workflows + the beginnings of a **judgement log**.

**Structure change:** this session is 80% lab. Participants bring one real,
current, annoying work challenge (arranged the week before — chase them).

**Teach (10 min only, Fatiha):** the experiment protocol, run on everything:
1. **Would AI help here?** (hypothesis, stated before touching the tool)
2. **Is it actually faster?** (time both paths — the manual estimate and the
   AI run; Week-1 homework numbers join the dataset)
3. **What must remain human?** (name the judgement point in the chain)
4. **What can be standardised?** (if it worked: save the prompt, name it,
   file it — a workflow is a prompt with a home)

**Practice lab (~50 min):** work in pairs — driver runs the experiment on
their real challenge, navigator holds the protocol and the timer. Swap at
half-time. Facilitators roam: Fatiha unblocks the tooling, Tessa catches the
narration ("I'm probably doing this wrong" → that's a confidence pattern, not
a tooling problem). The coaching target all session: **developing judgement,
not finding better prompts.**

**Coaching circle:** Where did you trust the AI and regret it? Where did you
*not* trust it and waste 20 minutes redoing good work? What does your gut
actually know about when to trust yourself first? (This is the "trust both AI
and yourself" muscle — it only builds on real reps, which is why this week
exists.)

**Homework:** run the protocol solo on 2 more tasks. Log each in the
judgement log: hypothesis → result → keep/kill → what I learned about *me*.

## Week 4 — Designing Your Human + AI Operating System

**Promise:** sustainability — a way of working that survives September.
**Outcome:** a personalised, one-page **Human + AI Operating System**, applied at work the next morning.

**Teach (15 min, both):** an operating system is just principles you don't
re-decide daily. Five sections, and everything from the pod feeds one of them:
1. **When I use AI** (Week 1's Automate/Assist quadrants)
2. **When I don't** (Week 1's protected-Human list + Week 2's manual reps)
3. **How I protect focus** (Week 2's guardrails + PPP + inbox blueprint)
4. **How I keep thinking** (Week 3's judgement log habit — AI challenges my
   thinking; it doesn't replace it. One rule that scales: ask AI to argue
   *against* your conclusion once a week.)
5. **How I keep learning** (a monthly 30-min review: what's new, what broke,
   one experiment — instead of drowning in AI news daily. Delete the FOMO.)

**Practice lab:** write the OS, one page, no more. Then the exchange: pairs
swap OSs and attack them — "which rule dies in your first busy week?" Rules
get rewritten until they'd survive a Monday.

**Coaching circle (Tessa, the closing one):** Who were you with AI four weeks
ago — and who are you now? What's the one habit that, if you keep only it,
keeps the rest alive? Same question as the workshop, wider: *if work felt
lighter from here on — what would you do with the capacity?*

**Graduation (last 10 min):**
- Each person reads their OS header line out loud.
- The transformation, named (from the brief): more **clarity** instead of more
  noise · better **use of time** instead of just working faster · **confidence**
  instead of second-guessing. *"The future doesn't belong to the people who use
  AI the most. It belongs to the people who know when to use it, when not to,
  and how to stay human while doing both."*
- Next doors, 60 seconds, no pitch theater: Fatiha's systems track (Starter
  Kit / community) for the "now automate my whole business" people · Tessa's
  coaching track for the "the inbox was never the real problem" people ·
  everyone stays on the shared list either way.
