## 5. The production engine — you already built it, now make it a ritual

You have the inputs. What is missing is a fixed weekly loop that ends in *posts published*, not drafts filed.

**Weekly content ritual (90 min, batched — Chris Donnelly's separated modes):**

1. **Monday — Intake (15 min):** Read the week's research digest + competitor watch. Pull 1–2 ideas. Assign each to a pillar.
2. **Tuesday — Write (45 min, no editing, no design):** Draft the one new Build-pillar piece for the week. Run it through your inspiration-library patterns.
3. **Wednesday — Approve & schedule (30 min):** Score against your critic bar (≥9.0), fact-check flags cleared, then **schedule the whole week at once.** Scheduling is the single change that breaks the 7-week zero-post streak.
4. **Throughout — the 30/30/30 rule:** 30 min engaging before you post, 30 min on comments right after, 30 min more in the first hour. The first hour decides distribution.

**The vault-audit becomes your scoreboard.** Right now it reports "0 posted" every week. The goal is that next month's audit reports a POSTED count climbing and a backlog being actively consumed and refilled. That number is the only one that matters at the start.

## 6. The metrics that actually matter

Ignore vanity. Track these, in order:

1. **Posts shipped / week** — target 4. This is the only metric for the first 30 days. Nothing else moves if this is zero.
2. **Repost / share ratio** (Chris Donnelly) — shares cross network boundaries; likes stay inside your audience. A post with 50 reposts beats one with 500 likes. This is your growth signal.
3. **Comment depth** — replies that tell a story (your CTAs are built for this). Quality of conversation > count.
4. **DM conversations started** — the actual pipeline to high-ticket advisory. Build comment-trigger CTAs into resource posts to feed this.
5. **Saves on carousels** — leaders saving "send slide 3 to your COO" content signals real B2B intent.

Follower count is a lagging side-effect, not a goal. Clare Kitching's model: depth of resonance from a smaller audience converts to consulting better than broad reach.
