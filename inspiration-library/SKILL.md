AI, startups, scaling, personal branding, digital business, leadership.
He does not deviate. Fixing to six pillars trains LinkedIn's topic-association algorithm to assign him authority in those areas. Every post reinforces one of the six — nothing off-topic, nothing personal.

**Format signature:**

**Visual brand — the green system:**
Consistent signature green color across every carousel, banner, and graphic. Every post is identifiable at a glance before the viewer reads a word. The color IS the brand recognition mechanism — it stops the scroll before the hook does.

**Hook formula — contrarian operational:**
Open with a direct statement that contradicts a widely held belief, using operational language (not inspirational language).

Internal test: "Is that good enough to stop you?" — he applies this to the first line before publishing anything.

- Operational, not aspirational. "Execution over performance" not "believe in your vision."

**The 30/30/30 engagement rule:**
Spend 30 minutes engaging with others' content before posting, 30 minutes responding to comments immediately after posting, and 30 minutes engaging again in the first hour. The first-hour engagement window determines algorithmic distribution.

**Content batching — three separated work modes:**
1. Ideation (while walking — no screens, ideas only)
2. Writing (no design, no editing, just drafting)
3. Design approvals (separate session, not combined with writing)
This prevents creative fatigue and maintains quality across high volume. He takes 3-week breaks while the business runs — evidence that the system is genuine, not performance.

## Cross-Creator Pattern Playbook

These patterns appear across multiple creators. Apply them actively when writing scripts.

### Pattern 1: Provocation-First Hook (Allie K. Miller)
First sentence of the video IS the take. No intro, no context-setting, no warm-up.
**Format:** "[Uncomfortable truth about the audience's situation]."
**Examples for this user's audience:**
- "You're still copy-pasting the same DMs by hand, aren't you?"
- "Your business stops the second you stop. That's not a business — it's a job."
- "You don't need another AI tool. You need one system that runs without you."
- "You're working nights to look 'available.' The smart ones automated that part."

### Pattern 2: Fear-First Hook (Brooke Wright + Allie K. Miller)
Name the client's specific fear in second person before the reframe.
**Format:** "You [fear state]. Here is what is actually happening / what to do instead."
**Examples for this user's audience:**
- "You keep buying courses and you're still doing everything manually."
- "You want out of the 9-to-5, but the side hustle eats every evening you have."
- "You're scared that if you step away for a week, the whole thing falls apart."

### Pattern 3: Teach Your System, Not the Tool (Sabrina Ramonov)
Show the process for developing AI strategy, not just the output. The method IS the content.
**Format:** "Here is how I [built / designed / solved] [specific thing] — step by step."
**Apply:** Frame videos around your methodology, not just the topic. "Here's the exact setup I use to turn one video into a week of content" > "here's why repurposing matters."

### Pattern 4: Named Numbered Series (Harper Carroll)
Group topics into a named, numbered series. Creates completion urgency, forces follow, builds recurring distribution events.
**Format:** "Day N of [Series Name]" or "Episode N: [Topic]" or "[Series Name] — Part N"
**Apply:** Every cluster of related videos should have a series name. Plan the arc before filming Episode 1.

### Pattern 5: Comment-Trigger CTA (Sabrina Ramonov)
End videos with a comment trigger instead of a link click.
**Format:** "Comment '[keyword]' and I will send you [the framework / the checklist / the template]."
**Apply:** Replace bio link CTAs with comment triggers for high-distribution posts. Works especially well for resource-delivery content.

### Pattern 6: Identity Intersection Positioning (Dr. Nici Sweaney)
Name the specific intersection you occupy so precisely that your ideal client self-selects.
**Apply:** Content should consistently reinforce: AI + automation × everyday entrepreneurs × winning back your time. The intersection is the moat.

### Pattern 7: Vulnerability Before Credentials (Brooke Wright)
Lead with the relatable struggle before the expertise. Removes the "that is not for me" objection.
**Format:** "I [did this hard thing] without [the thing people assume is required] — here is what I learned."
**Apply:** Use this format for origin story content and trust-building early-funnel videos.

### Pattern 8: Platform-Native Creation (Silicon Valley Girl)
Create natively for each platform. Do not upload the same video everywhere.
**Minimum adaptation rule:**
- LinkedIn: open with the business insight, not the personal hook
- TikTok: open with the payoff, not the setup
- Instagram Reels: open with a visual or physical action, then talk

### Pattern 9: Daily Consistency at Set Time (Allie K. Miller + Jordan Wilson)
The posting schedule becomes an audience expectation. Consistency is the strategy.
**Apply:** Recommend a set posting day/time for each platform and hold it across all content planning. Jordan Wilson proves that a daily show format can eliminate content decision fatigue — the structure is fixed, only the topic rotates.

### Pattern 10: Result-First Demo (Riley Brown + Nate Herk)
Show the finished output in the first 10-15 seconds, then walk back through how to get there.
**Format:** "[Result appears on screen / is stated] — here is exactly how I built that."
**Apply:** For any tutorial or demonstration script, open with the end state. The audience already knows where they are going — they are watching for the path. Removes the "is this worth my time" decision.

### Pattern 11: Specific Number as Hook (Neil Patel + Kieran Gilmurray)
Open with a concrete, specific statistic before making any argument.
**Format:** "[Specific %] of [audience] are [doing/not doing X]. Here is why that is a problem."
**Examples for this user's audience:**
- "Around 95% of AI projects never make a business a penny. Here's what the few that work do differently."
- "Most solopreneurs lose hours every week to work AI could do in seconds. Here's the first task to hand off."
**Apply:** When the script topic has supporting data, lead with the number — and verify the stat is current before posting. The stat earns the explanation.

### Pattern 12: Named Framework as Content Asset (Paul Roetzer + Brooke Wright)
Every methodology or approach gets a name. Named frameworks become searchable, citeable, and self-distributing.
**Apply:** When introducing a repeatable process the user uses with clients, name it before publishing content about it. The name does distribution work on its own over time. References across posts build the framework into a brand asset.

### Pattern 14: Contrarian Operational Hook (Chris Donnelly)
Open with a direct statement that contradicts conventional wisdom, using operational language — not inspirational language.
**Format:** "[What most people believe] is wrong. Here is what actually works: [operational reframe]."
**Internal test before publishing:** "Is that good enough to stop someone mid-scroll?"
**Examples for this user's audience:**
- "AI doesn't fix chaos. It scales it."
- "You don't have a tool problem. You have a too-many-tools problem."
- "Using AI saves you a minute. Building with it gives you your week back."
**Operational language rule:** Replace aspirational words with operational ones. Not "transform your business with AI" — "here's the exact 10-minute setup that turns one idea into a week of content."

### Pattern 15: Fixed Content Pillars + Topic Authority (Chris Donnelly)
Define six (or fewer) content pillars and never deviate. Every piece of content belongs to one pillar. Repetition of pillars trains both the algorithm and the audience to associate you with specific expertise.
**Apply:** When planning content for the user, assign every piece to one of her pillars before writing it. If a topic does not fit a pillar, either assign it to the closest one or question whether it should be published at all.
**Repost ratio as the real metric:** Measure reposts/shares, not likes. Likes stay inside your existing network. Reposts reach new audiences. Content with high repost ratio is content that is crossing network boundaries — the growth signal.

### Pattern 13: Before/After Contrast (Adam Digital + Angelica Automates)
Show the old way, then show the AI way. The contrast IS the argument.
**Format:** "This used to take [time/effort]. Now it takes [less time/effort]. Here is what changed."
**Apply:** Use for any content that shows an automation in practice — show your manual workflow before, then the automated version after. The contrast makes the time saved concrete without needing a spreadsheet.

## Script Application Rules

When writing a script for this user, apply these rules in order:

1. **Open with a provocation or fear** — never start with "hey," context-setting, or a title restatement. First sentence = the hook.

2. **Pick a pattern** — identify which pattern from the playbook fits the topic. State it internally before writing.

3. **Reference the positioning** — every script is anchored to: everyday entrepreneurs, AI + automation to win back their time, and exactly one of the six pillars. If a script drifts from this, redirect.

4. **Check the platform** — adapt opening and pacing for the platform this video is for. TikTok ≠ LinkedIn ≠ YouTube.

5. **End with a comment trigger or clear next step** — not a vague "follow for more." A specific, earned CTA.

6. **Voice check** — casual and conversational (contractions welcome), warm with a provocative edge, authority + relatability. No corporate jargon. Read aloud before delivering.

7. **Series check** — is this a standalone or part of a series? If part of a series, reference the series name and episode number in the script.
