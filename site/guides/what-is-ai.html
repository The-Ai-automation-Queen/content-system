What AI actually is.

What is AI, in plain English? AI is software that studies huge amounts of examples and learns patterns, not a robot that thinks like a human. Everyone says "AI." Nobody says what it is. You are not behind, because nobody bothered to explain it simply. So it is time to fix that right now, no sci-fi, no jargon.

01

Forget the sci-fi version

First course · what AI is not

When most people hear "AI," they picture robots, Terminator, or some kind of digital brain that thinks like a human. That is not what we are talking about. The AI you are hearing about every day is software. It runs on computers. It does not think, feel, or have opinions. It is a very powerful tool, like a calculator, but for language.

(And yes, even the best chef makes mistakes. The allergen card comes right before you eat: course four.)

Here is the simplest way to put it: what follows is the one idea that makes everything else about AI make sense.

02

Traditional software vs. AI: how it actually learns

Second course · the one idea that explains everything

Imagine you hire a new assistant. You have two options for how you train them.

That is genuinely the core idea. Traditional software follows instructions a human typed out. AI learned the instructions on its own by studying examples.

Two ways to train an assistant
Option A · the manual

Rule by rule

You write a 500-page manual for every situation: "if a client emails about pricing, reply with Template #7." A human writes every single rule. If a case is not in the manual, the software is stuck.

Option B · the examples

Pattern by pattern

You show thousands of examples of great work: great emails, great replies, great reports. Nobody wrote the rules. The software found the patterns by studying a massive amount of examples.

AI is not a genius. It is a very fast learner that studied a lot of homework.

03

What it can actually do for you today

Third course · generative AI, in practice

There are different types of AI. The one everyone is talking about is called generative AI. Generative means it creates new things: writes new text, creates new images, generates new audio, makes new video. It does not just sort data or recognize faces. It produces work that did not exist before.

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Midjourney, ElevenLabs: different brands, same basic idea. They all studied enormous amounts of human-created content and now they can create new content that looks and sounds like the real thing. When someone says "AI," nine times out of ten, this is what they mean.

Fourth course · what it cannot do (yet)

Know before you eat.

AI is powerful, but it has real limits. Knowing them will save you from trusting it with the wrong things.

Makes things up

It can confidently say something that is completely false. It is not lying on purpose, it is generating what sounds right based on patterns. Always double-check facts, numbers, and anything legal or financial.

Does not know your business

It has no idea who your clients are, what you sell, or what happened in last Tuesday's meeting, unless you tell it. You have to give it that context every time.

Does not decide for you

It gives you options, drafts, and analysis. The judgment call is still yours: think of it as a brilliant intern who works fast but needs you to approve everything.

Does not replace expertise

A doctor, a lawyer, an accountant, it can help you prepare, research, and draft, but it should not be the final word on anything where being wrong has real consequences.

05

Try it right now, and why it matters

Last course · 3 prompts, one takeaway

Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, any of them, they all work for this. Copy one of the prompts on the right and paste it in. You will get back a real, usable answer in about 10 seconds. That is the moment it clicks. Not the theory. Not the definitions. The moment you see it do something useful for your situation.

If you are running a business, starting one, or thinking about leaving corporate to do your own thing, AI changes the math. Tasks that used to require hiring someone or spending your evening on them can now get done in minutes: first drafts of content, research on a new market, replies to repetitive client questions, summaries of competitor strategies.

This does not mean you replace people. It means you get hours back, hours you can spend on the parts of your business that actually need you, or on the life you are building outside of it. That is the real point. Not the technology itself. The time it gives you back.

Next on the map: the names you keep hearing: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, LLM, model, agent. They are not all the same thing, and sorting them out takes about 5 minutes.

Try this

"I am a solopreneur who sells online courses. Write me a short, friendly email to send to someone who signed up for my free workshop but did not attend. Keep it under 100 words."

Or this

"I am comparing two project management tools for my small team: Notion and ClickUp. Give me 5 things to consider when choosing between them, written for someone who is not technical."

Or this

"I just got a long email from a potential client. Here it is: [paste the email]. Summarize what they are actually asking for in 3 bullet points."
