The basics

What AI actually is

It is pattern-matching at enormous scale. Once you see that, most of the hype stops working on you.

  • Best forAnyone who nods along in meetings and would rather not.
  • Careful withAssuming it understands anything. It does not.
  • What I would doRead this once, then never feel behind in an AI conversation again.

Forget the version you have seen in films

The thing being sold to you is not a mind. It does not know things, want things or understand your business. Once you have that clear, the rest of this gets much simpler, and a lot of what you are being told starts to sound different.

What it actually does: it has read an enormous quantity of text, and from that it learned which words tend to follow which other words in which situations. When you ask it something, it works out, one piece at a time, what should come next. That is genuinely most of it.

That sounds like it should produce nonsense. It does not, because at sufficient scale, predicting the next word well requires something that behaves a great deal like reasoning. To finish the sentence "the contract is void because the counterparty failed to" you need to have absorbed something about how contracts work. The capability is real. The understanding is not.

How it differs from ordinary software

Ordinary software is a set of instructions somebody wrote. If a spreadsheet adds a column wrongly, a person made a mistake in the rules, and you can find the line and fix it. It does the same thing every time, which is exactly what you want from a spreadsheet.

AI was not given rules. It was shown examples until it worked out the patterns itself. Nobody wrote the instruction for how to summarise a contract. It inferred that from having seen a great many contracts and summaries.

Three consequences follow, and they matter more than anything else on this page.

  • It is not consistent. Ask the same question twice and you can get two different answers. Both may be fine. That is not a fault, it is how the thing works, and it means you should never use it where the output must be identical every time.
  • Nobody can fully explain a specific answer. Not you, not the company that built it. There is no line of code to point at.
  • It will be confidently wrong. Because it produces what is plausible rather than what is true, and those two things usually overlap, right up until they do not.

Do this

Next time somebody pitches you an AI product, ask one question: what happens when it gets this wrong? If they have a good answer, keep listening. If the question surprises them, you have learned what you needed to know.

It is not thinking. It is predicting. At a scale that looks a great deal like thinking.

What it can genuinely do for you today

Stripped of the marketing, the useful list is shorter than you have been told and more valuable than you think.

  • Turn a blank page into a first draft. The single biggest time saving available to most people.
  • Read something long and tell you what is in it. Contracts, reports, forty-message email threads.
  • Change the shape of information. Notes into an email, a transcript into an action list, a spreadsheet into an explanation.
  • Answer the same question a hundred times. The work that is not hard, just relentless.
  • Explain something you do not understand, at whatever level you need. Underrated, and free.

Notice what is not on that list. It cannot decide anything for you. It cannot be accountable. It cannot know something that is true only inside your business unless you tell it.

What to check before you trust an answer

These are not edge cases. They are the ordinary failure modes, and knowing their names means you will spot them.

What it is calledWhat actually happensWhat you do
HallucinationIt states something false as fact, with no hedge.Open the source. Check numbers, dates and quotes yourself.
ConfabulationIt fills a gap with a plausible story rather than saying it does not know.Ask what it is unsure about before you ask for the answer.
SycophancyIt agrees with the way you framed the question.Ask for the strongest argument against your position.
Prompt injectionA document or webpage contains text designed to redirect it.Treat material you did not write as untrusted.
BiasPatterns in the training data produce uneven treatment.Ask who this answer works for and who it leaves out.

Try it in the next ten minutes

Reading about this is worth about a tenth of using it. So do this today, not this week.

Open any free AI tool. Paste in the longest email thread in your inbox. Ask: what was decided here, what is still open, and what needs me to do something? Read what comes back.

Two things will happen. You will get a genuinely useful answer in about fifteen seconds. And you will spot at least one thing it got slightly wrong. Both of those are the lesson. That is the whole relationship in one exercise: fast, useful, and it needs you.
