Heard “prompt” 50 times. Still no idea what it is?

Every AI article tells you to write better prompts. Nobody tells you what a prompt is. It’s the thing you type. Here’s what makes one actually work.

The full definition

A prompt is what you write to the AI. The AI reads it and writes back. Every message you send is a new prompt. Every reply you get is the response.

That’s all.

“Prompt engineering” is just writing it well. There’s no secret vocabulary. No magic word that unlocks a hidden mode. There are 4 things to do well. When you do all 4, you get useful answers every time.

The 4 things a good prompt does

1. Role

Tell the AI who it’s being.

“You are a small business accountant.”

2. Context

Give it the situation.

“I run a 4-person agency, monthly revenue around $30K, no in-house bookkeeping.”

3. Task

Say exactly what you want.

“Draft 5 questions I should ask before hiring a bookkeeper.”

4. Format

Say how you want it back.

“Numbered list. One sentence each.”

That’s the entire skill.

Bad vs good

Bad prompt

“Help me hire a bookkeeper.”

5 words. Vague, generic output.

Good prompt

“You are a small business accountant. I run a 4-person agency, monthly revenue around $30K, no in-house bookkeeping. Draft 5 questions I should ask before hiring a bookkeeper. Numbered list. One sentence each.”

50 words. Specific, usable output.

The difference isn’t engineering. It’s specificity.

What this buys your business

AI pattern-matches the shape of your request. Vague in, vague out. Role + context + task + format = specific, usable answer.

Run 20 prompts using this template. That’s the complete skill. Everything else is refinement.

The next level is chaining prompts so the AI builds a whole workflow. The same job runs on a schedule, without you starting it each time. That’s where the real hours come back.
