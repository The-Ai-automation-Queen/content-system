01

What it is: just what you type

First course · 1 min

A prompt is just what you type. That is it. When you open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini and type something into the message box, that message is your prompt. When the AI replies, that is the response.

Every time you hit send, you are prompting. You have been doing it since the first time you asked AI anything. You just did not know the word.

So why do people talk about "prompt engineering" like it is a skill? Because how you write that message changes everything about what you get back. (And yes, even the best chef makes mistakes. The allergen card comes right before you eat, in course four.)

The quality of your answer is almost entirely determined by the quality of your question. That is the whole game.

02

The Formula: four things, every time

Second course · the floor plan

There is no secret language. No magic words. But there are 4 things that, when you include them, consistently turn vague AI answers into ones you can actually use: role, context, task, and format.

Same chef, different order every time. The skill is knowing what to put on the ticket. That is what the floor plan below is for.

The order ticket
Ingredient 1

Role

Tell the AI who it should be. This shapes the tone, the vocabulary, and the type of advice it gives. Without a role, you get a generic answer. With one, you get an answer from the right perspective.

"You are an experienced social media manager for small businesses."
Ingredient 2

Context

Give it your situation. AI does not know anything about you, your business, or your problems unless you tell it. The more relevant detail you share, the more tailored the answer will be.

"I run a small online store selling handmade candles. About 2,000 Instagram followers, posting 3 times a week. Engagement has dropped this month."
Ingredient 3

Task

Say exactly what you want it to do. Not "help me with social media", that is too broad. Be specific about the deliverable.

"Give me 5 Instagram post ideas that could increase engagement this week, each with a caption and content format."
Ingredient 4

Format

Tell it how you want the answer structured. Without this, AI tends to write long paragraphs. With it, you get something you can use immediately.

"Numbered list, format in bold, caption below it, each under 50 words."

Role + context + task + format. Four pieces. Works every time, for anything.

03

See It Work: three dishes, side by side

Third course · before & after, then three more orders

Same AI. Same tool. Same subscription. Here is the exact same request, once without the formula and once with it.

Without the formula

"Help me with my Instagram."

You get: a generic 500-word essay about Instagram best practices. Nothing specific to you. Nothing you can post today.

With the formula

Role + context + task + format, all in one message

"You are an experienced social media manager for small businesses. I run a small online store selling handmade candles, 2,000 followers, engagement dropped. Give me 5 Instagram post ideas to increase engagement this week, each with a caption and content format. Numbered list, captions under 50 words."

You get: 5 specific ideas you can use today. Tailored to your product. Ready to adapt and post.

Starter

Writing a client proposal

Role: "You are a freelance consultant who writes clear, professional proposals." Context: "A potential client wants a website redesign, a local bakery, ~50 visitors/day, budget ~$3,000." Task: "Draft a one-page proposal I can send today." Format: "Scope, timeline, price, next steps. Under 400 words. Professional but warm."

Main

Preparing for a meeting

Role: "You are a business strategy advisor." Context: "Meeting a potential partner, a marketing agency wanting to co-create an AI workshop. First partnership like this."Task: "8 questions to evaluate whether this partnership is worth pursuing." Format: "Numbered list, one sentence each, grouped by fit, logistics, money."

Chef's special

Handling a difficult customer email

Role: "You are a customer service expert who specializes in de-escalation." Context: "Customer upset their order arrived late, wants a refund. We can offer 20% off next order, not a full refund." Task: "A reply that acknowledges frustration, apologizes, offers the discount, without sounding corporate." Format: "Under 150 words, friendly and genuine, no bullet points."

See the pattern? Role + context + task + format, every order, every time.

Before you hit send: did you tell the AI who to be, what your situation is, exactly what you want, and how to format it?

Fourth course · the two mistakes everyone makes

Know before you eat.

Being too vague

"Help me with marketing." "Write me something for my business." "Give me some ideas." These are so broad the AI has to guess everything. It guesses wrong, you get useless answers, and you conclude "AI does not work." It works fine. The prompt did not.

Not iterating

Your first prompt rarely gives you the perfect answer, and that is okay. The magic is in the follow-up: "Make option 3 shorter." "Sound less formal." "Add a line about free shipping." Think of it as a conversation, not a one-shot command. You refine until it is right.

05

The Bill: why this is worth learning once

Last course · 1 min

Every task you use AI for starts with a prompt. Email drafts, content ideas, research, customer replies, strategy brainstorms. The better you get at writing prompts, the more useful AI becomes for everything you do.

And here is the thing most people miss: a good prompt saves you time every single time you use it. Write a great prompt for client proposals once, save it somewhere, and reuse it with different context every time. That one prompt might save you an hour a week, every week, forever. That is not a small thing. That is your time back.

You now have the formula. Role, context, task, format. Try it 5 times today with real work you are already doing, and you will feel the difference immediately. Once this clicks, everything else in AI gets easier, because every tool, every feature, every workflow starts with you telling the AI what you need. And now you know how.
