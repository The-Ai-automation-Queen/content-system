Tool verdict / Microsoft

Copilot

The one that lives inside Word, Excel and Outlook. Buy it for where it sits, not for how clever it is.

  • Best forTeams already paying for Microsoft 365, especially in Excel and Outlook.
  • Careful withThe price. Thirty dollars per person per month adds up fast on a small team.
  • What I would doTrial it on the five people who live in Excel and Outlook. Not the whole company.

Where it earns its keep

I spent a long stretch of my career inside Microsoft, so let me be direct about this one. Copilot is not the cleverest assistant available. It is the one that is already sitting in the corner of the document your team is working in, and for most companies that beats clever.

The two places it genuinely pays for itself are Excel and Outlook. In Excel it will write the formula you cannot remember, explain the one the previous person left behind, and tell you what a sheet of numbers is actually saying. In Outlook it will summarise a thread of forty replies into the three lines you needed, and draft the response.

In Teams it takes meeting notes, which sounds small and is not. The person who takes notes stops participating properly. Getting them back into the conversation is a real gain, and it is the thing people notice first.

Underneath it all is Microsoft Graph, which is the connective layer holding your company's mail, files, calendar and org chart. That is why Copilot can answer "what did we agree with this client last quarter" without you finding the file first. It is also why the permissions question below matters so much.

Where it'll burn you

Start with the price, because it is the thing people underestimate. Thirty dollars per user per month is three hundred and sixty a year, per person. For a team of ten that is three and a half thousand dollars annually, on top of what you already pay for Microsoft 365. That is a real budget line and it deserves a real business case.

The second thing is that it inherits your permissions, which is exactly what it should do and also exactly where organisations get caught out. If your SharePoint has been quietly over-shared for six years, and most companies' has, Copilot makes that visible in a hurry. Someone will ask an innocent question and get an answer from a file they were never meant to see. The tool did nothing wrong. It just surfaced a problem that was already there.

And the quality in the Office apps is uneven. Excel and Outlook are strong. PowerPoint generation is mediocre and you will rewrite most of it. Word drafting is fine but no better than what you would get from ChatGPT for a quarter of the price.

Do this

Before you roll it out to anyone, run a permissions review on SharePoint and OneDrive. Ask your IT person for a report on what is shared with "everyone in the organisation". Fix that first. Copilot after, not before.

It does not create your data problems. It just makes them findable.

What I'd actually do

Do not buy it for the whole company on day one. Buy five licences and give them to the five people who spend most of their day in Excel and Outlook. Those are the roles where it pays back fastest and where you will get an honest answer within a month.

Then measure something. Hours on a recurring report, time to clear the inbox, however long the monthly numbers usually take. If those move, expand. If nobody can point to a change after six weeks, you have your answer and you have only spent a hundred and fifty dollars a month finding out.

The words you will keep hearing

  • Microsoft Graph. The layer that knows your company's mail, files, calendar and who reports to whom. The reason Copilot can answer questions about your own business.
  • Grounding. Checking an answer against your real company data before giving it to you, rather than making something up.
  • Copilot Studio. Where you build a custom agent for a specific job without writing code. Genuinely useful once someone owns it.
  • Declarative agents. A saved assistant with fixed instructions and a fixed set of sources. Same idea as a Custom GPT or a Gem.
  • Purview. Microsoft's data protection layer. If you are in a regulated industry, this is the reason to choose Copilot over the alternatives.

Is it the right one for you?

Yes, if your company runs on Microsoft 365, if a meaningful share of your people live in Excel and Outlook, and if you have the budget for a per-seat licence that is more expensive than every alternative on this site.

No, if you are a small team on a tight budget. At thirty dollars a head you can put ChatGPT Plus in front of the same people for twenty and get a more capable assistant, and you lose the Office integration. For many small businesses that is the correct trade.
