01

The word: what it actually means

First course · 1 min

Agentic AI. AI agents. Agentic workflows. The word showed up everywhere in 2025, and nobody stopped to explain it. It is simpler than it sounds. And once you understand it, you will see why every tech company in the world is racing to build it.

Agentic means the AI does not just answer your question. It goes and does the thing.

A regular chatbot waits for you to ask something, gives you a response, and stops. An agentic system takes your goal, breaks it into steps on its own, figures out which tools to use, runs those tools, checks whether it worked, and keeps going until the job is actually done.

The difference is not intelligence. It is autonomy: how much you let the AI do on its own before checking back in with you.

02

Think of it like delegation

Second course · a comparison

You already understand this concept. You use it every day. You just have not heard it described with the word “agentic” before.

Short leash

You review every step

You tell your assistant: “Draft a reply to this email. Show me before you send it.”

They write it. You review it. You hit send. They did one task and waited for your next instruction.

Long leash

It runs the whole job

You tell your assistant: “Handle my inbox this morning. Respond to anything routine. Flag anything that needs my attention. Reschedule the 2pm if there is a conflict.”

They read, decide, reply, schedule, flag. They come back with a summary when they are done. You did not approve each step.

The first is a chatbot. The second is agentic. Same assistant. Different level of trust, different level of independence.

The question with agentic AI is never “can it do this?” It is always “how much leash should I give it for this task?”

03

The four levels of AI autonomy

Third course · the whole spectrum

Not everything called “agentic” works the same way. There is a spectrum, and it helps to think of it as four levels. Most of what you use today is Level 1 or 2. The headlines are about Level 4.

Level 1

Chat

You ask, it answers. No memory between sessions, no tools, no actions. You type a question, get text back, and do everything else yourself.

Level 2

Chat + tools

Same conversation, but it can reach outside the chat window: search the web, read a file, run a calculation. You still drive; it can just do more per turn.

Level 3

Workflow

A fixed sequence that runs the same way every time. You define what happens; the AI executes it: step 1, then 2, then 3. Predictable and repeatable.

Level 4

Agentic

The AI decides the steps itself. You give it a goal and access to tools. It plans, executes, evaluates whether it worked, and adjusts. If one approach fails, it tries another. If it needs information it does not have, it goes and gets it. You set the destination, and it drives.

Level 1 · real example

The thank-you email

You type “Write a thank-you email for a client who just signed.” You get the email, paste it into Gmail, tweak a few words, hit send. The AI did one thing. You did everything else.

Level 2 · real example

The 30-page report

You upload a consulting report and ask “What are the 3 biggest risks mentioned?” It reads the file, finds the risks, writes a summary. You never had to read the whole thing.

Level 3 · real example

The Monday report

Every Monday, the system pulls last week's social stats, writes a summary, drops it in a shared doc, and messages the team a link. Nobody pressed a button. It just runs.

Level 4 · real example

The competitor scan

You say “Research 5 competitors, find what they posted this week, compare it to our plan, flag gaps.” It decides which competitors and platforms to check, what counts as a gap, and returns a report. You never defined the steps.

Levels 1 and 2 save you time on individual tasks. Levels 3 and 4 save you time on entire processes. That is the difference.

04

Why this word is everywhere, and how to hear it in the wild

Fourth course · 3 min

Every major AI company shipped agentic features in 2025. They all used the same word, and they all meant roughly the same thing: AI that does not just talk, but takes action in the real world.

The shift is simple. AI is moving from “answer my question” to “do my task.” That changes three things for you.

What you buy

Compare the level, not the price

A tool that only answers questions is not the same as one that completes tasks. A Level 2 tool at $20/month might beat a Level 4 tool at $200/month if all you need is chat with tools.

What you automate first

Boring before clever

Start with tasks that follow predictable patterns (Level 3 workflows). Move to agentic (Level 4) only when the steps change every time and need judgment.

How much you trust it

More autonomy, more risk

An agentic system that sends emails on your behalf could send a wrong one. One that books meetings could book the wrong time. Start with a short leash; extend it as it earns trust.

The one question that separates hype from reality: when someone pitches you an “AI agent,” ask: “Does it decide the steps, or do I?”

If you define the steps, it is a workflow. If the AI decides the steps, it is agentic. Both are useful. But they solve different problems, cost different amounts, and fail in different ways. Now you know which one you are looking at.

Hear it in a real conversation

A colleague says: “We are building an agentic pipeline that handles inbound leads end to end.”

What they actually mean:

  • A potential customer reaches out (through a form, a DM, an email)
  • The AI reads the message, decides if this person is a real prospect or not, writes a personalized reply, and logs the interaction in their CRM
  • If the prospect asks a question the AI cannot answer, it flags it for a human
  • Nobody wrote a specific reply for every possible scenario, the AI figures out the right response from context

That is Level 4. The AI is making decisions, not following a script.

You can follow that sentence now. And you could ask: “What happens when it gets the qualification wrong? Does it lose leads you should have followed up on?” That is the question that separates people who understand agentic from people who just repeat the word.

Read this before you trust it

Know before you delegate.

Autonomy risk

More independence means more room for mistakes. An agentic system that sends emails on your behalf could send the wrong one. Start with a short leash.

Wrong tool for the job

Using Level 4 for a task that just needed a fixed workflow wastes time and money. Match the level to the job, not the hype.

Silent failure

An agentic system that misjudges a step (like a wrongly-qualified lead) may not tell you. Ask what happens when it gets it wrong.

05

What this means for your business

Last course · 1 min

You do not need Level 4 for everything. Most businesses should start at Level 2 or 3 — chat with tools, or fixed workflows. They are cheaper, more predictable, and easier to trust.

Agentic matters when the task is too variable for a fixed workflow — when the steps change depending on what the AI finds, when you need judgment, not just execution.

The point is not to hand your business over to AI. The point is to stop spending your time on tasks that do not need your brain. The more of those you delegate, the more hours you get back for the work that actually grows your business.

Step 1

Start with chat + tools

Use AI to help you write, research, summarize, and brainstorm (Level 2). Get comfortable with how it thinks.

Step 2

Automate one repetitive process

Pick the most boring, predictable task in your week. Build a workflow that handles it (Level 3). Get your time back.

Step 3

Try agentic on one task

Pick something where the steps change every time. Give the AI a goal and some tools (Level 4). Watch it closely. Expand the leash when it earns your trust.
