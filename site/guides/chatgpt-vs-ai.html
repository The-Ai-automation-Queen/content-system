ChatGPT vs AI — 6 words that always get mixed up.

Most people use “ChatGPT” and “AI” like they mean the same thing. They don’t. Mixing them up is like calling every car a Toyota. Here are the six words that keep tripping people up — sorted out for good.

01 · The Car Analogy

Think of it like a car

Before we get into the tech names, here’s an analogy that makes the whole thing click — and yes, even the best explainer makes mistakes now and then. If any of the six terms below still feel fuzzy after this, that’s normal. Come back to this page any time.

Part 1 of 3

The Engine

“The invisible thing that does the work.” It’s the technology doing the thinking. Powerful, but you never touch it directly.

Part 2 of 3

The Car

“What you actually sit in and drive.” It’s built around the engine, with a dashboard, a steering wheel, and seats — the part you interact with.

Part 3 of 3

The Brand

“Toyota, BMW, Tesla.” Who made it. Same concept — a car with an engine — different makers, different feel.

Now replace those words:

  • The engine = the AI model (the technology doing the thinking)
  • The car = the chatbot or app (what you type into)
  • The brand = the company that made it (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google)

That’s the entire structure. Everything below is just putting the specific names in the right box.

02 · The Six Words

The names, sorted from biggest to smallest

Six words. Once you know where each one sits, you’ll never confuse them again.

Open the full station menu · 6 items
Word 1 · The Category

AI — Artificial Intelligence

“Any software that learns instead of following handwritten rules.” The broadest term there is. Your spam filter, Netflix recommendations, and phone face-unlock are all AI. On its own, the word tells you almost nothing.

Word 2 · The Type

Generative AI

“The kind that creates, not just sorts.” Unlike older AI that classifies or recommends, generative AI makes new text, images, audio, and video. This is the kind in every headline right now.

Word 3 · The Engine

LLM — Large Language Model

“The engine from our car analogy.” “Large” because it studied enormous amounts of writing. “Language” because text is its specialty. Examples: GPT-4o, Claude Opus, Gemini Pro.

Word 4 · The Car

Chatbot / App

“The thing you actually open and type into.” Built around an LLM, plus the interface and design. ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are all chatbots — each with an LLM of the same name inside.

Word 5 · The Maker

Brand / Company

“Who built the car.” OpenAI makes ChatGPT. Anthropic makes Claude. Google makes Gemini. Same concept, different makers — which is exactly why the names get tangled.

Word 6 · The Doer

Agent

“Hiring the friend, not just texting them.” A chatbot that takes action — searches the web, reads files, fills forms, runs code. A regular chatbot gives advice; an agent goes and does it.

AI is the category. Generative AI is the type. LLM is the engine. ChatGPT is one car, made by one company, using one engine. Now you know.

Yes, sometimes the car and the engine share a name — Claude the chatbot runs on an LLM also called Claude. That overlap is a big part of why this gets confusing. But now you see the difference underneath it.

03 · Hear It In A Meeting

Hear it in a real conversation

Someone at a meeting says: “We tested the new Claude model and it’s way better for long documents. Let’s switch our team’s chatbot over.”

Starter

“Claude model”

= the engine. The LLM made by Anthropic.

Main

“Better for long documents”

= this engine can handle more text at once than the one they were using.

Chef's Special

“Switch our chatbot over”

= swap the engine inside the app their team already uses — the app itself doesn’t change.

You follow that now. And you could ask: “How long is ‘long’? Are we talking 10-page reports or 200-page contracts?” That’s a question that shows you understand the difference. It moves the conversation forward instead of nodding through it.

04 · Where People Get Burned

Before you nod along in the next meeting

The confusion between these words isn’t just academic. It costs people money and credibility. Here’s where it actually bites.

Paying twice for the same engine

“Someone pitches you an ‘AI tool’ for $99/month.” If it’s just a thin wrapper around ChatGPT or Claude, you might be paying a markup for something with a free tier underneath.

Comparing the wrong things

“ChatGPT vs. AI” isn’t a real comparison. “AI” isn’t a competitor to ChatGPT — it’s the category ChatGPT belongs to. Compare “ChatGPT vs. Claude for writing proposals” instead.

Assuming the car and engine match

“Claude the app” and “Claude the model” share a name but aren’t identical. An app can quietly swap its engine. Ask which model version you’re actually getting before you rely on it for something important.

Trusting an “agent” that only chats

“It says agent, but does it actually take action?” Plenty of products borrow the word for marketing. A real agent does things in the world — not just talks about them.

05 · Why It Pays Off

Why this matters for you

When you know the names, three things change — and none of them cost you anything.

Change 1

You stop overpaying

Someone pitches an “AI tool” subscription. You now ask: which model does it use? ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini all have free tiers — maybe you don’t need the fancy wrapper at all.

Change 2

You compare properly

Instead of “ChatGPT vs. AI,” you start asking “ChatGPT vs. Claude for writing client proposals.” Specific. Useful. Answerable.

Change 3

You stop feeling lost

Half the confusion around AI is just vocabulary. You just cleared most of it in six minutes — and that quiet confidence compounds. Every guide from here builds on this foundation.
