The basics

ChatGPT and AI aren't the same thing

Six words get used as if they mean one thing. They don't, and the difference costs money.

  • Best forFollowing a sales pitch and knowing which bit is real.
  • Careful withBuying a model when what you needed was a workflow.
  • What I would doLearn these six words. They're the difference between buying well and buying twice.

Think of it like a car

This is the analogy that makes it stick, so I'll use it once and then get out of the way.

AI is transport: the whole category. Machine learning is the internal combustion engine: the method that made modern transport possible. A large language model is a particular engine design. ChatGPT is a specific car you can buy, with that engine in it. An agent is a car that drives itself somewhere. Automation is the traffic light system, which involves no engine at all and has been working since long before any of this.

Now, when someone tells you their product has AI in it, you can ask the useful question: which part?

The six words, from biggest to smallest

WordWhat it actually meansWhere you meet it
AIThe whole field. Any software doing something that used to need a person. So broad it tells you almost nothing on its own.Every pitch deck since 2023.
Machine learningSoftware that learned patterns from examples instead of being given rules. The method underneath most of this.Fraud detection, recommendations, forecasting.
Large language modelA machine learning system trained on enormous amounts of text, which predicts what comes next. The engine in the tools you use.Underneath ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and the rest.
ChatGPTOne product, from one company, built on top of a model. A brand name, not a technology.The thing everybody means when they say AI.
AgentSoftware that takes multi-step actions towards a goal instead of answering and stopping.The current favourite word on pricing pages.
AutomationIf this happens, do that. Fixed rules, no learning, completely predictable.Most of what a small business actually needs.

That last row is the one that saves people the most money, and it's the one nobody puts in a pitch. A great deal of what gets sold as AI is a rules-based workflow with a chat box bolted on the front. Rules-based workflows are excellent. They're also cheap, predictable and have existed for twenty years.

Do this

When something is pitched to you as AI, ask: is the clever part learning from data, or is it a set of rules somebody wrote? Both are fine. Only one of them justifies the price.

Half of what gets sold as AI is automation with better marketing. Automation is cheaper and more reliable.

Hearing it in a real conversation

Here's the kind of sentence you'll meet this month, translated.

What they say: "Our AI-powered platform uses advanced agentic workflows to automate your customer journey."

What that could mean: There's a language model somewhere in the product. It takes a few steps rather than one. And there's a rules-based sequence sending emails on a schedule, which is the automation part and probably the part that actually does the work.

None of that's dishonest. It's just imprecise in a direction that happens to favour the seller. Three questions cut through it:

  • Which model is underneath, and can I choose?
  • Which parts are fixed rules and which parts genuinely decide something?
  • What happens when it gets something wrong, and how would I find out?

Why this matters for what you buy

Because the three things have completely different costs, risks and failure modes, and confusing them is how a small business ends up paying subscription prices for something a rule could have done.

Automation is cheap, predictable and breaks loudly, which is good. A model is flexible, sometimes wrong, and breaks quietly. An agent is both, plus it acts while you're not watching.

The right answer for most small businesses is more automation than they have and less AI than they're being sold. Work out which of your problems is genuinely a judgement problem. Those need a model. Everything else is a rule you have not written down yet.
