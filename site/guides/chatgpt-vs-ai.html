ChatGPT and AI are not the same thing.

Most people use “ChatGPT” and “AI” like they mean the same thing. They don’t. And mixing them up is like calling every car a Toyota. Let’s sort out the names so you never get confused in that conversation again.

Think of it like a car

Before we get into the tech names, here’s an analogy that makes the whole thing click.

The car analogy

The engine is the technology that does the work. It’s powerful but invisible. You never interact with the engine directly.

The car is what you actually sit in and drive. It’s built around the engine. It has a dashboard, a steering wheel, and seats.

The brand is who made it. Toyota, BMW, Tesla. Same concept (a car with an engine), different makers, different feels.

Now replace those words:

  • The engine = the AI model (the technology doing the thinking)
  • The car = the chatbot or app (what you type into)
  • The brand = the company that made it (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google)

That’s the entire structure. Everything below is just putting the specific names in the right box.

The names, sorted from biggest to smallest

1

AI (Artificial Intelligence)

The broadest term. It covers all software that learns from examples instead of following handwritten rules. Your email spam filter is AI. Netflix recommending shows is AI. The face unlock on your phone is AI. It’s a huge category.

When someone says “AI,” they could mean almost anything. On its own, the word tells you very little.

2

Generative AI

The specific type of AI in every headline right now. Unlike older AI that sorts, classifies, or recommends, generative AI creates new things. New text, new images, new audio, new video.

When your friend says “I used AI to write my bio,” they mean generative AI. This is the kind that matters for your daily work.

3

LLM (Large Language Model)

This is the engine from our car analogy. It’s the technology that powers the text-based AI tools. “Large” because it studied an enormous amount of writing to learn how language works. “Language” because text is what it’s best at. “Model” because it’s one specific trained piece of software.

Examples of LLMs: GPT-4o (made by OpenAI), Claude Opus (made by Anthropic), Gemini Pro (made by Google). You’ll probably never need to pick one yourself. But when someone drops “LLM” in a meeting, you now know they’re talking about the engine, not the car.

4

Chatbot / App

This is the car. The thing you actually open, type into, and use. It’s built around an LLM, but it adds the interface, the features, the design, and the experience.

ChatGPT is a chatbot made by OpenAI. The engine inside it is an LLM called GPT.

Claude is a chatbot made by Anthropic. The engine inside it is an LLM also called Claude.

Gemini is a chatbot made by Google. The engine inside it is an LLM also called Gemini.

Yes, sometimes the car and the engine share a name. That’s part of why it’s confusing. But now you see the difference.

5

Agent

A chatbot that doesn’t just talk. It takes action. It can search the web, read your files, send messages, fill in forms, or run code. It does things in the real world, not just in a chat window.

Think of it this way: a regular chatbot is like texting a smart friend for advice. An agent is like hiring that friend for the afternoon. They don’t just tell you what to do. They go and do it.

This word is going to keep coming up. There’s a whole guide on it later in this series.

AI is the category. Generative AI is the type. LLM is the engine. ChatGPT is one car, made by one company, using one engine. Now you know.

Hear it in a real conversation

Someone at a meeting says: “We tested the new Claude model and it’s way better for long documents. Let’s switch our team’s chatbot over.”

What they actually mean:

  • Claude model = the engine (the LLM made by Anthropic)
  • Better for long documents = this engine handles more text at once
  • Our team’s chatbot = the app their team uses to interact with AI
  • Switch over = swap the engine inside their existing app

You follow that now. And you could ask: “How long is ‘long’? Are we talking 10-page reports or 200-page contracts?” That’s a question that shows you understand the difference. It moves the conversation forward instead of nodding through it.

Why this matters for you

When you know the names, three things change.

You stop overpaying. Someone pitches you an “AI tool” for $99/month. You now know to ask: which model does it use? Because ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini all have free tiers. Maybe you don’t need the fancy wrapper. Maybe you just need the engine directly.

You compare properly. Instead of “ChatGPT vs. AI,” you start asking “ChatGPT vs. Claude for writing client proposals.” Specific. Useful. Answerable.

You stop feeling lost in conversations. Half the confusion around AI is just vocabulary. You just cleared most of it in 5 minutes.

The next time someone says “AI” and means ChatGPT, you’ll know the difference. You don’t need to correct them. But you’ll know.
