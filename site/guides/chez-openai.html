1
Know Before You Eat
The chef is brilliant, but not perfect. Read this before you order.
Before you sit down, know this: even the best chef makes mistakes. Sometimes they confidently say "this is nut-free" when it's not, that's a hallucination. Sometimes they fill gaps with a convincing story instead of saying "I don't know", that's confabulation. They might agree with everything you say even when you're wrong, that's sycophancy. Someone could slip a fake ticket into the kitchen to hijack your order, that's prompt injection. The chef might accidentally reveal another table's secrets, that's data leakage. They carry unconscious habits from their training, that's bias. And sometimes they state things as certain when they're actually guessing, that's overconfidence. These risks apply to every AI restaurant, not just this one.
Now meet this restaurant's chef
2
The Chef & Their Brain
Before anyone walks in, the chef already exists. This is who they are.
OpenAI's chef has been through many culinary schools. The current generation is the GPT family (Generative Pre-trained Transformer, the AI engine behind ChatGPT). The context window (their countertop) determines how much they can juggle at once. Memory (their notebook) remembers you across visits. They can read photos, listen to audio, watch video, that's multi-modal. When a dish is complex, they pause and plan, that's reasoning, sometimes shown as a "thinking" toggle. You can set how creative they cook with the temperature dial. And every word, in and out, is a token, like an individual ingredient.
A customer walks in through one of these doors
3
The Front Door
There are multiple ways to enter this restaurant
You can walk into ChatGPT, the dining room, and chat face to face. Developers can call the API (Responses API), like a phone line for bulk orders. There's Agent Mode, a ghost kitchen where ChatGPT browses the web, fills forms, and completes tasks for you. For coding, Codex is the specialized kitchen for writing and reviewing code. And if you're a regular with a big project, Projects keep everything organized in one folder.
You place your order, how it's written matters
4
The Order Slip
Instructions, preferences, and shortcuts that shape every dish
Every order has layers. The system prompt is like special instructions clipped to the ticket, invisible to you but shaping the chef's approach. AGENTS.md is like house rules on the kitchen wall, standards for how agents behave on a website or codebase. Custom Instructions are like standing orders from a regular: "always keep it concise for this customer." Prompt engineering is learning to write better order tickets. And GPT Actions are like shortcut buttons, pre-configured connections to external services from inside a Custom GPT.
The order slip goes to the kitchen
5
The Kitchen
Where the real cooking happens, tools, code, and reference books
The order arrives. The chef grabs built-in tools mid-cooking, like web browsing, DALL-E for images, or the code interpreter for running Python. Deep Research is like the chef pausing to read multiple reference books before answering, thorough, slow, high quality. Canvas is a side workspace where the chef drafts and iterates on documents or code with you watching. And file search lets the chef dig through your uploaded documents like checking the pantry shelves.
For complex meals, the team kicks in
6
The Kitchen Team
Custom GPTs, workspace agents, and the tools to build your own
Workspace Agents, like a kitchen manager, handle complex team workflows: shared, scheduled, running autonomously across tools. Custom GPTs are like sous-chefs, each one specialized for a task with their own instructions and tools. The Agents SDK and AgentKit are like a kit to build your own restaurant, blueprints, tools, and a connector registry for developers. And Frontier is like the restaurant management company, an enterprise platform governing fleets of agents.
The kitchen needs ingredients from outside suppliers
7
The Supply Chain
How the kitchen connects to the outside world
The restaurant doesn't grow its own food. Connectors are like supplier trucks, Gmail, Google Drive, GitHub, Slack each deliver their goods. MCP support is arriving as a universal delivery dock, one standard door for all suppliers. Plugins (legacy) were the original snap-on attachments, now replaced by GPT Actions and connectors. And the Apps SDK lets developers build custom UIs alongside their tools.
The dish gets plated, packaged, and delivered to you
8
Plating & Delivery
How the finished dish reaches your table
The dish is cooked, but not done yet. Canvas is like a plating station where you and the chef refine the output together, editing, iterating, polishing. The finished dish might arrive via streaming, course by course as it's ready. Structured Outputs ensure the dish is presented exactly how the developer ordered, precise formatting, no surprises. Voice Mode lets you talk to the chef directly. And the ChatGPT app (desktop, mobile) brings the dining room wherever you go.
After service, the restaurant reviews and improves
9
Training & Improvement
How the chef gets better over time
After service ends, the restaurant improves. Evals are like food critics, they score outputs on a rubric to reveal what needs work. Fine-tuning is like a specialized cooking class, retrain the chef on your specific data. Distillation is like teaching an apprentice, a smaller, faster chef learns from the master's best dishes. And RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) is like customer reviews shaping the menu.
Wrapping around everything, the rules, limits, and the bill
10
Health, Safety & The Bill
The non-negotiables that wrap around the entire restaurant
This zone wraps around everything. Safety systems are like health inspectors, rules for what the kitchen can and can't serve. Guardrails are like safety rails on equipment, content filters, refusal behaviors. Rate limits are like a maximum capacity, the kitchen can only take so many orders per hour. Plans and pricing are the bill, Free, Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise each get different menus and portions. And admin controls let the restaurant owner decide who gets kitchen access.
