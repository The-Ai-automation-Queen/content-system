The Door. What you're walking into

First course · 2 min

Every restaurant tells you what it is before you sit down. DeepSeek's door says: no dress code, no cover charge, and the receipt is almost embarrassingly small. This is the kitchen that cooks coding and reasoning dishes to the same standard as the famous places across town, at roughly a hundredth of the price, and hands you the recipe if you want to cook it yourself at home. (And yes, even the best chef makes mistakes. The allergen card comes right before you eat, in course four.)

Two chefs share this kitchen. One is fast and dirt cheap for everyday orders. The other thinks longer and harder when the problem deserves it. Both speak fluent English and Chinese, and both will happily read a document a thousand pages long without losing the thread.

The Kitchen. How it actually works

Second course · the floor plan

One brain, four ways to order. You can walk into the free dining room (chat.deepseek.com or the mobile apps), call in a wholesale order for your own product (the developer API), or take the recipe home and build your own kitchen (open-source self-host).

Same chef, radically different price tags depending on the counter. The floor plan below shows where each surface sits.

The floor plan

The Chef & Their Brain

Before anyone walks in, the chef already exists. Two chefs share the kitchen. V4 Flash is the everyday chef: fast and incredibly cheap. V4 Pro handles harder problems with stronger reasoning. Both support a thinking toggle (slow and careful, versus fast and direct), and both work off a context window reaching roughly one million words, because every word costs a token. Both are strongly bilingual in English and Chinese, with solid coverage in many other languages. The signature edge: 10 to 100x cheaper per token than ChatGPT or Claude for comparable quality on coding and reasoning.

Fourth course · read this before you trust it

Know before you eat.

Hallucination

"Saying 'nut-free' when it's not." A confident answer with made-up facts. Every AI does this, DeepSeek included, so verify any high-stakes claim against the actual source.

Confabulation

"Making up where the eggs came from." Fills gaps with a convincing story instead of saying "I do not know," including invented library names for code.

Sycophancy

"A yes-chef who always agrees." Agrees with everything you say even when you're wrong, building on a flawed approach instead of flagging the gap.

Prompt injection

"Sneaking a fake order ticket into the kitchen." Hidden instructions in a webpage or document can try to steer the chef off course.

Data leakage

"Telling table 5 what table 3 ordered." On the hosted product, conversations may be used for training or improvement. Self-hosting fully removes this.

Bias

"Unconsciously serving bigger portions to some." Inherits biases from training data, including a Chinese-internet skew on certain sensitive topics.

Overconfidence

"Full certainty when they should warn you." States debated facts as settled with no hedge, so cross-check anything important.

China-hosted data

"Foreign kitchen handles your order." Hosted chat and API process data in China, fine for personal use, worth checking for regulated industries. Self-host removes this entirely.

Chinese content rules

"Different health codes in different countries." Safety filters follow Chinese regulation, so sensitive political topics may get cautious or non-answers on the hosted product.

The Bill. What it costs, what's next

Last course · 1 min

The hosted chat and mobile apps are entirely free: no subscription, no credit card, generous daily caps. On the API, V4 Flash runs around $0.14 per million input tokens, and V4 Pro (stronger reasoning) is currently sold at a 75% discount off list. That's roughly 1-3% of what GPT-class APIs charge for comparable work. Self-hosting the open-source weights is free to download. You only pay for your own compute.

Where DeepSeek fits: it wins on cost (10-100x cheaper API for similar coding and reasoning quality), on open-source (full weights, self-hostable, audit-friendly), and on free chat (no $20/month for the hosted product). It loses on integrations versus ChatGPT or Copilot, on English polish for nuanced professional writing versus Claude, and on privacy for compliance-heavy industries unless you self-host. Pick DeepSeek for high-volume API workloads, coding-heavy teams, bilingual EN/ZH work, or self-hosting needs.
