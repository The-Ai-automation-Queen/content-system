The Launch Announcer setup.

You finished building something good three weeks ago and never told anyone it existed. This guide hires you an AI employee that writes the announcement the moment something ships, before the launch goes quiet. He never publishes anything or invents a feature you didn't build.

What you are building

One workflow with four jobs:

  1. You tell him what just shipped: what it is, who it’s for, what it does. That’s the whole input.
  2. He writes one short announcement post, plain language, no hype words.
  3. He suggests one place to post it, based on who it’s for.
  4. You review it and send it yourself. He never sends anything.

Time to build: about 10 minutes. The tools are boring on purpose: one AI (Claude), the thing you just finished building.

The safety rules. Read these first.

This employee only ever works from what you give him, so the rules are not optional:

  1. 🟢 Green: what you shipped, who it’s for, what it actually does. That is the entire source.
  2. 🟠 Orange: the drafted announcement he builds from it.
  3. 🔴 Red: he never posts anything himself, and never invents a feature, a stat, or a claim you didn’t give him.

The rule that matters most: it only describes what’s actually built. A plain, honest announcement beats a hyped, invented one.

Part 1. Say what shipped. 5 minutes, no AI.

Do this even if you build nothing else:

  1. Write three lines: what it is, who it's for, the one thing it does.
  2. Note where the people who'd want it already hang out.

That is your entire input. You can already see what to send him.

Part 2. The announcing brain. 5 minutes.

With Claude (or your AI tool of choice) directly:

  1. Input: your three lines from Part 1.
  2. AI step: send it with the job description below. Copy it exactly, fill the blanks.

His job description (the prompt):

You are my launch announcer. Here is what just shipped: [WHAT IT IS, WHO IT'S FOR, THE ONE THING IT DOES, WHERE THE RIGHT PEOPLE ALREADY HANG OUT].

My rules:
• Write one short announcement post, under 100 words.
• Plain language, no hype words, no exclamation overload.
• Suggest one place to post it based on who it's for.
• Never add a feature, stat, or claim I didn't give you.

Return the announcement and the suggested place only.

Notice the blanks are yours to fill and the prompt never invents a feature. That is the redaction habit: the structure is public, the specifics stay home.

Part 3. Do it the same day it ships. 10 minutes.

  1. Write and post the announcement the same day the thing is actually done, not whenever you get around to it.
  2. A launch announced a month late reads like old news.

The feeling you are buying: you stop finishing things quietly, and start telling the people who'd actually want them the same day.

The upgrade path

Once the updates feel routine, you have built trust with your thirty-seventh AI employee. The same pattern, describe honestly, write plainly, flag what's blocked, is how every AI employee works. One employee at a time. That is the whole method.
