Tool verdict / xAI

Grok

FREE TOOL VERDICT · Updated 28 Jul 2026 · 5 min read

Fast, loud, and wired into X. Good for watching a story break. Not for work you'll put your name on.

  • Best forReal-time chatter on X, plus image and video generation.
  • Careful withTone and accuracy. It's built to be provocative, and it succeeds.
  • What I would doStay on the free tier unless you genuinely work in real-time news.

Where it earns its keep

There's one thing Grok does that nothing else does, and it's worth being clear about it: it can see what is being said on X right now. Not a search index from last week. The conversation happening this minute.

If your work involves knowing what people are saying about a story, a brand or a market as it develops, that's a genuine advantage. Journalists, people running social accounts, anyone whose job includes the phrase "what is the reaction". For everybody else it's a novelty.

The image and video generation is strong, and its content rules are looser than the alternatives, which some people will read as freedom and some as a warning. Both readings are fair. It also has an unusually large context window, so it will take a very long document without complaining.

Where it'll burn you

The personality isn't a setting you can fully turn off. Grok is designed to be blunt and a bit provocative, and it will slip that tone into a piece of writing you asked to be professional. If you paste its output into a client email without reading it properly, you'll eventually send something you regret. That's not a hypothetical risk, it's a Tuesday.

The real-time access is a double-edged thing too. What is being said on X right now includes a great deal that's wrong, and Grok will relay it with the same confidence it relays anything else. Real-time isn't the same as accurate. Treat it as a thermometer for what people are saying, not as a source for what is true.

And the pricing is a maze. There are eight ways to pay, they overlap with X subscriptions, and it's genuinely difficult to work out what you get for what. When a company makes its pricing that hard to follow, that in itself tells you something.

Do this

If you use it for anything client-facing, read every line before it leaves your hands. Not skim. Read. The tone slips in exactly the sentence you were not paying attention to.

Real-time isn't the same as accurate. It's barely even related.

What I'd actually do

Use the free tier. Ten prompts every two hours is enough to find out whether the real-time thing matters to you, and for most business owners the honest answer is that it doesn't.

If you're already paying for X Premium, you have some of this bundled already, so check before you buy anything else. And if you're considering the three hundred dollar a month tier, you're not the audience for this page and you already know what you're doing.

The words you'll keep hearing

  • DeepSearch. A longer research pass across the web and X, rather than an instant answer.
  • Big Brain mode. More thinking time before it answers. Slower, better on hard questions.
  • Aurora. The image and video generation system.
  • Grok 4 Heavy. Several instances working on the same problem and comparing answers. Only on the top tier.
  • Real-time X data. The one genuine advantage. Also the source of most of its wrong answers.

Is it the right one for you?

Yes, if your work genuinely depends on knowing what is being said on X as it happens, or if you want image and video generation with fewer restrictions and you have thought about why that matters to you.

No, for almost everything else. As a general business assistant it's behind ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, and the personality that makes it entertaining is the same personality that makes it risky in a client email. Free tier, watch it, move on.
