01

The Door. What you're walking into

First course · 2 min

Every restaurant tells you what it is before you sit down. Manus's door says: hand me the ticket, walk away, come back to a finished dish. This is not a kitchen you chat with: it is a kitchen you leave a written order at, and it goes to work while you do something else entirely. (And yes, even the best chef makes mistakes. The allergen card comes right before you eat, in course four.)

You reach the dining room through manus.im on the web, sign in, and get a free trial allocation of credits to taste the kitchen before you commit. A mobile app lets you drop off a ticket from the train and pick up the result at the office, because the kitchen keeps working in the cloud whether you're watching or not. There's no public developer API in the way other kitchens offer one: here, the product is the agent itself, not the raw model.

02

The Kitchen. How it actually works

Second course · the floor plan

Manus is not one chef. It is a whole brigade working a single ticket. A Planner reads your order and breaks it into steps. Executors, a browser agent, a code agent, a file agent, a research agent, each run their part: browsing sites, writing scripts, reading and writing files, aggregating sources. A Verifier checks each finished step before the next one starts. Underneath, the brigade reportedly calls several foundation models (Claude among them), picking whichever suits the sub-task. The whole loop runs asynchronously: you queue the ticket and it can cook for hours while you sleep.

The kitchen's supply line is the entire public web: because Manus drives a real browser, any site with a front door is reachable, no API contract required. Give it your login and it can reach the rooms behind authentication too, your inbox, your CRM, though that's where the risk goes up (more in course four). Everything in progress lives in a private sandbox worktop until the dish is plated.

The Menu. What to order first

Third course · three dishes

Every order has layers, and here they matter more than at any other kitchen, because the chef will actually run what you write down. A clear goal description (the end state, not the steps), honest constraints (budget, sources, a do-not-do list), any attached files the brigade needs, and, only when truly required, credentials, handed over carefully. Before anything runs, Manus shows you the plan so you can approve or trim it, which is the single best way to keep a task from wandering or burning credits it shouldn't.

Starter · 5 minutes

The Goal Description

Describe the desired end state, not how to get there. Manus plans the steps. "Compile a 10-supplier comparison spreadsheet for solar panels under 400W, in EUR" beats writing out the steps yourself.

Main · 20 minutes

The Plan Review Gate

Manus drafts a plan before it cooks. Read it, edit it, cut a "browse 200 sites" plan down to 20. This is the single biggest lever you have over cost and scope.

Chef's special

The Replay Link

Every task ships with a recorded, click-by-click walkthrough. Watch it once before you trust the output. It's the fastest way to learn how this kitchen actually thinks.

Fourth course · read this before you trust it

Know before you eat.

Hallucinations

Saying "nut-free" when it's not. Every AI does this, but in an agent, a hallucination becomes a wrong action: it confidently clicks the wrong button or fills the wrong form.

Confabulation

Making up where the eggs came from. In agentic mode this becomes a fabricated "the form was submitted" report when it actually wasn't. Always check the replay.

Prompt Injection (high-stakes)

Sneaking a fake order ticket into a kitchen with real cleavers. A webpage can hide instructions to hijack the agent, higher-stakes here because the agent acts, it doesn't just talk.

Credential Leakage

Sharing the safe combination with the kitchen. Many useful tasks need your logins, so prefer dedicated, low-privilege accounts over your main email or bank.

Credit Burn Risk

An open-bar tab nobody is watching. Long autonomous tasks consume credits at variable rates: a deep research task can burn half the monthly pool by dinner.

Butterfly Effect (maker)

The restaurant's HQ. Manus is made by Butterfly Effect, a Chinese startup, so factor data residency for sensitive workflows and regulated industries.

Inherits Foundation-Model Risks

Chef plus their teacher's bad habits. Manus reportedly runs on Claude and other models underneath, so it inherits their failure modes plus the agent-loop's own.

Novel Agent Failure Modes

New kitchen accidents nobody has seen yet. Infinite loops, scope creep, wrong-tab actions, captcha stuck states: always review results before trusting them.

05

The Bill. What it costs, what's next

Last course · 2 min

Manus wins on autonomous task completion: queue it, walk away, come back to results, on computer-use breadth (any public site is in scope), on multi-agent orchestration out of the box, and on long horizons: hours of work you don't babysit. It loses on predictability (agents fail in novel ways and results need review), on privacy for sensitive logins, and on cost predictability, because credit burn varies wildly per task. Pick this kitchen for repeatable research, market scans, data gathering, and application filling, where a quick review-and-fix pass is acceptable.

Pricing is credit-based, not per-token: simple tasks burn few credits, deep research or long autonomous sessions burn many. Set per-task credit caps so a runaway task can't drain the monthly pool. The plan review gate helps here too. By default, Manus also pauses before submitting forms, making purchases, or sending messages on sensitive sites, and asks you to confirm.
