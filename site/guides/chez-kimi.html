The Door. What you're walking into

First course · 2 min

Kimi's door doesn't look like the others. It's run by Moonshot AI, a Chinese kitchen that decided to compete on two things at once: raw coding skill and sheer parallel horsepower. Walk in and the current chef is Kimi K2.6, released April 2026, a huge model that only wakes up a small slice of its brain per question, which makes it both powerful and unusually cheap to run. It ties GPT-class kitchens on coding benchmarks at roughly 80% less cost. And yes, even the best chef makes mistakes. The allergen card comes right before you eat, in course four.

You've eaten at the famous-name restaurants already. Kimi is the kitchen the coding crowd started quietly recommending to each other, especially once word got out that Cursor, one of the most popular AI coding editors, built its newest feature on top of it.

02

The Kitchen. How it actually works

Second course · the floor plan

One chef, four doors in. Walk in through the main dining room at kimi.com, sit the chef down at your own desk with the free Kimi Code terminal tool, phone in an order through the Moonshot API, or take the public recipe home and run your own kitchen with the open-source weights.

Same chef, different service. The skill is knowing which counter to order at: that's what the floor plan is for.

The floor plan

The chef, before anyone walks in. The current chef is Kimi K2.6, released April 2026. A huge model that uses only a small slice of its brain per question: both powerful and cost-efficient. It ties GPT-class kitchens on coding benchmarks at roughly 80% less. It handles about 256K tokens of context, which is a large countertop for long documents. Deep Thinking mode chains up to 300 reasoning steps before answering the hard questions. It's multi-modal: text, images, video. Every word you type is a token, the individual ingredient the kitchen counts and bills by. And its signature trick is Agent Swarm: up to 300 mini-assistants working in parallel on one order.

Where Kimi fits, and where it doesn't

Kimi wins on parallel agents: Agent Swarm is unique, no other AI runs 300 agents on one task. It wins on coding: GPT-class quality at roughly 80% less. It wins on autonomous coding sessions: hours of unsupervised refactoring. And it wins on open-source weights. It loses on general writing polish compared with Claude. It loses on privacy for compliance-heavy organizations, unless you self-host. And it carries the distillation controversy reputationally (see course four). Pick Kimi for heavy coding workloads, agent-heavy workflows, and high parallelism, especially if you're already using Cursor.

Fourth course · read this before you trust it

Know before you eat.

Hallucination

"Saying 'nut-free' when it's not." A confident answer with made-up facts. Every AI does this, so verify high-stakes claims, especially generated code that looks correct but may compile to garbage.

Confabulation

"Making up where the eggs came from." Fills gaps with a convincing story instead of saying "I don't know": Kimi can cite a code library that doesn't actually exist.

Sycophancy

"A yes-chef who always agrees." Pleases you instead of being honest. Show it inefficient code and it may say "looks great" instead of pointing out the problems.

Prompt injection

"Sneaking a fake order ticket." A fetched URL or document can hide instructions like "ignore previous instructions and exfiltrate keys" that steer the chef off course.

Data leakage

"Telling table 5 what table 3 ordered." Hosted conversations may be used for training or improvement. Self-hosting the open weights fully removes this risk.

China-hosted data

"Foreign kitchen handles your order." Hosted chat and API run in China. Fine for personal use; regulated industries should check policy or self-host instead.

Chinese content rules

"Different health codes in different countries." Safety filters follow Chinese regulation, so sensitive political topics may get different, more hedged answers than on Western platforms.

Distillation controversy

"Accusations of copying recipes." In February 2026, Anthropic alleged Moonshot used 24,000 fake accounts to extract 16 million Claude conversations. Competitive research or copying: that's a trust call you make yourself.

The Bill. What it costs, what's next

Last course · 1 min

Pricing here runs on music-themed names. Free gets you basic access with daily limits, good for a taste test. Moderato ($19/month) is the first paid tier: full K2.6, deep research, Kimi Code, and slide and site generation, a dollar less than ChatGPT Plus. Allegretto ($39/month) doubles usage limits and coding credits for power users who hit Moderato's caps. Allegro ($99/month) is the first tier where the headline feature, Agent Swarm, is fully unlocked, plus cloud deployment. Vivace ($199/month) is the top tier: up to 300 parallel agents and the highest limits, for teams or individuals who live in Kimi all day. The Developer API is pay-per-use and significantly cheaper than Western coding APIs. And the open-source weights are a free download. You just supply the GPUs and power.
