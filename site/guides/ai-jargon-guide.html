The sentence that started it

Overheard in a meeting

“We’re RAG-ing our customer service docs into a Claude agent with an MCP connection to Zendesk. If quality slips, we’ll fine-tune. The context window should handle it. Tokens are cheap now.”

If that sentence made you blink, you’re not behind. That sentence is 7 technical terms in 30 words. Nobody learns vocabulary by pretending they already know it.

Let’s break it apart.

The words inside the sentence

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

The AI looks something up in your documents before answering. Instead of guessing from its training data, it quotes from files you gave it. Cuts hallucinations. Used when you want answers from your own data, not the internet.

Agent

A chatbot that can take actions. Not just talk. Click buttons, send emails, read your calendar, run code. When someone says “Claude agent,” they mean Claude is doing things, not just suggesting them.

MCP (Model Context Protocol)

A standard plug that lets AI tools connect to other apps. Like USB-C, but for software. “MCP connection to Zendesk” means Claude and Zendesk speak the same plug language.

Fine-tune

Retrain a general model on your specific data so it behaves a certain way. Expensive and slow. Often replaced by good prompting + RAG. The fallback when everything else fails.

Context window

How much text the AI can hold in its head at once. Claude’s is around 1 million tokens. Go past the limit and it starts forgetting the beginning of your conversation.

Token

A chunk of text, roughly ¾ of a word. AI charges by tokens. 1,000 tokens is about 750 words. Now you can read the pricing page without flinching.

You just decoded that meeting sentence. Read it again. It makes sense now.

The rest of the room

Six more words you’ll hear before the quarter ends. Same treatment: one sentence, no jargon inside the jargon.

Hallucination

When AI confidently invents wrong information. The single biggest reason to verify AI answers on legal, medical, or financial topics. It doesn’t know it’s lying. That’s what makes it dangerous.

Skill

A reusable instruction set you trigger by name. A prompt + tools + steps, packaged and ready to run. Like a recipe card the AI follows every time.

Embedding

The math version of a sentence. Two sentences with similar meaning have similar embeddings. Powers search inside AI tools. Recognise the word. You don’t need to think about it further.

Temperature

A dial from 0 to 1 controlling how creative the AI gets. 0 = same answer every time. 1 = wildly different every time. Default is usually 0.7. You’ll rarely touch it.

Open weights / open source

The model file is publicly downloadable. You can run it on your own hardware. Mistral, Llama, DeepSeek are open. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini are not. Matters for privacy, cost, and control.

Multimodal

AI that handles text, images, audio, and video together. Most major models are multimodal now. Paste a screenshot, get a paragraph back.

Now try the sentence again

Same sentence, translated

“We’re RAG-ing our customer service docs into a Claude agent with an MCP connection to Zendesk. If quality slips, we’ll fine-tune.”

Means:
  • Their docs are searched in real time so the AI quotes from them (RAG)
  • Claude takes actions inside Zendesk, not just answers questions (agent + MCP)
  • If the answers get bad, they’ll retrain the model on their specific data (fine-tune)

You follow that now. And the next time someone says it, you can ask the question nobody else will: “What happens when it can’t find the answer in your docs?”

These 12 words cover roughly 90% of the AI vocabulary in any business conversation. The other 10% you look up the moment you need it.

The gap was never intelligence. It was just vocabulary.
