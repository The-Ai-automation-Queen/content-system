The lead follow-up that runs itself

Most leads are lost to silence, not to competitors. This fixes the silence.

Where the money actually goes

Here is the uncomfortable part. Most people do not lose business because someone else was better. They lose it because someone raised their hand, nobody answered quickly enough, and by the time anyone did the moment had gone.

That is not a sales problem. It is a plumbing problem, and plumbing is fixable in an afternoon.

What you are building is a straight line with no gaps in it. Someone comments your keyword, an instant message goes out with the link, they opt in, the resource is delivered and they get tagged in your CRM, and a three-message follow-up runs on its own.

Step one. The trigger

Set a workflow to fire when a comment or a form opt-in contains your keyword. Something short and easy to type: STACK, FOLLOW UP, TEAM. One workflow per offer, so you always know which promise you are keeping.

Keep the keyword list somewhere you can see it. Every keyword you have ever put on a post is a promise, and a keyword pointing at a resource you have since taken down is worse than no keyword at all.

Step two. The instant reply

Speed decides this. The reply that arrives in ten seconds converts. The one that arrives in ten hours does not, because in ten hours they have scrolled past four hundred other things.

The message needs to do three things and nothing else: confirm you saw them, hand over the link, and tell them what happens next. Do not sell here. They have already said yes to something small. Let that be enough.

Do this

Send yourself through your own funnel today. Comment your own keyword from another account. Whatever annoys you in the next five minutes is what is quietly annoying everybody else.

Step three. Capture and tag

The email address is the point of the whole exercise. Everything before this is traffic and traffic is rented.

Tag the record with which keyword brought them in. It takes one extra field and it means that in three months you can answer the only question that matters: which of these actually turns into work?

Traffic is rented. The list is owned. Everything in this guide exists to move people from the first to the second.

Step four. The three messages

Three, spread over four days. More than that and you are pestering. Fewer and you are hoping.

Message one, same day

Deliver, and set up what comes next. Short.

Here is the thing you asked for. It is yours, no strings. One thing before you go: most people get stuck on the same step, which is picking the first task. I will send you how I choose it in a couple of days.

Message two, day two

Value, and quiet proof. Do not ask for anything.

Teach the one idea that makes the resource work. Then mention, once and without ceremony, something real that happened for someone who did it. Not a testimonial with a photograph. One sentence, in passing.

Message three, day four

The invitation, and it should be easy to decline. If the answer is no, you want that clearly and early, not as a slow fade.

The point

None of this is clever. That is deliberate. A follow-up sequence that runs is worth ten times a brilliant one you never build, and the whole thing above takes an afternoon.

Build the trigger and the instant reply first. Those two catch most of what you are currently losing. The three messages can wait a week. Perfect is the reason most of these never go live.
