How to set up your first AI employee.

Forget “using AI.” That’s a toy. The shift happens when you stop using it and start employing it — giving it a real job, real tools, and a real shift to work, so a whole task leaves your plate for good. Here’s how to hire your first one this week. No tech degree required.

What an “AI employee” actually is

Not a chatbot you poke at. A worker you set up once that:

  • owns one repeatable task end to end,
  • has the tools it needs to do it,
  • works on a schedule without you starting it,
  • and hands you a result to approve.

That’s it. The skill is in the setup, not the babysitting.

The 5-step hire

1. Pick the task (the boring, repeating one)

Look at your week. What do you redo every few days? Drafting captions. Sorting leads. Turning a long video into clips. Pick the one you most resent doing by hand. That’s your first hire.

2. Write the job description

One page, plain language: what “good” looks like, the steps, the rules, what to never do. This is the single highest-leverage thing you’ll write — a clear job description is what separates a reliable employee from a random one.

Template:
Role: ________
Goal: ________
Steps: 1)… 2)… 3)…
Rules / brand voice: ________
Never do: ________
Hand me: ________ (what the finished result looks like)

3. Give it tools

An employee with no access can’t work. Connect what the task needs — your content tool, your CRM, your scheduler. (This is exactly what Claude Code does: it can run real tools, not just chat.)

4. Put it on a shift

Set a schedule — daily at 7am, every Monday, whatever the task needs. Now it shows up to work on its own. This is the difference between “a tool I open” and “an employee that runs.”

5. Approve, don’t redo

You stay the boss. It produces; you give a thumbs up (or one note) and it goes out. You went from doing the task to approving it — that’s your time back.

Your first week

  • Day 1: pick the task + write the job description.
  • Day 2: connect one tool, run it once manually, fix the description.
  • Day 3: put it on a schedule.
  • Rest of the week: just approve. Notice the hours that come back.

That’s one employee. Now imagine five. That’s the freedom business — it runs without running your life.

Stop using AI. Start employing it.
