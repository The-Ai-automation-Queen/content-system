What an AI employee actually is

Forget "using AI". Using AI is a toy: you open a chat window, ask something, get an answer, close the tab, and next week you do it all again from scratch.

The shift happens when you stop asking and start employing. An AI employee is a task you set up once, with a real job, real tools and a real shift, so it runs without you starting it. That's the whole difference, and it's the difference between a novelty and an hour back every day.

I'm hiring ninety-nine of these to run my own business, in public, and the method below is the one I use every time. It's not technical. It's the same method you would use to hire a person, which is exactly why it works.

One. Pick the task

Look at your week. What do you redo every few days? Drafting captions. Sorting leads. Turning a long video into clips. Chasing the same three suppliers.

Pick the one you most resent doing by hand. Not the most valuable, not the most impressive. The one that makes you sigh. Resentment is a reliable signal that a task is repetitive, well understood and low judgement, which is exactly the profile you want for a first hire.

Two. Write the job description

One page, plain language. What good looks like, the steps, the rules, and what never to do. This is the highest-leverage thing you'll write this month.

A clear job description is what separates a reliable employee from one you have to check constantly, and that's as true here as it's with people. Use this shape:

RoleWho it's being while it does this work.
GoalThe outcome, in one sentence.
StepsOne, two, three. In order.
Rules and voiceHow it should sound, what it must always include.
Never doThe lines it doesn't cross, written plainly.
Hand meWhat the finished thing looks like when it arrives.

If you get stuck writing this, stop. Being unable to describe the job isn't a writing problem, it's a sign the task isn't ready to hand over. Do it by hand three more times, notice what you actually do, then write it.

Do this

Write the "never do" section before anything else. It's the part people skip and the part that keeps you out of trouble. Two lines is enough.

Three. Give it tools

A job description with no access is a suggestion. Give it what it needs to actually finish: the folder, the document, the inbox, the account, the examples of past work you were happy with.

And give it exactly what it needs and nothing more. The same rule you would apply to a new starter on their first week.

You wouldn't hand a new hire the keys to everything on day one. Don't do it here either.

Four. Put it on a shift

This is the step that turns a tool into an employee, and it's the one almost everybody misses.

An employee doesn't wait for you to ask. It has a time. Every weekday at seven. Every Monday morning. Whenever a new lead arrives. Put the job on a schedule or a trigger, and it starts happening whether or not you remembered.

Until you do this, you don't have an employee. You have a very good tool that still depends entirely on you opening it, which means on your worst week it does nothing at all. Your worst week is exactly when you needed it.

Five. Approve, don't redo

The first week won't be right. Neither is a person's first week.

The instinct is to take the output, fix it yourself, and move on. Resist that, because it teaches you nothing and it teaches the system nothing. Instead, when something comes back wrong, don't correct the output. Correct the job description.

Wrong tone? That's a rules line. Missed a step? That's a steps line. Did something you didn't want? That's a never-do line. Every correction goes back into the page, and after a fortnight the page is good and the work arrives finished.

Your first week

DayWhat you do
MondayPick the task. The one you resent.
TuesdayWrite the one-page job description, never-do section first.
WednesdayGive it access to what it needs. Nothing else.
ThursdayRun it by hand three times. Edit the page after each one.
FridayPut it on a shift. Then leave it alone.

By the following Friday you'll know whether this hire worked. If it did, that task is gone from your week permanently, and you go and find the next one you resent.
