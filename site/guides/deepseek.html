Tool verdict / DeepSeek

DeepSeek

Very good, very cheap, and hosted in China. That last part is the whole decision.

  • Best forHigh-volume API work where cost decides it, and coding.
  • Careful withAnything client-confidential on the hosted version.
  • What I would doRun the open weights on your own hardware, or leave it alone.

Where it earns its keep

Let me give you the capability answer first, because it's genuinely impressive and it's the reason this guide exists. DeepSeek does work of a standard comparable to the expensive American models, at a fraction of the price. On the API, roughly a thirty-fifth of what a GPT-class model costs for comparable work.

If you're building something that makes thousands of calls a day, that's not a small saving, it's the difference between a viable product and one that never launches. Coding is where it's strongest, and it will take a very long document without losing the thread.

The hosted chat is free, with generous daily limits and no subscription. Web search, file upload, a thinking mode you can toggle when a question needs working through rather than answering.

And the models are open-weight. You can download them and run them on hardware you own, which is the part of this story that matters most for a business, and I'll come back to it.

Where it'll burn you

The hosted service is operated in China and subject to Chinese law. That's not an insinuation about the company, it's a plain fact about jurisdiction, and it's the same kind of fact as Mistral being subject to European law. It changes what you should put into it.

What that means in practice: don't put client data in it. Don't put anything covered by a confidentiality agreement, anything about your customers, your contracts or your numbers. If you're in the EU or the UK and you handle personal data, using the hosted version for that data is a GDPR question you would have to be able to answer, and most small businesses cannot.

There's also content filtering on politically sensitive topics, which won't affect most business use but will surprise you if you hit it. And there are fewer integrations and connectors than the major tools have.

Do this

Apply one test before every message: would I be comfortable if this text were stored on a server in a country whose legal system I don't understand? If yes, carry on. If you hesitated, that's your answer.

The model isn't the risk. The address it's stored at is.

What I'd actually do

For a business, there are two sensible positions and one bad one.

The first sensible position: use the hosted version for work with nothing confidential in it. Generic code, public research, learning what the model can do. Free, capable, no harm done.

The second, and the one I would recommend to anyone with a real technical reason to be here: run the open weights on your own hardware or in your own cloud. You get the capability, the cost advantage and complete control over where the data sits. That's a genuinely good option and almost nobody talks about it.

The bad position is pasting client material into the hosted chat because it's free. Don't do that. Not because of where it's, but because you cannot answer for it if you're ever asked.

The words you'll keep hearing

  • Open weights. The model file is published, so you can download and run it yourself. This is the whole reason DeepSeek is worth understanding.
  • Thinking mode. A toggle that makes it work through a problem before answering. Slower, and much better on hard questions.
  • Tokens. How API use is billed. Roughly three-quarters of a word each.
  • Self-hosting. Running the model on hardware you control. The answer to the jurisdiction problem, if you have the appetite for it.

Is it the right one for you?

Yes, if you're building something at volume where API cost is the constraint, or if you have the technical capacity to self-host and a genuine reason to keep everything in-house.

No, if you're a small business looking for a daily assistant and you would be typing client details into it. In that case the twenty dollars a month for ChatGPT or Claude buys you something you can actually defend, and that's worth more than the saving.
