## Draft 1 — LinkedIn Text Post

**Angle:** Adapted from Allie K. Miller's capability step-change hook. CEO audience.  
**Pillar:** AI confidence / internal capability  
**Target:** 150–250 words. Comment-trigger CTA.

---

The hardest AI shift is not saving your team 20 minutes a day.

It is your business becoming capable of things that were structurally impossible before now.

In 2017, building a high-quality AI video avatar of a real person cost $250,000 and took weeks.

Last month, it took 30 minutes.

That is not efficiency.
That is a different category of business entirely.

The CEOs who are 12 months ahead of you are not running the same processes faster.

They are running processes your company could not run at all before now.

Things like:
— Client-facing AI that answers complex questions at 2am on a Sunday.
— Competitive analysis complete before the board meeting, not after.
— Due diligence on 50 suppliers in the time it used to take to read three reports.

None of that required a technology team.
None of it required a six-month implementation project.

It required a decision to treat AI as a capability question, not a cost-saving question.

The efficiency frame keeps you making incremental improvements.
The capability frame makes you dangerous to your category.

Most CEOs are still in the efficiency frame.

That gap is the opportunity.

What is the one thing your business could do for the first time — not faster — if you approached AI as a capability question?

Drop it in the comments. I read every reply.

## Draft 2 — LinkedIn Text Post

**Angle:** The uncovered gap — shadow AI and unauthorised tool use on company data. Mid-market CEO ($5M–$100M).  
**Pillar:** Data and security awareness  
**Target:** 150–250 words. Direct question CTA that provokes replies.

---

Right now, your employees are using AI tools on your company data.

Without your approval.
Without a policy.
Without you knowing which tools, which vendors, or where your data is sitting.

This is not speculation.

Research published in 2026 found that 67% of employees at mid-market companies use AI tools their employer has not approved.

They use them because the tools are free, fast, and genuinely good.
You would use them too.

The problem is not the tools.

The problem is your client contracts sitting in a third-party server under terms of service your legal team has never read.

The problem is your pricing models, your internal strategy documents, your financial projections — training someone else's model, right now, without your knowledge.

The CEOs who regret this are not the ones who knew and accepted the risk.

They are the ones who found out after the incident.

You do not need to ban AI.
You do not need a six-month policy project.

You need 90 minutes, a clear list of approved tools, and one conversation with your team.

That is it.

Before you do anything else with AI this week: do you know which tools your team is already using without company approval?

Reply below.
I want to understand where business owners actually stand on this — not where they think they should stand.

**Word count:** ~215 words
