## Draft 1 — LinkedIn Text Post

*Adapted from: Ethan Mollick's "obsolescence gap" / chatbot-to-agent paradigm
shift (July 2026). Pillar: Stop Doing That by Hand.*

---

The chatbot era is over.

The skill most professionals spent the last 12 months building — better prompting,
better questions, better ChatGPT workflows — is already being automated away.

One prompt can now run 16 hours of autonomous work.

That is not a chatbot.
That is an employee who never clocks off.

The problem: most business owners are still optimizing for last year's tool.
They are writing better prompts.
Their competitors are directing systems that run without them.

The shift is not technical. It is managerial.

You do not need to understand how the agent works.
You need to know what to hand it, what outcome to describe, and how to review the
result.

That is a skill that takes an afternoon to learn.
It does not require a developer.
It does not require a background in tech.
It requires the same judgment you already use to delegate to a person — applied
to a system that works while you sleep.

The question is not whether AI can do the work.
The question is whether you know what work to give it.

If you have been waiting for AI to get simple enough to act on — this is the week.

Drop "AGENT" in the comments and I will send you the one-page framework I use
to identify which part of your business is ready to delegate right now.

**Pillar:** Stop Doing That by Hand
**Pattern applied:** Provocation-First Hook (Pattern 1) + Contrarian Operational

## Draft 2 — LinkedIn Text Post

*Gap piece: The AI displacement → entrepreneur pivot. Topic none of the monitored
creators covered. Pillar: The Freedom Business.*

---

Every week, another round of layoffs.
Every announcement names AI as the reason.

Here is what nobody in your LinkedIn feed is saying:

If AI is eliminating your role, the worst possible move is to find another
corporate job that AI will eliminate next.

The professionals who are winning right now are not the ones who found a safer
company.
They are the ones who looked at the disruption and said: if AI can do this work,
then I can run a business built on AI — instead of competing against it.

That pivot is not about technology.
It is about timing.

Right now, the tools that would have taken a development team six months to build
are available to a single person with no technical background.
The business models that required 10 full-time employees two years ago can run on
a team of two and a set of automations.

The window is not permanent.

The solopreneurs who build AI-powered businesses in 2026 will set the pricing
floor for everyone who follows in 2027 and 2028.

This brand exists because I made that pivot before most people believed it was
possible.
The path is repeatable.
The timing is better than it looks.

If your company has cited AI in a restructuring announcement this year — what is
stopping you from building the thing on your own terms?
