## Draft 1 — LinkedIn text post

**Pillar:** Stop Doing That by Hand
**Pattern:** Provocation-first (Allie K. Miller) + Fear-first (Brooke Wright)
**Hook source:** Ethan Mollick, "Twilight of the Chatbots" / Forbes July 14, 2026
**Word count:** ~160 words

---

You're bad at feeling exponentials.

Not because you're slow.

Because your brain wasn't built for compounding.

Every week you treat AI as background noise, the gap between you and the people actually using it grows.

A solopreneur who set up one automation six months ago has a different business today.

Not because they worked harder.

Because their system kept running while they slept.

The chatbot era is already over.

Typing questions into a box was never the destination.

Agents are doing real work now — seven, eight hours of knowledge work, unattended. While you're at dinner. While you're at school pickup.

A quarter of workers at one major AI company run four or more agents simultaneously. Every single week.

The question isn't whether AI will change how you work.

That already happened.

The question is: which version of AI are you still using?

Tell me in the comments — are you still in chatbot mode, or have you made the switch?

## Draft 2 — LinkedIn text post

**Pillar:** The Freedom Business / Real Talk
**Pattern:** Vulnerability before credentials (Brooke Wright) + Lived experience as the differentiator
**Angle:** The gap this week — nobody is covering the corporate → entrepreneur exit and how AI made it possible
**Audience:** Mid-career professional in the transition — still in corporate, or newly out, wondering when the freedom starts
**Word count:** ~200 words

---

Nobody tells you what the exit actually costs.

Not in courage. In time.

You leave corporate. You start building. And for the first year, you trade one kind of exhaustion for another.

The commute goes. The late nights come back.

Here's what doesn't make the highlight reels: most people who leave corporate don't automate their way out. They just move the grind to a different address.

I had the tech background. I had the network. I still worked nights longer than I care to admit.

What changed things wasn't a new strategy.

It was one system. Built once. Running without me.

Emails responded to. Content published. Leads captured. Follow-ups sent.

All of it moving while I was offline.

That's not automation as a productivity hack.

That's automation as a life change.

If you're in the middle of that transition — still in corporate, or newly out, wondering when the freedom part actually starts — you're not behind.

You're exactly where I was.

One question: what part of your business do you most wish would just run itself?
