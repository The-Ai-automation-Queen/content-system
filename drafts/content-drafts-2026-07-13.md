## Draft 1 — LinkedIn Text Post

**Pillar:** Build Once, Runs Forever
**Hook adapted from:** Ruben Hassid, "This boring AI agent is the only one I
actually use"
**Pattern:** Contrarian operational hook → personal proof → practical teaching
→ comment trigger

---

The most boring automation I built is still the most important one I own.

It runs every morning.
It does not break.
It does not need me to babysit it.
I set it up once, about 18 months ago.

Meanwhile, I've watched people spend weeks building "intelligent" multi-agent
systems that impress at demos and fail in production.

Here is the difference:

The boring automation does one thing.
It does it the same way every time.
It hands off a clean output to the next step without asking for help.

The impressive ones try to do everything, infer what you meant, and handle
edge cases on the fly.

That sounds better.
It is not better.

I am not telling you to ignore new tools.
I am telling you: build the boring one first.

Get one automation running reliably for 30 days.
Then build the next.

The business owners who are actually running less of their business by hand
right now?
They did not chase the flashiest thing.
They picked one workflow that hurt the most and fixed it completely.

What is the one manual task in your business that costs you the most time every
single week?
Drop it in the comments — I want to see what we are all still doing by hand.

---
**Word count:** ~195
**CTA type:** Comment trigger (surfaces real pain, builds engagement and post
signals simultaneously)

## Draft 2 — LinkedIn Text Post

**Pillar:** What's Worth It
**Topic:** The most urgent gap — "Stop model-hopping" (no creator covered the AI
switching cost this week despite four major model drops)
**Audience:** Mid-market business owners ($5M–$100M) building internal AI capability
**Pattern:** Provocation → honest counter-narrative → specific take → direct question

---

Four new AI models dropped this week.

GPT-5.6. Grok 4.5. Fable 5 extended. Meta Muse.

Every newsletter in your inbox is telling you to try them all.

Here is what they are not telling you:

Switching between AI models constantly is one of the most expensive habits a
business owner can build right now.

Not in subscription cost.
In context cost.

Every time you open a new tool, you start over.
It does not know your business.
It does not know your voice.
It does not know the decisions you made last week.

So you brief it again.
And the output is generic again.
And you wonder why AI is not saving you time.

The people who are actually getting results from AI right now are not chasing
every new release.

They have one or two tools configured properly — set up with their company
context, their processes, their constraints.

Those tools know them.
And the outputs show it.

A new model is only better than your current one if your current one is actually
set up.

Most are not.

Here is the question that matters more than "which AI tool is best this week":
What does your current AI tool know about how your business actually runs?

If the answer is "not much" — that is the problem to fix before you try anything
new.

---
**Word count:** ~225
**CTA type:** Direct question (provokes honest self-assessment, invites replies
that reveal audience's setup gaps — useful sales intelligence)

## Draft 4 — Twitter/X Thread (5 Tweets)

**Pillar:** What's Worth It
**Topic:** AI judgment improving — the most significant trending observation of the
week (Allie K. Miller + Ethan Mollick both circling it, but neither translated it
for solopreneurs)
**Pattern:** Counterintuitive claim → evidence → implication → practical step →
direct close

---

**Tweet 1 (hook — provocation / counterintuitive claim):**
Six months ago I did not trust AI with a single client decision.

Last week I handed it 14 judgment calls.

It got 13 right.

The game has changed. Here is what that means for your business. 🧵

---

**Tweet 2 (develop — what changed):**
AI used to be good at tasks with one right answer.

Fill in the blank. Summarise this. Write this in a different tone.

Now it is getting good at trade-offs.

"Given these two options and these constraints, which do you recommend?"

That is a different category of useful.

---

**Tweet 3 (develop — the implication):**
That means the things you refused to delegate to AI before?

Some of them are ready to hand off now.

Not all. Not forever. But more than last year.

The entrepreneurs winning right now are auditing that list.
The others are still treating AI like a fancier search engine.

---

**Tweet 4 (develop — what to do):**
Practical test to run this week:

Take one decision you make regularly that you have always done yourself.
Write out the factors you weigh.
Hand it to your AI with that context.
See what it recommends.

Do not outsource the decision.
Just see how close it gets.

You will be surprised.

---

**Tweet 5 (close — direct question):**
AI is not replacing your judgment.

It is getting close enough to assist it.

That is worth your attention.

What is one business decision you would never trust AI with today?
Drop it below. I want to know where the line still sits.

---
**Each tweet character count:** all under 280 ✓
**Thread logic:** counterintuitive claim → what changed (capability) → implication
(for you) → practical action → open question that invites replies
