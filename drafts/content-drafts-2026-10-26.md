## Draft 1 — LinkedIn Text Post
**Angle:** Adapted from Mollick's price-collapse hook
**Audience:** CEOs and business owners
**Target length:** 150–250 words

---

The benchmark that cost 30 cents per correct AI answer now costs 0.04 cents.

That is a 750x collapse in 30 months.

Every automation task you dismissed as too expensive two years ago is now nearly free.

The question is not whether you can afford to build internal AI capability.

The question is what you are still paying a human to do that a system could do for less.

Most business owners have not revisited that decision since 2024.

They accepted certain manual processes as permanent.

Those processes are not permanent anymore.

The founders who understand this are not chasing the latest model release.

They are going back through every bottleneck they accepted and asking: does this still make sense?

Some are building capability that will look like an unfair advantage in 18 months.

Some are still waiting.

The cost barrier to automating your business just collapsed.

The only thing that has not changed is the decision to start.

What is the first manual process you would rebuild if the price were zero?

Drop it in the comments — I read every reply.

## Draft 2 — LinkedIn Text Post
**Angle:** Gap — customer trust deficit translated for the CEO audience
**Audience:** Mid-market CEO ($5M–$100M company)
**Target length:** 150–250 words

---

62% of Americans do not trust any of the major AI developers.

That number comes from a recent survey of 5,000 people.

Most business owners read that statistic and move on.

The ones who should pause are the ones using AI to serve those same people.

Without a written policy.
Without staff training.
Without telling their customers.

The risk is not that AI makes a mistake.

It makes mistakes every day.

The risk is that when it does, your business has no explanation.

Internal AI capability means your team knows what the tools do — and what they do not do.

It means you have clear rules for where AI is applied and where a human is required.

It means you can answer a customer who asks.

That is not a technology problem.

It is a leadership decision.

The companies that navigate the next three years well are the ones whose leaders thought about this before the first public failure — not after.

Most of your competitors have not thought about it yet.

What is your current policy on AI in client-facing work?

One sentence in the comments. I want to see what people are actually doing.
