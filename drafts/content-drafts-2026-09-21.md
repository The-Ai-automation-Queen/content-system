## Draft 1 — LinkedIn Text Post

**Hook source:** Ethan Mollick "The Overhang" — adapted for CEO/business owner audience
**Pillar:** AI confusion to AI confidence / internal capability
**Target word count:** 200–230 words

---

AI did not get slower this week.

Your competitors did not get less ambitious.

And yet most leadership teams are still using AI the same way they did a year ago.

Wharton professor Ethan Mollick published a paper this week called "The Overhang."

The argument: even if AI stopped improving today, the catching-up alone would take years.

Because the gap between what current AI can do and what most people are asking it to do is already enormous.

This is not a technology problem.

It is a readiness problem.

And for a business owner, it is a competitive gap that compounds every month you wait.

Here is what "the overhang" looks like inside a real company:

Your AI tools can draft proposals, brief new hires, summarise calls, flag risks in contracts, and build first-version reports.

Most of your team is still using AI to write emails faster.

The gap is not in the software.

It is in what your people believe AI is for.

Changing that belief does not require a new tool.

It requires one honest conversation about what you are still doing by hand that you do not need to do by hand.

That conversation starts with the CEO.

What is one task your team is still doing manually that should not be?

Drop it in the comments — I read every one.

## Draft 2 — LinkedIn Text Post

**Source:** Gap identified in competitor watch — the AI liability problem inside mid-market businesses
**Pillar:** Data and security awareness / internal capability not consulting dependency
**Audience:** Mid-market CEO ($5M–$100M company)
**Target word count:** 200–230 words

---

Your employees are using AI with your company data right now.

Do you know which tools they are using?

Do you know what data they are pasting in?

Do you know what those tools do with that data after the session ends?

Most CEOs at the $10M–$80M level have no answer to any of these questions.

And this is not a hypothetical risk.

Client contracts. Financial models. HR conversations. Internal strategy documents.

These are going into free AI tools that were not designed for business confidentiality.

The response most companies reach for is a ban.

Bans do not work.

People keep using the tools — they just stop telling you.

The response that actually works: build an approved internal stack and train the team on what it is for.

That stack does not need to be expensive.

It does not need a consultant to build it.

It needs one owner inside your business who understands both the tools and your data obligations.

That is what internal AI capability actually looks like.

Not a policy document.

Not an external vendor relationship.

One informed person who can protect the business and move it forward at the same time.

Who is that person in your company?

If the answer is unclear, that is the problem to solve this quarter.
