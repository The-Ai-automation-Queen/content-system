Tool verdict / OpenAI

ChatGPT

The generalist. It will have a go at anything, which is exactly why you have to check it.

  • Best forFirst drafts, thinking out loud, images, and a bit of everything else.
  • Careful withAnything where roughly right is not good enough.
  • What I would doIf you are only ever going to pay for one, pay for this one.

Where it earns its keep

ChatGPT is the one most people meet first, and for once the popular choice is a reasonable one. Not because it wins on any single measure. Because it is competent at more things than anything else on this list, and for a business owner with eleven jobs, breadth beats depth.

It is very good at getting you from a blank page to something you can react to. That is a real skill and it is the one most people underrate. Editing something mediocre takes ten minutes. Starting from nothing takes an hour and usually a walk around the block first.

It generates images, reads the ones you upload, searches the web when it needs current information, runs code to do actual arithmetic on your spreadsheet rather than guessing at it, and holds a voice conversation if you would rather talk than type. Deep Research will go away for several minutes and come back with a sourced report. Agent Mode will use a browser on your behalf and complete a task with steps in it. Codex handles the coding work.

The feature nobody uses and everybody should is Projects. Put your standing instructions, your brand voice and your reference documents in one, and every conversation inside it starts from context instead of from zero. That single habit is worth more than any model upgrade.

Where it'll burn you

Its greatest strength is that it will attempt anything. That is also the problem. It does not tell you when a task is outside what it can do well. It just produces something confident and plausible, and confident and plausible is precisely what a wrong answer looks like.

Numbers are the classic trap. It will do arithmetic in its head and get it wrong in a way that looks entirely reasonable. If a calculation matters, make it use the code tool, or check it in a spreadsheet yourself. Do not accept mental arithmetic from a machine that is fundamentally guessing at the next word.

Memory across conversations cuts both ways. Handy when it remembers your business. Awkward when it quietly carries an assumption from three weeks ago into today's work and you cannot see where the assumption came from. If an answer feels oddly off-target, clear the memory and try again cold.

And it agrees with you. Frame a question as though the decision is already made and it will help you justify it. Ask instead: what is the strongest case against doing this?

Do this

Put one line at the end of any prompt that matters: "Tell me which parts of this answer you are least sure about." It costs you nothing and it turns a confident wall of text into something you can actually check.

It is a very fast first draft and a very poor last word.

What I'd actually do

Use the free tier for a fortnight on real work. If you find yourself in it most days, twenty dollars a month for Plus is the easiest software decision you will make this year, and it is the plan I would recommend to almost everyone reading this.

Do not buy Pro because it sounds more serious. Pro is for people whose day is genuinely made of this tool, all day. If that is not you, the money is better spent elsewhere.

The words you will keep hearing

  • GPT. The model underneath. Everything else is packaging around it.
  • Custom GPTs. A saved setup with its own instructions and files, for a task you repeat. Useful. Slightly overhyped.
  • Agent Mode. It gets a browser and completes multi-step tasks. Impressive, and the place to be most careful about permissions.
  • Deep Research. A long sourced report instead of an instant answer. Check the sources. It cites real ones and occasionally misreads them.
  • Connectors and MCP. Controlled access to your other tools. Switch on what you need and nothing else.

Is it the right one for you?

If you want one tool that does most things adequately, this is it, and there is no shame in that being the right answer. Most small businesses do not need the best tool for each job. They need one tool they will actually open.

If your work is mostly deep reading and long documents, Claude is better at it. If your business lives inside Google Workspace, Gemini's access to your own files beats raw capability most days. And if you are choosing your first three tools rather than your only one, start with the stack.
