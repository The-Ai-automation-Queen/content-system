# The News Digest Setup

**Keyword: DIGEST** | Pillar: Stop Doing That by Hand | Employee #010 (Maya) playbook

The complete setup for an AI employee that reads your industry sources so
you don't have to scroll them at midnight. She reads only what you tell
her to watch, ranks it by what matters to your business, and sends you
one honest weekly digest instead of 14 unread tabs.

---

## What you are building

One workflow with four jobs:

1. You **give her the 3-5 sources** you already follow. That's the whole
   input.
2. She **reads what came out this week**, those sources only.
3. She **ranks it by what matters to your business**, most important
   first.
4. You get **one weekly digest**, one line per item, links included.

Time to build: about 20 minutes. Cost: one AI (Claude) + a way to collect
the week's links (a bookmarks folder, an RSS reader, or just pasting URLs)
+ your inbox or notes app to receive the digest.

## The safety rules (read these first)

- 🟢 Green: the headlines and links from your named sources. That is the entire input.
- 🟠 Orange: the one-line relevance summary she writes for each item. Drafted from what the source actually said, never speculation.
- 🔴 Red: she never invents a headline, a source, or a link that wasn't given to her. If a source did not publish anything relevant this week, that item is left out, not manufactured.
- The rule that matters most: if a week is quiet, the digest says "Nothing major this week," instead of inventing urgency to look useful.

## Part 1 — Pick your sources (10 minutes, no AI)

Do this even if you build nothing else:

1. Name the 3-5 sources you already open out of habit: a newsletter, a
   subreddit, a specific account, a competitor's blog. Not an
   aspirational list, the ones you actually check.
2. Write down what counts as "must-know" for you (a new tool release, a
   pricing change, a regulation shift) so the AI has a real target
   instead of a vague brief.
3. Each week, collect the headlines or links from those sources into one
   place: a note, a bookmarks folder, a forwarded email.

## Part 2 — The ranking brain (10 minutes)

With Claude (or your AI tool of choice) directly:

1. **Input**: this week's collected headlines or links from your named
   sources.
2. **AI step**: send them with the job description below. Copy it
   exactly, fill the blanks.

```
You are my industry news analyst. Here is this week's list of headlines
and links: [PASTE HEADLINES OR LINKS].

My rules:
- Rank by relevance to [YOUR FOCUS AREA], most important first.
- One plain-English line per item: what happened, why it might matter to
  me.
- Never invent a headline or a link that wasn't given to you.
- If nothing this week matters, say exactly: "Nothing major this week."

Return the ranked digest only.
```

3. **Send**: paste the digest into a note to yourself, or a weekly email
   you send yourself, so it lands somewhere you'll actually open it.

## Part 3 — Keep the source list honest (5 minutes a month)

Once a month, check that your 3-5 sources are still the right ones.
Industries move faster than reading lists get updated.

The feeling you are buying: you stop opening 14 tabs out of anxiety,
because you know the one thing that actually matters will land in your
digest on schedule.

## The upgrade path

Once the digest feels reliable and you trust it over your own scrolling,
you have built trust with your eighth AI employee. The same pattern, read,
rank, summarize honestly, is how every AI employee in this system works.
That is the system my paid products install across a whole business.
