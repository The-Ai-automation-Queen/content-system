# The Proposal Writer Setup

**Keyword: PROPOSAL** | Pillar: Stop Doing That by Hand | Employee #032 (Khalil) playbook

The complete setup for an AI employee that turns a good discovery call
into a real proposal, so it doesn't sit half-written until the moment
passes. He never sends anything or invents a scope item.

---

## What you are building

One workflow with four jobs:

1. You **give him your discovery notes**: need, deliverable, rough
   cost. That's the whole input.
2. He **drafts the full proposal**: scope, timeline, pricing, next
   steps.
3. He **writes a summary** short enough that a busy client will
   actually read it.
4. You **review it and send it yourself**. He never sends anything.

Time to build: about 15 minutes. Cost: one AI (Claude) + the notes from
your last real discovery call.

## The safety rules (read these first)

- 🟢 Green: your discovery notes. That is the entire source.
- 🟠 Orange: the drafted proposal and summary he builds from it.
- 🔴 Red: he never sends anything himself, and never adds a scope item, a timeline, or a price you didn't actually give him.
- The rule that matters most: nothing gets promised that wasn't in your notes. A shorter, honest proposal beats an impressive, invented one.

## Part 1 — Capture the call (5 minutes, no AI)

Do this even if you build nothing else:

1. Right after a discovery call, write four lines: what the client
   needs, what you'd deliver, roughly what it costs, roughly how long.
2. Note anything they said in their own words worth echoing back.

## Part 2 — The writing brain (10 minutes)

With Claude (or your AI tool of choice) directly:

1. **Input**: your four lines from Part 1, plus anything else you
   remember.
2. **AI step**: send it with the job description below. Copy it
   exactly, fill the blanks.

```
You are my proposal writer. Here is what came out of a discovery call:
[NEED, DELIVERABLE, ROUGH COST, ROUGH TIMELINE, ANYTHING THEY SAID IN
THEIR OWN WORDS].

My rules:
- Draft the full proposal: scope, timeline, pricing, next steps.
- Mirror the client's own language and stated goals where you can.
- Write an executive summary under 150 words.
- Never add a scope item, timeline, or price I didn't give you.

Return the full proposal and the summary.
```

3. **Review and send**: check every line against what was actually
   discussed, then send it yourself.

## Part 3 — Do it the same day (10 minutes)

Send a proposal within a day or two of the call, while the
conversation's still fresh for both of you. The same proposal sent two
weeks later has to work twice as hard.

The feeling you are buying: a good call stops losing momentum to a
blank document, and starts turning into a proposal the same day.

## The upgrade path

Once the writing feels routine, you have built trust with your twenty-
ninth AI employee. The same pattern, capture honestly, draft plainly,
never invent a term, is how every AI employee in this system works.
That is the system my paid products install across a whole business.
