# The Check-In Writer Setup

**Keyword: CHECKIN** | Pillar: Stop Doing That by Hand | Employee #043 (Hiba) playbook

The complete setup for an AI employee that writes the proactive
check-in you keep meaning to send a client, so you're not only
reaching out when something's wrong. She never sends anything or
invents how a client is doing.

---

## What you are building

One workflow with four jobs:

1. You **tell her how the client is actually doing**: what's going
   well, what might be a worry. That's the whole input.
2. She **writes one short check-in message**, warm and specific, not
   generic.
3. She **suggests one question to ask them**, based on what you told
   her.
4. You **review it and send it yourself**. She never sends anything.

Time to build: about 10 minutes. The tools are boring on purpose: one
AI (Claude), a quick read on how a client is actually doing.

## The safety rules (read these first)

- 🟢 Green: your own read on how the client is doing, what's going well, what might be a worry. That is the entire source.
- 🟠 Orange: the drafted check-in message and suggested question she builds from it.
- 🔴 Red: she never sends anything herself, and never invents how a client is doing or a detail you didn't give her.
- The rule that matters most: it only reflects what you actually told her. A short honest check-in beats a generic, invented one.

## Part 1 — Say how they're doing (5 minutes, no AI)

Do this even if you build nothing else:

1. Pick one client you haven't proactively reached out to in a while.
2. Write two lines: what's going well, what might be worth asking
   about.

## Part 2 — The check-in brain (5 minutes)

With Claude (or your AI tool of choice) directly:

1. **Input**: your two lines from Part 1.
2. **AI step**: send it with the job description below. Copy it
   exactly, fill the blanks.

```
You are my check-in writer. Here is how this client is actually doing:
[WHAT'S GOING WELL, WHAT MIGHT BE WORTH ASKING ABOUT].

My rules:
- Write one short, warm check-in message, under 80 words.
- Suggest one specific question to ask them.
- Never invent how the client is doing or add a detail I didn't give you.
- Keep it specific to this client, not a generic template.

Return the message and the suggested question only.
```

3. **Review and send**: check it sounds like you and is accurate, then
   send it yourself.

## Part 3 — Make it a habit, not a reaction (10 minutes)

Pick one or two clients a week and send a check-in, even when nothing's
wrong. A client who only hears from you when there's a problem starts
to expect bad news every time you reach out.

The feeling you are buying: you stop only showing up when something's
broken, and start being the one who checks in before you have to.

## The upgrade path

Once the check-ins feel routine, you have built trust with your
fortieth AI employee. The same pattern, describe honestly, write
specifically, never invent how someone's doing, is how every AI
employee in this system works. That is the system my paid products
install across a whole business.
