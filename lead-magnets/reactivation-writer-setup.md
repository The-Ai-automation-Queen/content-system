# The Reactivation Writer Setup

**Keyword: REVIVE** | Pillar: Stop Doing That by Hand | Employee #018 (Nassim) playbook

The complete setup for an AI employee that writes the first line back to
a lead who went quiet, honestly, without pretending the silence never
happened. He never sends anything, and he'll tell you when a lead isn't
worth the effort.

---

## What you are building

One workflow with four jobs:

1. You **tell him about the lead** and what's changed since they went
   quiet. That's the whole input.
2. He **drafts one honest reactivation email**, referencing the real
   gap in time.
3. If the lead genuinely **isn't worth reactivating**, he says so
   instead of forcing a draft.
4. You **review it and send it yourself**. He never sends anything.

Time to build: about 15 minutes. Cost: one AI (Claude) + your existing
list of leads who went cold, even a rough mental list works to start.

## The safety rules (read these first)

- 🟢 Green: the lead's history and what's changed, as you describe it. That is the entire source of the email.
- 🟠 Orange: the drafted reactivation email itself.
- 🔴 Red: he never sends anything, never fabricates a reason to reach out, and never pretends time didn't pass.
- The rule that matters most: he references the real gap honestly. An email that pretends three months of silence didn't happen reads as fake, and he won't write that.

## Part 1 — Name one lead (5 minutes, no AI)

Do this even if you build nothing else:

1. Name the one lead who went quiet that you still think about.
2. Write down why they went cold, as best you remember: timing, budget,
   life, no clear reason at all.
3. Write down what's genuinely changed since then, on your side or
   theirs.

## Part 2 — The reactivation brain (10 minutes)

With Claude (or your AI tool of choice) directly:

1. **Input**: the lead's history and what's changed since.
2. **AI step**: send it with the job description below. Copy it
   exactly, fill the blanks.

```
You are my reactivation writer. Here is a lead who went quiet:
[DESCRIBE THE LEAD, WHY THEY WENT COLD, WHAT'S CHANGED SINCE].

My rules:
- Write one honest reactivation email that references the real gap in
  time, don't pretend it didn't happen.
- If this lead doesn't look worth reactivating, say so plainly instead
  of forcing a draft.
- Never invent a reason to reach out that isn't true.
- Never mark this as sent, that is always my step.

Return the email draft, or the honest "not worth it" note.
```

3. **Review and send**: read it, adjust anything that doesn't sound
   like you, and send it yourself.

## Part 3 — Work the list, a few at a time (10 minutes a week)

Don't reactivate your whole list in one sitting. A few leads a week,
reviewed honestly, beats a mass email that reads like a mass email.

The feeling you are buying: the leads who were worth keeping get one
more honest chance, and the ones that weren't stop taking up space in
your head.

## The upgrade path

Once reactivation feels routine instead of awkward, you have built
trust with your fifteenth AI employee. The same pattern, describe
honestly, draft honestly, flag what isn't worth it, is how every AI
employee in this system works. That is the system my paid products
install across a whole business.
