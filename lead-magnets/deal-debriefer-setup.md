# The Deal Debriefer Setup

**Keyword: DEBRIEF** | Pillar: Stop Doing That by Hand | Employee #048 (Ghassan) playbook

The complete setup for an AI employee that writes down why a deal was
won or lost while it's still fresh, so the same mistake doesn't repeat
itself quietly. He never contacts the client and never invents a
reason you didn't actually give him.

---

## What you are building

One workflow with four jobs:

1. You **tell him what happened**: won or lost, and why, as best you
   know it. That's the whole input.
2. He **writes one clean debrief**: what worked, what didn't, one
   thing to try differently.
3. He **flags any pattern** if it echoes something you've told him
   before.
4. You **review it and file it yourself**. He never contacts the
   client or anyone else.

Time to build: about 10 minutes. The tools are boring on purpose: one
AI (Claude), your own honest read on why the deal went the way it did.

## The safety rules (read these first)

- 🟢 Green: your own account of what happened and why, as best you know it. That is the entire source.
- 🟠 Orange: the written debrief and pattern flag he builds from it.
- 🔴 Red: he never contacts the client or anyone else, and never invents a reason you didn't actually give him.
- The rule that matters most: "I'm not sure why" is a valid answer. A short honest debrief beats a confident invented one.

## Part 1 — Say what happened (5 minutes, no AI)

Do this even if you build nothing else:

1. Right after a deal closes or dies, write three lines: won or lost,
   why as best you know it, what you'd do differently.
2. Note anything that feels familiar from a past deal.

## Part 2 — The debriefing brain (5 minutes)

With Claude (or your AI tool of choice) directly:

1. **Input**: your three lines from Part 1.
2. **AI step**: send it with the job description below. Copy it
   exactly, fill the blanks.

```
You are my deal debriefer. Here is what happened: [WON OR LOST, WHY AS
BEST I KNOW IT, WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY, ANYTHING THAT FEELS FAMILIAR].

My rules:
- Write one clean debrief: what worked, what didn't, one change to try.
- If I said "not sure why," keep that, don't invent a reason.
- Flag it if this echoes a pattern I've mentioned before.
- Keep it under 120 words.

Return the debrief only.
```

3. **Review and file**: check it's honest, then save it somewhere
   you'll actually look again.

## Part 3 — Do it for every deal, not just the big losses (10 minutes)

Debrief wins too, not only losses. The pattern that's working deserves
the same honest write-up as the one that isn't.

The feeling you are buying: you stop repeating the same quiet mistake
three deals in a row, and start actually learning from each one.

## The upgrade path

Once the debriefing feels routine, you have built trust with your
forty-fifth AI employee. The same pattern, say it honestly, write it
plainly, never invent a reason, is how every AI employee in this
system works. That is the system my paid products install across a
whole business.
