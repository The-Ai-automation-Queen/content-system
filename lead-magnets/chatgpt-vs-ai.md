# ChatGPT and AI are not the same thing. Here's what each one actually is.

*From Fatiha — the AI Automation Queen*

ChatGPT. AI. Chatbot. Assistant. Model. LLM. People mix all of these in the same sentence.

You assume they mean the same thing. They don't. Here are the 6 words, sorted biggest to smallest, so you never get lost in that conversation again.

---

## The 6 words, in order

### 1. AI (Artificial Intelligence)
The biggest box. Any software that learned by example, not by typed rules. Includes everything below. When someone says "AI," they could mean almost anything.

### 2. Machine Learning (ML)
A specific way of doing AI. Software that improves by being shown examples. Your spam filter is ML. So is Netflix's "you may also like." Boring, old, and quietly running half the internet.

### 3. Generative AI
The new exciting kind. It *generates* new text, images, audio, or video — it's not just recognising patterns, it's creating. This is the one in every headline right now.

### 4. LLM (Large Language Model)
The text type of generative AI. "Large" because it trained on a huge amount of writing. "Language" because that's what it makes. "Model" because it's one specific trained piece of software.

GPT-4 is a model. Claude is a model. Gemini is a model.

### 5. Chatbot / Assistant
The product wrapper around the model. The chat window you actually type into.

ChatGPT is a chatbot. The model behind it is GPT. Claude.ai is a chatbot. The model behind it is Claude Opus or Sonnet. Same distinction everywhere.

### 6. Agent
A chatbot that can take *actions*, not just talk. It can click buttons, send emails, read your calendar, run code.

Most "agents" you hear about are just chatbots with one tool plugged in — but the word is everywhere, so it's worth knowing.

---

## How it sounds in practice

Someone says: *"I asked Claude to draft the email and the model gave me 3 options."*

Translation:
- Claude = the chatbot they typed in
- The model = the AI engine running behind it
- 3 options = what the model generated

You now follow the sentence. You can ask: *"Which model — Opus or Sonnet?"* and sound like you've been in 30 of these conversations.

## Why this matters for your business

Once you see the layers, the words stop blurring. You stop assuming "AI" is one thing. You start asking the right questions when someone pitches you a tool — *is this a chatbot or does it actually take actions?*

That's the difference between buying something useful and buying something shiny.
